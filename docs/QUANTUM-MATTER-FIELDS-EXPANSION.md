New investigations cover normalized quantum densities, spin projection, occupation counting, coherent transitions, perturbative mixing, variational bounds, elastic scattering, mixed states, relativistic energy and aberration, atomic/nuclear scales, lattice and carrier models, field-theory benchmarks, exterior clock factors, Kerr radii, convergence, sampling and research uncertainty. Proof lessons use counterexamples and hypothesis checks rather than pretending that a plot establishes a theorem.

Models state their limitations. A Rabi curve is not an unlimited golden-rule rate; an energy eigenvalue does not specify a Dirac spinor; a supplied integer Hall plateau is not a derived Chern number; a scalar double well does not solve electroweak theory; a Schwarzschild static clock is not a freely falling observer; a continuum cutoff is not a measured observable. Planning, literature and numerical benchmark widgets do not count as real data collection, executed capstones, actual catalog queries, numerical relativity runs or solutions to open problems.
