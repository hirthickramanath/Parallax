<LessonSection id="concept" title="Definitions and hypotheses" layer="concept">

S_n is the group of bijections of n labels under composition. Use the convention that the rightmost permutation acts first.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="theory" title="Derivation, proof and limitations" layer="theory" fullOnly>

Every finite permutation decomposes into disjoint cycles, which commute when their supports are disjoint. Its order is the least common multiple of cycle lengths, not generally their sum. The same permutation can have different written products of overlapping cycles.

A k-cycle can be decomposed into k−1 transpositions, so its parity is $(-1)^{k-1}$. Although transposition factorizations are not unique, parity is well defined, for example through the determinant of a permutation matrix. The sign map S_n to the two signs ±1 is a homomorphism with even-permutation kernel A_n.

There are n! permutations because successive images have n,n−1,… choices. Cycle type controls conjugacy classes in S_n: relabelling preserves cycle lengths and any matching cycle structure is related by a relabelling. A conjugacy claim depends on which group’s conjugation is allowed.

In quantum identical-particle theory, exchanging labels acts on a state space; the permutation group alone does not settle every allowed physical representation. Spatial dimension, spin-statistics assumptions and internal state structure matter. A relabelling convention should not be confused with physically rotating an apparatus.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="interactive" title="Investigate the claim" layer="interactive">

<LessonSection id="worked" title="A worked argument" layer="worked" fullOnly>

A three-cycle has order three and sign +1 because it uses two transpositions. S₃ contains six elements. A product of disjoint cycles of lengths two and three has order six, not five.

</LessonSection>
