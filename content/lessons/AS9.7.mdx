<LessonSection id="concept" title="Definitions and hypotheses" layer="concept">

Cold dark matter is modeled as approximately pressureless clustering matter on relevant cosmological scales; dark energy describes a component or modification responsible for accelerated background behaviour in a chosen framework.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="theory" title="Derivation, proof and limitations" layer="theory" fullOnly>

For a separately conserved constant-w component, $\rho\propto a^{-3(1+w)}$. Pressureless matter has w≈0 and dilutes as a⁻³. A cosmological constant has w=−1 and constant density. The ratio of constant dark-energy density to matter density therefore grows as a³ in that model.

Dark-matter constraints include lensing, dynamics, CMB and structure, not only one galaxy curve. Candidate particles or other mechanisms must satisfy abundance, interaction and small-scale constraints. Gravitational evidence does not itself identify a unique laboratory particle.

Dark-energy inference uses distances, expansion and growth with parameter/model assumptions. A fluid w description may be an effective parameterization, not a literal material with a known microscopic Hamiltonian. Modified-gravity models can affect growth and geometry differently and must be tested consistently.

Parameter degeneracies, calibration and evolution can mimic departures in limited data. Compare independent probes and conservative model extensions before claiming a discovery of time-varying dark energy. This draft does not assert a new present-day measurement or resolve open particle/cosmological questions.


### Derive, test and transfer

For a conserved homogeneous component with constant equation-of-state ratio w=P/(ρc²), energy conservation gives $\rho\propto a^{-3(1+w)}$. Pressureless matter has w=0 and dilutes as a^−3; a cosmological constant has w=−1 and remains constant. Their density ratio therefore falls as a^−3.

Dark matter is inferred through several gravitational and cosmological effects, while dark energy refers to the component or modification associated with accelerated expansion. They are not the same substance merely because both are poorly identified. A constant-w fit is itself an assumption; time dependence or interactions modify the simple scaling. The investigation isolates the matter–Λ comparison and makes no particle identification.

</LessonSection>
