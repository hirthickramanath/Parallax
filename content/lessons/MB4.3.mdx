<LessonSection id="story" title="Why this matters" layer="story">

Astronomical source detection combines a likelihood model with how common sources are. Ignoring a rare event’s base rate can make an apparently accurate instrument seem far more decisive than it is.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="intuition" title="Build the picture" layer="intuition">

Conditioning changes the population you are counting. P(A given B) asks about the B group; it does not ask about the A group.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="concept" title="State the model" layer="concept">

Probability assigns nonnegative weights totaling one. Conditional probability is P(A∩B)/P(B) when P(B)>0.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="theory" title="Derive and qualify the result" layer="theory" fullOnly>

For events A and B, $P(A\cup B)=P(A)+P(B)-P(A\cap B)$. Independence means $P(A\cap B)=P(A)P(B)$. Mutual exclusivity means the intersection is empty. Two disjoint events with nonzero probabilities are not independent: knowing one occurred rules out the other.

For $P(B)>0$, $P(A|B)=P(B|A)P(A)/P(B)$. If H and its complement partition the sample space, $P(B)=P(B|H)P(H)+P(B|H^c)P(H^c)$. These statements derive Bayes' rule; they do not require a special kind of detector.

Suppose a true source occurs in 1% of locations. A test flags 90% of true sources and 5% of blank locations. Among 10,000 locations, expected true flags are 90 and false flags 495. Thus a flag has posterior source probability $90/(90+495)\approx0.154$. Sensitivity is not the probability that a flagged object is real.

For sequential evidence multiply conditional probabilities along the sequence. Multiplying marginal probabilities is valid only under independence. Even tests that are independent conditional on a source state need not be independent after the state is mixed out.

A continuous distribution assigns zero probability to an exact point while intervals may have positive probability. A probability density is integrated over a range and can exceed 1 in units reciprocal to the variable. Undefined conditioning on a probability-zero event needs a density or regular conditional construction, beyond simple event division.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="worked" title="A worked example" layer="worked" fullOnly>

Two fair independent coin flips have four equiprobable ordered outcomes. Given at least one head, HH, HT and TH remain. Only HH has two heads, so the conditional probability is 1/3, not 1/2. The wording of the observed evidence determines the conditioning event.

</LessonSection>
