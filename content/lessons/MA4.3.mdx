<LessonSection id="story" title="Why the question matters" layer="story">

Coupled ODEs describe oscillators, chemical populations and orbital dynamics. A phase portrait records states, not simply positions, and helps separate geometry from the time history along a curve.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="intuition" title="Build a mental model" layer="intuition">

A state includes every variable needed to predict the next local change. Two different velocities at one position are different phase-space points.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="concept" title="Definitions and hypotheses" layer="concept">

An autonomous first-order system x′=F(x) assigns a tangent vector to each state. An equilibrium is a zero of F; a trajectory is an integral curve with its time orientation.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="theory" title="Derivation, proof and limitations" layer="theory" fullOnly>

A second-order scalar equation becomes a two-component first-order system by defining v=y′. For y″+ω²y=0, the system is y′=v and v′=−ω²y. Position alone is not a complete state. The conserved quantity $(v^2+\omega^2y^2)/2$ makes nonzero trajectories ellipses in this coordinate plane; their aspect ratio depends on the axis units.

For a constant matrix A, x′=Ax has solution $x(t)=e^{tA}x_0$. Real eigenvalues with negative signs give decay along associated eigenvectors; opposite signs yield a saddle. A complex conjugate pair α±iβ gives rotation with exponential envelope eᵅᵗ when a real plane representation applies. Repeated eigenvalues require the Jordan structure already introduced in linear algebra.

For nonlinear autonomous systems with local uniqueness, two trajectories meeting the same state at a given time must coincide after a time shift as long as both solutions exist. Drawn crossing in a projection can occur when omitted coordinates differ. Nonautonomous systems can cross in the original state plot at different times because the forcing time supplies additional state information.

In two dimensions, nullclines F₁=0 and F₂=0 identify where one component stops changing. Their intersections are equilibrium candidates; elsewhere one nullcline only makes the flow tangent to a coordinate direction. Nullclines are not generally trajectories, and a zero of one derivative need not be an equilibrium.

Phase-plane sketches should show arrows, axes, equilibrium classifications and any conserved or monotone function. Numerical step errors can create false spirals in a true centre. To infer physical damping from a plot, compare an analytic invariant or step-refined solution rather than interpreting a numerical artifact as a new force.


### Derive, test and transfer

For a linear autonomous system u′=Au, the flow is exp(tA)u₀. A diagonal A with negative entries contracts along both eigen-directions; a matrix with eigenvalues of opposite sign has a saddle. Off-diagonal coupling rotates or shears the coordinate description, so individual coordinate slopes are not eigenvalues.

A phase-plane vector field gives local directions at every state. A trajectory is an integrated curve following those directions, not an arbitrary line drawn through two arrows. For nonlinear systems the linearization describes nearby behavior when its hypotheses apply; zero or purely imaginary linear eigenvalues can leave stability undecided. The investigation should distinguish an exact linear flow from an approximation to a nonlinear one.

</LessonSection>
