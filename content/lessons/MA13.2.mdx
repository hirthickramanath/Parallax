<LessonSection id="story" title="Why the question matters" layer="story">

Noise with state-dependent amplitude needs an integration convention; the physical interpretation and probability evolution must use it consistently.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="intuition" title="Build a mental model" layer="intuition">

Start with the concrete worked example below. Track which quantities belong to the system, which are imposed by its surroundings, and which conclusions require a limiting approximation.

<LessonSection id="concept" title="Definitions and hypotheses" layer="concept">

An Itô stochastic differential equation dX=a(X,t)dt+b(X,t)dW uses quadratic variation (dW)²=dt in its differential calculus.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="theory" title="Derivation, proof and limitations" layer="theory" fullOnly>

Itô’s formula gives $df=(f_t+af_x+b^2f_{xx}/2)dt+bf_xdW$. The second-derivative term comes from quadratic variation, not an ordinary differentiable trajectory. A Wiener path is almost surely not classically differentiable.

For appropriate coefficients and boundary conditions, the density obeys $\partial_t p=-\partial_x(ap)+\tfrac12\partial_x^2(b^2p)$. State-dependent diffusion stays inside the derivative. Absorbing/reflecting boundaries and existence/uniqueness conditions are part of the stochastic model.

In one dimension a Stratonovich drift converts to Itô drift by adding b b′/2 when regularity permits. The convention must match the physical limiting procedure and data model. Writing the same symbols without declaring convention can describe different processes.

For geometric Brownian motion dX=αXdt+σXdW, Itô’s formula yields dlnX=(α−σ²/2)dt+σdW for positive X. Mean growth, typical logarithmic growth and sample extrema are different. Euler–Maruyama has convergence regimes and can violate positivity at finite steps for this process; an exact log update is available here.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="interactive" title="Investigate the claim" layer="interactive">

Simulate a labelled geometric Brownian model with exact log updates and compare with decreasing-step Euler–Maruyama. Check positivity, ensemble mean and log mean separately. State Itô/Stratonovich convention in every reported equation.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="worked" title="A worked argument" layer="worked" fullOnly>

With α=3 and σ=2, logarithmic drift is 3−4/2=1. For additive noise b=2, free diffusion coefficient in the standard Fokker–Planck form is b²/2=2. The noise amplitude and diffusion coefficient are not interchangeable.

</LessonSection>
