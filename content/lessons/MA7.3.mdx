<LessonSection id="puzzle" title="Commit to a prediction" layer="puzzle">

Why do circular drum frequencies fail to follow an integer harmonic sequence? State a prediction and a reason before reading the argument.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="story" title="Why the question matters" layer="story">

Cylindrical separation produces Bessel functions in waves, diffraction and transport. Radial regularity and the outer boundary jointly select the spectrum.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="intuition" title="Build a mental model" layer="intuition">

Radial geometry changes the balance between curvature and amplitude. Regular oscillations near the centre are not simply a sine wave shifted along a straight line.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="concept" title="Definitions and hypotheses" layer="concept">

Bessel’s equation is x²y″+xy′+(x²−ν²)y=0. The first-kind solution Jν is selected by its small-argument behaviour; a second independent solution is needed on a general annular domain.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="theory" title="Derivation, proof and limitations" layer="theory" fullOnly>

For integer n≥0 the first-kind series is $J_n(x)=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^j}{j!(j+n)!}(x/2)^{2j+n}$. It converges for all finite x and gives J₀(0)=1 and Jₙ(0)=0 for n>0. Substitution into the differential equation supplies the recurrence of coefficients. For noninteger order the gamma function replaces the factorial and branch behaviour of xν must be declared in a complex domain.

The other standard solution Yν is singular at the origin for nonnegative integer order, so a regular field on a full disk excludes it. On an annulus that exclusion need not apply because the origin is outside the domain. The polar Laplacian contains a radial first-derivative term: separation with angular order m gives $r^2R''+rR'+(k^2r^2-m^2)R=0$, hence regular disk modes Jₘ(kr).

For a fixed circular boundary r=a, Jₘ(ka)=0 selects k=jₘ,ₙ/a, where jₘ,ₙ is a positive zero. A uniform membrane has ω=ck, so zeros rather than consecutive integers set its frequencies. A zero normal derivative instead selects derivative zeros, with special handling of any constant mode. Mixing fixed and free boundary spectra changes the physical problem.

For distinct Dirichlet radial modes of the same angular order, Sturm–Liouville orthogonality uses weight r: $\int_0^a rJ_m(j_{m,n}r/a)J_m(j_{m,p}r/a)dr=0$ for n≠p. Their squared norm is a²Jₘ₊₁(jₘ,ₙ)²/2. The weight comes from the polar area element and self-adjoint radial operator; it is not an optional plotting factor.

For large positive real x at fixed ν, $J_\nu(x)\sim\sqrt{2/(\pi x)}\cos(x-\nu\pi/2-\pi/4)$ at leading order. This fixed-order approximation is not uniform when ν grows with x or near relevant transition regions. Replacing k² by a negative separation parameter gives modified Bessel functions Iν and Kν, with exponential rather than oscillatory large-positive-argument behaviour. Spherical Bessel functions involve an additional radial geometry transformation and are not identical to Jℓ.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="worked" title="A worked argument" layer="worked" fullOnly>

If a chosen positive J₀ zero is supplied as j=2.4 for an approximate apparatus calculation, a=2 and c=3 give k=j/a=1.2 and ω=ck=3.6. The value 2.4 is explicitly a rounded supplied zero, not a claim of the exact first root. Doubling the radius at fixed tension/density halves the frequency.

</LessonSection>
