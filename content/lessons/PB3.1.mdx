<LessonSection id="story" title="Why this matters" layer="story">

Sound and stellar oscillations connect local restoring forces to signals that propagate. A wave’s pattern velocity is not generally the velocity of any particle.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="intuition" title="Build the picture" layer="intuition">

Adjacent oscillators pass a disturbance along while each moves near its equilibrium position. A snapshot hides the time dependence needed to identify travel direction.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="concept" title="State the model" layer="concept">

A linear one-dimensional nondispersive wave satisfies ∂²y/∂t²=v²∂²y/∂x². Sinusoidal phase relates angular frequency, wavenumber and propagation speed.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="theory" title="Derive and qualify the result" layer="theory" fullOnly>

A right-travelling disturbance has form $y(x,t)=f(x-vt)$. Differentiating twice gives the wave equation. For $y=A\cos(kx-\omega t+\phi)$, constant phase moves with $v=\omega/k=f\lambda$. Displacement y and wave speed v have distinct units and meanings; particle transverse velocity is ∂y/∂t.

For a taut string with tension T and linear mass density μ, balancing the small-angle transverse forces on an element gives $v=\sqrt{T/\mu}$. Large slopes or changing tension violate this linear approximation. Small-amplitude sound in a fluid has $v=\sqrt{B_s/\rho}$ using the adiabatic bulk modulus; for an ideal gas this becomes $\sqrt{\gamma RT/M}$.

Linearity permits superposition. Equal counter-propagating sinusoidal waves form $2A\cos(kx)\cos(\omega t)$ up to phase, a standing wave with fixed nodes. A string fixed at both ends L admits $\lambda_n=2L/n$ and $f_n=nv/(2L)$. An open or closed air column has different pressure and displacement boundary conditions; do not copy the string rule without checking them.

For uniform spreading in a lossless three-dimensional medium, mean intensity scales as 1/r² from energy conservation over a sphere. Absorption and directional sources change this. Sound level is $10\log_{10}(I/I_0)$ dB; a tenfold intensity increase is 10 dB, while a doubled intensity is about 3 dB.

For a stationary medium and approach along the source-observer line, classical sound Doppler formulas distinguish source and observer velocities. Light requires relativistic reasoning. In dispersive media phase velocity and group velocity can differ, so v=fλ describes phase, not automatically information transport.


### Transfer derivation and failure case

Write a fixed-end mode as $y(x,t)=A\sin(kx)\cos(\omega t+\phi)$. The second endpoint requires $\sin(kL)=0$, so $k_n=n\pi/L$ and $f_n=nv/(2L)$. A closed-open pipe instead requires a displacement node at the closed end and an antinode at the open end, giving $kL=(2n-1)\pi/2$. The resulting odd sequence assumes an ideal uniform tube; end corrections and losses shift observed resonances. Plotting a wave at successive times distinguishes particle displacement from propagation of a phase pattern.

</LessonSection>
