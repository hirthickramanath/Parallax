<LessonSection id="concept" title="Definitions and hypotheses" layer="concept">

A differential k-form is an antisymmetric covariant k-tensor field; its pullback and integral use orientation and dimension-appropriate domains.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="theory" title="Derivation, proof and limitations" layer="theory" fullOnly>

The wedge product antisymmetrizes combinations: dx∧dy=−dy∧dx and dx∧dx=0. Exterior differentiation raises degree by one and satisfies $d^2=0$. For a scalar f, df is its covector differential. Coordinate expressions must keep antisymmetry factors consistent.

Stokes’ theorem is $\int_M d\omega=\int_{\partial M}\omega$ for a suitably oriented smooth region/form with the appropriate support/regularity assumptions. The boundary orientation is induced by the region, not chosen independently to force a sign. Familiar curl circulation and divergence flux laws are metric/vector-calculus realizations.

Closed means dω=0; exact means ω=dη. Exact forms are closed, but closed forms need not be globally exact on a domain with nontrivial topology. A local Poincaré lemma does not remove a puncture or establish a global primitive.

A Hodge star requires a metric and orientation and depends on signature. Maxwell equations can be expressed using a field-strength two-form and its dual with source conventions, but metric-free d identities and metric-dependent duality are distinct. Differential forms simplify bookkeeping without eliminating boundary, gauge or topology assumptions.


### Derivation and transfer

Exterior products are antisymmetric: dx∧dy=−dy∧dx and dx∧dx=0. For α=Pdx+Qdy in a plane, dα=(∂xQ−∂yP)dx∧dy. Applying d twice gives zero for sufficiently smooth forms.

Stokes' theorem compares an oriented boundary integral to the integral of dα with compatible orientation. The display uses α=a x dy and an oriented rectangular area; it does not replace smoothness, orientation or domain hypotheses.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="worked" title="A worked argument" layer="worked" fullOnly>

For ω=x dy on a unit square oriented by dx∧dy, dω=dx∧dy and the boundary integral is 1. Reversing orientation gives −1. The values follow the induced counterclockwise versus clockwise boundary choice.
