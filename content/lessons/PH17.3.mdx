<LessonSection id="concept" title="Definitions and hypotheses" layer="concept">

Monte Carlo integration estimates an expectation with a specified sampling measure; uncertainty depends on variance and independence/mixing conditions.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="theory" title="Derivation, proof and limitations" layer="theory" fullOnly>

For uniform points in a square enclosing a unit-radius quadrant, an area ratio estimates $\pi\approx4N_{in}/N$. The estimate is random and finite-sample error is not exactly zero when rounded near 3.14. A binomial model gives one uncertainty route under independent uniform sampling.

Importance sampling rewrites an integral using weights relative to a proposal with support wherever the target integrand contributes. Missing support creates bias that more samples do not fix. Heavy weights can inflate variance, so effective weight diagnostics matter.

Markov-chain samples can be correlated and need effective-sample-size/mixing checks. Reproducible seeds and multiple runs help diagnose failures but do not prove every mode was explored. A rejection must be handled consistently with the algorithm’s estimator.

Validate on exact integrals or known distributions before a complex physical application. Check dimensions, normalization and any conserved quantities. A simulation’s stochastic noise is distinct from discretization and physical-model uncertainty.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="interactive" title="Investigate the claim" layer="interactive">

Run a labelled synthetic area estimator with multiple seeds and compare interval coverage. Deliberately use a biased proposal without weights to expose an error that increased N cannot repair.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="worked" title="A worked argument" layer="worked" fullOnly>

If 785 of 1000 independent uniform points fall inside the quadrant, π estimate is 3.14. Under an admitted finite-variance independent scaling, quadrupling sample count halves the standard error, not the guaranteed realized absolute error.

</LessonSection>
