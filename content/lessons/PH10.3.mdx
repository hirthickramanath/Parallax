<LessonSection id="concept" title="Definitions and hypotheses" layer="concept">

A configuration-space path integral is a regulated limit of short-time propagator products, with amplitude weight exp(iS/ℏ) and a specified measure/boundary prescription.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="theory" title="Derivation, proof and limitations" layer="theory" fullOnly>

Insert position resolutions between short-time factors of U(t). Under an appropriate Trotter construction, the kinetic short-time kernel supplies a normalization and a phase containing the discrete action. The formal symbol $K(b,a)=\int\mathcal D x\,e^{iS[x]/\hbar}$ is shorthand for that limiting prescription, not an ordinary finite-dimensional integral over a uniform probability of all paths.

For a free particle in one dimension, the exact kernel is √(m/(2πiℏt)) exp[im(x_b−x_a)²/(2ℏt)] for an appropriate nonzero-time branch. Its oscillatory complex amplitude is not a nonnegative path probability.

In a semiclassical regime, stationary-phase contributions localize around classical paths with fluctuation determinants and phase corrections. Multiple classical paths can interfere. Endpoints, caustics and topology change the approximation; writing only the classical action need not give the full propagator.

Imaginary-time continuation relates suitable amplitudes to statistical/Euclidean constructions under analytic and operator assumptions. It is not a universal replacement of every real-time process by a positive probability simulation; fermion signs, complex actions and continuation instability can remain.


### Derivation and transfer

A path-integral amplitude sums complex phase weights exp(iS/ℏ), not positive classical probabilities assigned independently to paths. Near stationary action, neighboring phases vary slowly, giving a semiclassical contribution under suitable conditions.

The display shows the real part of one relative phase and its oscillation with action. It is not an evaluated functional integral or a claim that destructive interference removes every nonclassical path individually. Normalization, measure and boundary data belong to the actual calculation.

</LessonSection>
