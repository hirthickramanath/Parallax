<LessonSection id="story" title="Why this matters" layer="story">

Series enter numerical approximation, perturbation theory and the repeated-reflection models used in optics. A finite expression and the limit of infinitely many terms are different mathematical objects.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="intuition" title="Build the picture" layer="intuition">

Watch the running total, not just the newest term. Tiny terms are necessary for convergence, but not sufficient: the harmonic series is the classic counterexample.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="concept" title="State the model" layer="concept">

A sequence is an ordered list. A series converges when its sequence of partial sums has a finite limit.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="theory" title="Derive and qualify the result" layer="theory" fullOnly>

For an arithmetic sequence $a_n=a_1+(n-1)d$, pair the first and last terms to obtain $S_n=n(a_1+a_n)/2$. A geometric sequence has $a_n=ar^{n-1}$. Subtracting $rS_n$ from $S_n$ gives $S_n=a(1-r^n)/(1-r)$ when r≠1. At r=1, the sum is na.

For $|r|<1$, $r^n\to0$, hence $\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}ar^k=a/(1-r)$. The error after n terms is $ar^n/(1-r)$, so a useful approximation includes an error estimate. At r=−1 the partial sums oscillate; at r=1 nonzero terms accumulate without bound. The vanishing of terms is necessary for convergence, but $\sum1/n$ diverges: grouping terms from $2^{j-1}+1$ through $2^j$ contributes at least 1/2 each time.

For a nonnegative integer n, expansion of $(1+x)^n$ selects x from k of n factors, giving coefficient $\binom nk=n!/[k!(n-k)!]$. Pascal's recurrence follows by distinguishing selections that contain a chosen object from those that do not. This finite binomial theorem needs no convergence argument.

For a general real exponent the infinite binomial expansion has a restricted convergence domain, typically $|x|<1$. Keeping $1+nx$ is a small-x approximation, not an identity. State which power was neglected and compare its size with the retained terms before applying it to a physical model.


### Transfer derivation and failure case

If $S_N=a+ar+\cdots+ar^{N-1}$, subtract $rS_N$ to get $(1-r)S_N=a(1-r^N)$. Only when $|r|<1$ does the remainder $ar^N$ vanish and give $S=a/(1-r)$. A noninteger binomial expansion is likewise a power series with a convergence domain; endpoint behavior must be checked separately.

### Derivation and transfer

For a geometric series with ratio r, the finite sum is (1−r^(N+1))/(1−r) from index zero through N when r≠1. The infinite sum exists for |r|&lt;1 and equals 1/(1−r). A sequence tending to zero is necessary but not sufficient for an arbitrary series to converge.

The display keeps a convergent positive ratio and shows the finite-sum remainder. It is an exact geometric benchmark, not a convergence test for every alternating or conditional series.

</LessonSection>
