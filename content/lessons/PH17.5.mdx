<LessonSection id="concept" title="Definitions and hypotheses" layer="concept">

A lab design specifies apparatus, controls, observations, analysis, uncertainty and a predeclared model comparison with scope and safety appropriate to the experiment.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="theory" title="Derivation, proof and limitations" layer="theory" fullOnly>

For the ideal small-angle pendulum, $T^2=(4\pi^2/g)L$. A regression across lengths can test linearity and estimate a slope, while intercept and residual trends reveal offsets or model failure. Length and timing uncertainties must be included consistently; an ordinary least-squares fit may be inadequate when both axes are uncertain.

Apparatus description should show pivot, bob, length reference, release and timing method, explaining what each controls. Repeatability tests do not replace calibration, and deliberately forcing the intercept to zero can hide a systematic offset.

Use separate calibration and evaluation data when feasible. Record exclusions and parameter choices before inspecting a desired result. Synthetic lab readings are valid for software/model verification only if labelled; they cannot certify a new experimental value of g.

The report includes raw data/provenance, unit-aware analysis, uncertainty, residuals, limitations and a reproducible method. If actual equipment or independent observations are unavailable, submit a design/simulation report with that limitation rather than claiming a performed experiment.


### Derivation and transfer

A capstone starts from a testable question, specified apparatus or model, a calibration and uncertainty budget, and a predeclared analysis that could disconfirm the hypothesis. A successful-looking result is not required; traceable methods and honest limitations are.

The display gives an independent-mean precision budget σ/√N. It is planning arithmetic, not collected data or proof of feasibility. Real completion requires raw records, code or notebooks, calibration and a reviewable report; an interactive lesson alone does not award research execution credit.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="worked" title="A worked argument" layer="worked" fullOnly>

If a hypothetical valid T²-versus-L slope is 4 in compatible units, the ideal estimate is g=π²≈9.8696. A slope uncertainty of 2 percent gives about 2 percent relative uncertainty in g in the linear propagation limit, before other systematic/model errors.
