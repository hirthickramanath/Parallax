<LessonSection id="ready" title="Before you start" layer="before">

Read the linked prerequisites and keep a proof notebook. State the domain, assumptions and units before using any formula. A soft-lock override does not establish prerequisite knowledge.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="puzzle" title="Commit to a prediction" layer="puzzle">

What makes an experiment a test of a model rather than a demonstration selected to agree? State a prediction and a reason before reading the argument.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="story" title="Why the question matters" layer="story">

A capstone needs a falsifiable target, independent calibration, a protocol and a discrepancy plan.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="intuition" title="Build a mental model" layer="intuition">

Start with the concrete worked example below. Track which quantities belong to the system, which are imposed by its surroundings, and which conclusions require a limiting approximation.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="concept" title="Definitions and hypotheses" layer="concept">

A lab design specifies apparatus, controls, observations, analysis, uncertainty and a predeclared model comparison with scope and safety appropriate to the experiment.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="theory" title="Derivation, proof and limitations" layer="theory" fullOnly>

For the ideal small-angle pendulum, $T^2=(4\pi^2/g)L$. A regression across lengths can test linearity and estimate a slope, while intercept and residual trends reveal offsets or model failure. Length and timing uncertainties must be included consistently; an ordinary least-squares fit may be inadequate when both axes are uncertain.

Apparatus description should show pivot, bob, length reference, release and timing method, explaining what each controls. Repeatability tests do not replace calibration, and deliberately forcing the intercept to zero can hide a systematic offset.

Use separate calibration and evaluation data when feasible. Record exclusions and parameter choices before inspecting a desired result. Synthetic lab readings are valid for software/model verification only if labelled; they cannot certify a new experimental value of g.

The report includes raw data/provenance, unit-aware analysis, uncertainty, residuals, limitations and a reproducible method. If actual equipment or independent observations are unavailable, submit a design/simulation report with that limitation rather than claiming a performed experiment.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="interactive" title="Investigate the claim" layer="interactive">

Use a foundation project or the pendulum/circuit bench to prepare a full protocol. Keep apparatus controls and model assumptions explicit, and distinguish a simulation report from physical measurements.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="worked" title="A worked argument" layer="worked" fullOnly>

If a hypothetical valid T²-versus-L slope is 4 in compatible units, the ideal estimate is g=π²≈9.8696. A slope uncertainty of 2 percent gives about 2 percent relative uncertainty in g in the linear propagation limit, before other systematic/model errors.

</LessonSection>

The conceptual checks ask which assumptions make a conclusion valid. Numerical responses use the stated units. A numerical sample can expose a false theorem or model, but agreement on samples is not a universal proof.
