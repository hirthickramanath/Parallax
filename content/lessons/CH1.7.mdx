<LessonSection id="story" title="Why the question matters" layer="story">

Nonideal mixtures retain the chemical-potential framework while replacing naive concentration rules with calibrated activity and fugacity models. Conventions matter especially when combining datasets.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="intuition" title="Build a mental model" layer="intuition">

Interactions change the free-energy effect of adding a component. Activity packages that effect into the logarithm relative to a chosen reference.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="concept" title="Definitions and hypotheses" layer="concept">

An activity coefficient relates a chosen composition scale to dimensionless activity. Fugacity plays an analogous role for gas chemical potential relative to a pressure standard.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="theory" title="Derivation, proof and limitations" layer="theory" fullOnly>

For a liquid mole-fraction convention, $a_i=\gamma_i x_i$ with a specified reference state. A concentration convention instead uses γi ci/c°. Coefficients from these conventions are not interchangeable numbers. Ideal behavior is a statement about the selected standard and limit, not a universal claim that every γ equals one everywhere.

For a gas, write $\mu=\mu^\circ+RT\ln(f/p^\circ)$ and f=φp for a pure component under the declared convention. The fugacity coefficient φ tends to one in the appropriate ideal-gas limit. For mixtures, partial fugacity includes composition and interactions; replacing it by yi p can fail at high pressure.

At fixed temperature for a pure gas, dμ=Vm dp. Comparing with the ideal reference gives $\ln\phi=\int_0^p[Z(p')-1]dp'/p'$ when the low-pressure limit and equation-of-state integration apply. Here Z=pVm/(RT). A model must approach the correct ideal limit for this reference integration to be well behaved.

Gibbs–Duhem links activity-coefficient variations across composition. Fitting each component independently without thermodynamic consistency can predict mutually incompatible chemical potentials. Excess Gibbs-energy models provide one way to build linked coefficients, but their parameters and validity range still require data.

Phase and reaction equilibrium use activities or fugacities consistently on both sides. A numerical K changes with a changed standard-state definition even when the underlying physical equilibrium does not. Always report reference pressure, composition scale, temperature and the model used to infer nonideality.


### Derive, test and transfer

A nonideal mixture can be described with dimensionless activities ai=γi xi under a chosen convention. Then μi−μi°=RT ln(γi xi). Activity coefficients are not arbitrary correction factors to insert without a standard state; different conventions can shift coefficients and standard potentials together while preserving physical predictions.

For gases, fugacity replaces the simple pressure role through a dimensionless f/f°. An equation of state or measured excess thermodynamics constrains these quantities. Setting γ=1 is an ideality assumption, not a claim that a solution is dilute in every useful sense. The display varies one coefficient as an illustrative local input; it does not construct a globally thermodynamically consistent multicomponent excess-Gibbs model.

</LessonSection>
