</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="concept" title="Definitions and hypotheses" layer="concept">

Weak interactions couple flavour structures through chiral fields; P, C, CP and T are distinct transformations with separate assumptions and tests.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="theory" title="Derivation, proof and limitations" layer="theory" fullOnly>

Parity reverses spatial orientation and charge conjugation exchanges particle/antiparticle descriptions. CP combines them. Weak processes can violate parity strongly, and selected processes violate CP; neither statement alone defines every other symmetry’s status.

A decay asymmetry can be defined $A=(\Gamma-\bar\Gamma)/(\Gamma+\bar\Gamma)$ for specified conjugate channels and acceptance corrections. Instrument/production asymmetries can imitate a signal if not controlled. Interference, mixing and phase conventions enter different CP observables.

Quark mixing is parameterized by a unitary CKM matrix in the model, while lepton mixing has its own masses/phases and experimental questions. A phase in one matrix entry is not automatically a basis-invariant observable; rephasing-invariant combinations matter.

CPT statements require the hypotheses of the relevant local relativistic quantum theorem. Neutrino oscillation measures differences and mixing, not a unique absolute mass scale. Explaining the cosmic matter asymmetry involves more than merely noting that CP violation exists in one laboratory channel.

</LessonSection>
