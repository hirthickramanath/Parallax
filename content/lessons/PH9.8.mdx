<LessonSection id="concept" title="Definitions and hypotheses" layer="concept">

Entanglement is nonseparability across a specified subsystem split. Bell inequalities constrain correlations in defined local hidden-variable models with measurement-setting assumptions.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="theory" title="Derivation, proof and limitations" layer="theory" fullOnly>

For product pure states, subsystem amplitudes factor; entangled states do not. Mixed separable states can still have classical correlations, so correlation alone is not entanglement. A subsystem split and allowed operations must be stated.

The CHSH combination in a local model has $|S|\le2$ under its setting-independence/local-response assumptions. Quantum theory allows 2√2 for suitable states and measurements. Actual tests must address detection, locality, memory and statistics rather than declaring a violation from an uncalibrated sample.

Entanglement does not permit controllable superluminal signalling: the unconditional remote reduced state is unchanged by a local trace-preserving operation. Conditioning on an outcome changes a subensemble description, but learning that outcome requires classical information.

Quantum information protocols require precise resources: teleportation includes shared entanglement, local operations and classical communication; it is not transport of matter or an unknown state copied while the original is retained. No-cloning concerns arbitrary unknown states under linear quantum operations, not every special orthogonal-state copying task.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="worked" title="A worked argument" layer="worked" fullOnly>

The ideal quantum CHSH maximum is 2√2≈2.828, above the local bound 2. Their ratio is √2. This theoretical number is not a claim that a particular experimental dataset has closed all loopholes.
