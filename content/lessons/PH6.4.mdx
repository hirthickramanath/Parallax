<LessonSection id="concept" title="Definitions and hypotheses" layer="concept">

Jones calculus describes a fully polarized coherent transverse field in a selected basis; Stokes parameters describe polarization through intensity/correlation observables, including partial polarization.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="theory" title="Derivation, proof and limitations" layer="theory" fullOnly>

For a linear anisotropic dielectric, D=ε·E with a tensor ε, so D and E need not be parallel. Direction-dependent normal modes can have different refractive indices and phases. Ordinary/extraordinary descriptions require the crystal symmetry and propagation direction; isotropic formulas cannot be reused unchanged.

A retarder introduces relative phase $\delta=2\pi\Delta n\,d/\lambda$ for a stated vacuum wavelength and effective birefringence. A quarter-wave plate gives π/2 and a half-wave plate π modulo 2π in the design condition. Wavelength, incidence and axis alignment affect actual performance.

For a fully linearly polarized input and ideal analyser at angle θ, Malus’s law gives I=I₀cos²θ. Unpolarized light transmitted by an ideal linear polarizer has mean intensity I₀/2, requiring ensemble averaging rather than a single deterministic Jones vector. A physical partial polarization state needs a coherency matrix or Stokes description.

Stokes sign conventions, especially circular-polarization handedness, differ with time and viewing conventions. Depolarization, dichroism and birefringence are distinct effects. Scattering and magnetized plasmas can rotate or mix polarization; infer material properties only with the corresponding transfer model and calibration.


### Derive, test and transfer

An ideal linear analyzer projects the electric-field amplitude onto its transmission axis. Squaring gives Malus' law I=I0cos²θ for an already linearly polarized incident beam. Unpolarized light instead averages over orientations, giving one half transmission for a single ideal analyzer.

A birefringent plate accumulates relative phase 2πΔnL/λ between its principal polarization components. A quarter-wave retardance requires ΔnL=λ/4 under the specified wavelength and axes. Intensity projection and coherent relative phase are different operations; a retarder can change polarization without the same power loss as an absorbing analyzer. Real dispersion makes a plate's retardance wavelength-dependent.

</LessonSection>

An ideal analyser 60° from a linear input transmits one quarter of its intensity. For Δn=.1 and λ=1 in normalized length units, a quarter-wave thickness is d=λ/(4Δn)=2.5. This is a design calculation, not a universal plate thickness.
