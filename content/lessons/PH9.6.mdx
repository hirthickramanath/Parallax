<LessonSection id="story" title="Why the question matters" layer="story">

Scattering connects asymptotic flux with interactions; normalization and long-range assumptions determine which formulas apply.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="concept" title="Definitions and hypotheses" layer="concept">

For short-range potential scattering, a stationary asymptotic state can be written as an incident plane wave plus f(θ,φ)eⁱᵏʳ/r under a stated normalization.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="theory" title="Derivation, proof and limitations" layer="theory" fullOnly>

With equal incident/outgoing channel speeds, $d\sigma/d\Omega=|f|^2$ follows from radial scattered current divided by incident flux. Different channels require speed factors. Total cross section integrates over solid angle, not merely polar angle without sinθ.

The first Born amplitude is proportional to the Fourier transform of the potential, with coefficient −m/(2πℏ²) for a common plane-wave/asymptotic convention. Weakness/high-energy conditions and convergence must justify the approximation; it is not exact for arbitrary strong potentials.

Partial waves encode phase shifts for central interactions. Unitarity constrains each elastic channel and links forward amplitude to total cross section through an optical-theorem convention. Absorption/inelastic channels change the elastic-only bookkeeping.

Long-range Coulomb scattering does not satisfy every simple short-range asymptotic assumption; forward divergences require physical screening/angular acceptance or a different treatment. A finite detector covers limited angles and response, so observed counts require acceptance/luminosity corrections before quoting a total cross section.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="interactive" title="Investigate the claim" layer="interactive">

Integrate a supplied angular amplitude with the correct measure and compare limited detector acceptance. Test a Born calculation against a weak-potential control before increasing coupling.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="worked" title="A worked argument" layer="worked" fullOnly>

If f=2 isotropically in length units with equal speeds, differential cross section is 4 and total is 16π. Integrating 4 over polar angle alone would miss the full 4π solid-angle measure.

</LessonSection>
