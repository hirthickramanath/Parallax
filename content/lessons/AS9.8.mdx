<LessonSection id="concept" title="Definitions and hypotheses" layer="concept">

A tension is a quantitative discrepancy between specified statistical inferences; its significance depends on model, shared data, distributions and uncertainty treatment.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="theory" title="Derivation, proof and limitations" layer="theory" fullOnly>

For two independent approximately Gaussian scalar estimates, a simple diagnostic is $z=|x_1-x_2|/\sqrt{\sigma_1^2+\sigma_2^2}$. Shared covariance changes the difference variance to σ₁²+σ₂²−2Cov. Non-Gaussian or multiparameter posteriors require more appropriate comparisons.

H₀ estimates from a distance ladder and from an early-universe model are conditional on different calibration chains and assumptions. An early-universe posterior is not a direct model-free local expansion measurement. Choice of dataset/release, nuisance parameters and sample selection affects comparisons.

A common late-structure combination is $S_8=\sigma_8\sqrt{\Omega_m/0.3}$, but its constraining direction is survey/model dependent. Lensing shape calibration, photometric redshifts, intrinsic alignments and nonlinear matter modelling contribute systematics; shared large-scale modes can correlate probes.

Testing a proposed resolution requires joint consistency with other observables and a justified model-complexity comparison. Look-elsewhere effects and retrospective dataset selection can inflate surprise. Record paper versions, releases and dates when describing a live scientific status. The numbers below are hypothetical and do not claim the current observed significance of any tension.


### Derive, test and transfer

For two Gaussian estimates, $\mathrm{Var}(x_1-x_2)=\sigma_1^2+\sigma_2^2-2\rho\sigma_1\sigma_2$. A standardized difference uses this variance, not the sum of quoted standard deviations. Positive correlation reduces the difference variance when the same-sign systematic is shared; unknown covariance must not be invented to strengthen a tension claim.

Real H₀ or S8 comparisons are conditional on datasets, calibration and cosmological model. Non-Gaussian posteriors and differing parameter definitions can make a one-number sigma summary incomplete. The investigation uses synthetic values, deliberately avoiding a claim about the current observational significance. A discrepancy motivates consistency checks and competing explanations, not an automatic proof of new physics.

</LessonSection>
