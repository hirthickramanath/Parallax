<LessonSection id="story" title="Why the question matters" layer="story">

Acquisition is an end-to-end measurement chain with bandwidth, sampling, quantization and calibration requirements.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="intuition" title="Build a mental model" layer="intuition">

<LessonSection id="concept" title="Definitions and hypotheses" layer="concept">

A data-acquisition chain converts a physical response through a sensor, conditioning/filtering, digitization and a time/reference convention.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="theory" title="Derivation, proof and limitations" layer="theory" fullOnly>

For a genuinely bandlimited signal, sampling above twice its highest frequency is a core reconstruction requirement under the sampling theorem. Real sensors need anti-alias filtering before digitization; post-processing cannot uniquely recover frequencies already aliased. A short finite record also limits spectral discrimination.

An ideal N-bit uniform converter over range V_FS has an approximate step $\Delta V=V_{FS}/2^N$ under the stated bin convention. Quantization noise formulas require assumptions about signal and error statistics. Saturation, nonlinearity, missing codes and reference drift can dominate that ideal step.

Sensor impedance/loading, amplifier gain, grounding and instrument bandwidth alter what reaches the converter. A voltage probe belongs across the relevant element; current sensing uses a different circuit arrangement. Use only appropriately rated low-risk equipment in a real experiment; the app bench supplies a model, not authorization to handle hazardous circuits.

Record timestamps, units, gain, filter, range and calibration with raw samples. A transformed plot without raw-data provenance is not a reproducible measurement. Synthetic test signals can verify software but cannot independently calibrate every physical sensor.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="worked" title="A worked argument" layer="worked" fullOnly>

For a 12-bit ideal converter over 4.096 V, the step is .001 V. A 100 Hz bandlimit needs a sampling rate above 200 samples/s under the ideal theorem, with practical guard/filter design still required.

</LessonSection>
