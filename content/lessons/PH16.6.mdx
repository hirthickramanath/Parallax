<LessonSection id="story" title="Why the question matters" layer="story">

FLRW solutions link EOS to scale-factor histories, but symmetry and coordinate choices must remain distinct from observations.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="concept" title="Definitions and hypotheses" layer="concept">

An FLRW background assumes homogeneous isotropic spatial slices with a scale factor; solving its evolution requires stress content and compatible initial parameters.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="theory" title="Derivation, proof and limitations" layer="theory" fullOnly>

In a flat Λ=0 perfect-fluid model with mass-equivalent ρ, $H^2=8\pi G\rho/3$ and conservation gives ρ∝a⁻³⁽¹⁺ʷ⁾ for constant w. Solving yields $a\propto t^{2/[3(1+w)]}$ in the admitted expanding power-law branch with its origin convention.

Dust gives a∝t²ᐟ³ and radiation a∝t¹ᐟ². A positive cosmological constant dominating a flat expanding solution instead yields exponential expansion. Mixed components require their joint equations and need not follow one power at all epochs.

Spatial curvature and particle/event horizons are different concepts. The particle horizon integrates past null travel; an event horizon depends on future global expansion. A finite Hubble radius alone does not answer every causal-access question.

Perturbations, light-cone observations and parameter inference extend the background solution. A coordinate scale factor is not a claim that every bound object follows the same stretching. Energy bookkeeping in an expanding spacetime differs from one Newtonian globally conserved box energy.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="interactive" title="Investigate the claim" layer="interactive">

Integrate dust, radiation and Λ toy backgrounds separately, then a mixed model. Check density conservation and distinguish Hubble from integrated horizon scales.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="worked" title="A worked argument" layer="worked" fullOnly>

For a dust power-law branch, increasing time by eight increases a by four. For radiation, increasing time by four doubles a. These ratios apply within their separate single-component regimes.

</LessonSection>
