<LessonSection id="theory" title="Derivation, proof and limitations" layer="theory" fullOnly>

Accretion power is parameterized $L=\eta\dot Mc^2$ with a model-dependent radiative efficiency. A luminosity-derived inflow estimate does not automatically equal black-hole mass growth because radiation and outflows remove energy/mass. Eddington ratios compare with a specified opacity-based force scale.

Host gas can feed accretion and star formation; AGN radiation, jets and winds can change gas thermodynamics and motion. Mechanical and radiative coupling efficiencies are environment dependent. A correlation is compatible with several shared-supply or feedback histories and does not by itself establish one direction of causation.

Broad-line, stellar/gas-dynamical, reverberation and other mass estimates have different geometry and calibration assumptions. Orientation and obscuration influence classification and visibility. Variability time scales differ greatly from galaxy-evolution averages, so a snapshot AGN luminosity need not correlate tightly with simultaneous host star formation even if long-term growth is related.

Population inferences require flux limits, duty cycles, nondetections and redshift evolution. A fitted black-hole/host relation can be biased by which masses were measurable. Compare independent observables and simulations with their selection functions rather than claiming co-evolution from one scatter plot alone.


### Derive, test and transfer

Balance outward electron-scattering radiation force with inward gravity in a spherical ionized gas. Since both scale as inverse square radius, the limiting luminosity is $L_{Edd}=4\pi GMc/\kappa$ for an adopted opacity κ. The Eddington ratio λ=L/LEdd separates a luminosity scale from mass, but requires a bolometric correction and opacity convention.

Observed black-hole/host correlations can be affected by selection, shared distance errors and how masses are measured. They motivate feedback and co-evolution models but do not alone establish a unique causal mechanism. Disc geometry, anisotropy and radiation trapping permit behavior outside a naive spherical balance. The display is a normalized budget, not an inference of a host's formation history.

</LessonSection>
