<LessonSection id="story" title="Why the question matters" layer="story">

Multielectron spectra combine Pauli restrictions, interactions and angular coupling; filling rules are model tendencies rather than unconditional theorems.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="intuition" title="Build a mental model" layer="intuition">

<LessonSection id="concept" title="Definitions and hypotheses" layer="concept">

In LS coupling, a term symbol is written ²ˢ⁺¹L_J with total electronic spin S, orbital L and total J.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="theory" title="Derivation, proof and limitations" layer="theory" fullOnly>

For an LS term, $J=|L-S|,|L-S|+1,\ldots,L+S$ when allowed by antisymmetry and configuration. The letters S,P,D,F correspond to L=0,1,2,3. The leading superscript is spin multiplicity, not electron count.

Electron–electron interaction, exchange and spin-orbit coupling compete. Hund-type ordering rules apply within qualified configurations/regimes; equivalent-electron Pauli constraints forbid some naive couplings. A term list cannot be obtained by adding every label independently.

LS coupling is useful when electrostatic coupling dominates appropriate spin-orbit mixing. Heavy atoms or particular configurations can require jj or intermediate coupling. State labels then describe dominant character rather than exact separate S and L conservation.

Observed multiplets and selection patterns test models with configuration interaction and correlation. Orbital energies do not simply sum to every measured excitation energy. A spectroscopic assignment must retain isotope, charge state and field/environment context.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="interactive" title="Investigate the claim" layer="interactive">

Enumerate angular couplings for a specified allowed configuration, then impose equivalent-electron restrictions. Compare LS and jj limiting descriptions without claiming one universal scheme.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="worked" title="A worked argument" layer="worked" fullOnly>

For an allowed L=1,S=1 term, possible J values are 0,1,2. Their magnetic-state counts are 1,3,5, totaling nine and matching (2L+1)(2S+1)=9.

</LessonSection>
