<LessonSection id="concept" title="Definitions and hypotheses" layer="concept">

A smooth n-manifold is locally modeled by open subsets of Rⁿ with smooth compatible transition maps, together with the standard topological conditions.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="theory" title="Derivation, proof and limitations" layer="theory" fullOnly>

A transition $x\prime=f(x)$ has $\det Df\ne0$ on its overlap. A chart assigns local coordinates on an open piece of the space. Overlap maps must be smooth invertible maps with smooth inverses for a smooth atlas. A global coordinate system is not required, and some manifolds cannot be covered by one chart. Sphere poles show the limitations of familiar longitude/latitude coordinates.

A tangent vector can be defined through directional derivatives on smooth functions, not as an arrow embedded in an arbitrary surrounding Euclidean space. Its chart components change by a Jacobian. The derivative of a smooth map is a linear map between tangent spaces, expressed locally by that Jacobian.

A differential form or metric is additional structure on the manifold, not already provided by coordinates alone. Smooth topology, distance and curvature answer different questions. Two different metrics can live on the same smooth manifold.

The rank of a map controls local regularity through inverse/implicit-function theorems under their hypotheses. A vanishing coordinate determinant can indicate a bad chart rather than a physical divergence. To diagnose a true singularity, seek coordinate-independent structure and the relevant extendibility/geodesic information rather than relying on one plotted component.


### Derivation and transfer

A coordinate chart maps an open region of a manifold to an open Euclidean region. Polar coordinates fail at the origin and need angle branches even though the plane itself is smooth. A chart singularity does not automatically signal a geometric singularity.

For x=r cosθ,y=r sinθ, the area Jacobian magnitude is r. The display follows one circle in its Cartesian x projection. It does not cover a sphere with one globally nonsingular chart or equate a projection with an embedding of every manifold.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="worked" title="A worked argument" layer="worked" fullOnly>

The chart rescaling x′=3x on an interval has Jacobian 3 and inverse Jacobian 1/3. It is a smooth valid transition on its stated domain. A longitude coordinate at a pole lacks such a nonsingular chart relation there, while the sphere remains smooth.
