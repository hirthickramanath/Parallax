<LessonSection id="story" title="Why this matters" layer="story">

A graph is a model connecting inputs to outputs. In astronomy, a calibration curve turns detector counts into a physical estimate. The allowed inputs and the invertibility of the calibration matter as much as its formula.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="intuition" title="Build the picture" layer="intuition">

Picture a function as a machine with an admission rule. Two inputs may produce one output, but one admitted input cannot produce two outputs. An inverse exists on the image only when the machine never merges different inputs.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="concept" title="State the model" layer="concept">

A function assigns exactly one output to each input in its domain. Solving an equation or inequality means finding the inputs in that domain that satisfy it.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="theory" title="Derive and qualify the result" layer="theory" fullOnly>

For $f(x)=1/(x-2)$ the real domain excludes 2. The graph has a vertical asymptote there, but the asymptote is not an admitted value. A composition $f(g(x))$ requires both that $x$ belongs to the domain of $g$ and that $g(x)$ belongs to the domain of $f$.

Adding the same real number preserves an inequality. Multiplying by a positive number preserves its direction; multiplying by a negative number reverses it. Multiplication by an expression of unknown sign requires cases. For example, solve $(x-1)/(x+2)>0$ by dividing the real line at the zero 1 and excluded point −2. The numerator and denominator have equal signs on $(-\infty,-2)$ and $(1,\infty)$. Neither endpoint is included.

Squaring is reversible only with sign information: $x=3$ implies $x^2=9$, but the latter also allows −3. Similarly, taking square roots means the principal nonnegative root. For $\sqrt{x+1}=x-1$, the domain and right-hand sign demand $x\ge1$. Squaring yields $x(x-3)=0$; substitution rejects 0 and keeps 3.

A transformation $y=a f(b(x-h))+k$ changes the graph horizontally about $h$ and vertically about $k$. Horizontal scale is $1/|b|$, not $|b|$. A function is one-to-one if distinct inputs have distinct outputs; restricting $x^2$ to $x\ge0$ makes its inverse $\sqrt{x}$. Always state the inverse domain.


### Transfer derivation and failure case

For a rational inequality, isolate zeros of the numerator and denominator before testing signs on the remaining intervals. A denominator zero is never included even if multiplication appears to remove it. In $\sqrt{x+6}=x$, the domain requires $x\geq0$; squaring yields $(x-3)(x+2)=0$, and substitution eliminates $-2$. An implication produced by squaring is not automatically an equivalence. For $5-2x>11$, subtract 5 and divide by the negative coefficient to get $x<-3$; a test point such as $x=-4$ checks the direction.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="worked" title="A worked example" layer="worked" fullOnly>

Solve 2−3x>8. Subtract 2 to get −3x>6, then divide by −3 and reverse the direction: $x<-2$. Checking x=−3 gives 11>8. The boundary −2 gives equality and is excluded.
