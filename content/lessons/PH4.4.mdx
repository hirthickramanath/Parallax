<LessonSection id="puzzle" title="Commit to a prediction" layer="puzzle">

Why can a neutral object still produce an electric field far away? State a prediction and a reason before reading the argument.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="story" title="Why the question matters" layer="story">

Multipole expansions separate the effects of total charge, dipole arrangement and finer structure. They approximate a localized source at distances larger than its size, not everywhere in space.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="intuition" title="Build a mental model" layer="intuition">

From far away, coarse charge moments matter before fine details. Canceling total charge removes only the first term, not every spatial imbalance.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="concept" title="Definitions and hypotheses" layer="concept">

For a localized charge distribution, the exterior potential can be expanded in inverse observation distance. Moments depend on origin according to their order and lower moments.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="theory" title="Derivation, proof and limitations" layer="theory" fullOnly>

For observation r outside a source region with |r′|&lt;|r|, expand the Coulomb kernel in powers of source size divided by observation radius. The leading terms are

$$V(r)=\frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\left[\frac Qr+\frac{p\cdot\hat r}{r^2}+\frac{1}{2r^3}\sum_{ij}Q_{ij}\hat r_i\hat r_j+\cdots\right].$$

Here Q=∫ρdV, p=∫r′ρdV and one common traceless quadrupole convention is $Q_{ij}=\int\rho(3x_i'x_j'-r'^2\delta_{ij})dV'$. Other quadrupole conventions move factors, so definitions must accompany formulas.

For a neutral pair ±q separated by vector d from negative to positive, p=qd. The far-field potential is kp cosθ/r², and differentiating gives a field of order r⁻³. On the dipole axis E=2kp/r³ along the appropriate axial direction, while the equatorial field points opposite p with magnitude kp/r³. These are far-field formulas, not exact near either point charge.

A shift of origin by a changes dipole moment to p′=p−Qa. Thus p is origin-independent for a neutral localized distribution, but not when total charge is nonzero. A quadrupole also mixes with lower moments under translation. Moment labels describe a chosen expansion, not an arbitrary coordinate-free decomposition at every order.

For a dipole in a slowly varying external field, U≈−p·E and torque τ≈p×E. A permanent dipole and an induced dipole have different energy bookkeeping: polarizing an induced dipole usually introduces the familiar half factor in the linear response energy. State whether p is fixed or produced by the applied field.

Truncation error is controlled by the source/observation size ratio under suitable bounded-source assumptions. Symmetry can force some moments to vanish; a missing monopole does not imply a missing dipole. At comparable distances, sum the actual charge distribution or retain more terms rather than extending the leading asymptotic term beyond its regime.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="worked" title="A worked argument" layer="worked" fullOnly>

For a neutral far-field dipole with k=1 and p=3, the axial field magnitude at r=2 is 2×3/8=0.75, provided r is much larger than its physical charge separation. Doubling r reduces that leading field by a factor of eight.

</LessonSection>
