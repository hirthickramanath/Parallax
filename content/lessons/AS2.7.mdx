<LessonSection id="story" title="Why the question matters" layer="story">

Multi-messenger astronomy combines electromagnetic observations with gravitational waves, neutrinos or other particles. Association is an inference problem involving timing, location, event rates and selection, not merely a memorable coincidence.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="intuition" title="Build a mental model" layer="intuition">

Each instrument supplies an uncertain patch of information. An association becomes stronger when their overlaps are unlikely under a carefully defined background model.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="concept" title="Definitions and hypotheses" layer="concept">

A multi-messenger association compares a common-source hypothesis with alternatives using joint data, detector responses and selection effects. Messengers can be emitted at different stages and arrive with different delays.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="theory" title="Derivation, proof and limitations" layer="theory" fullOnly>

For hypotheses H and B, posterior odds equal prior odds times the likelihood ratio $P(D|H)/P(D|B)$. Conditional independence can permit a product of messenger likelihoods given source parameters, but those parameters must be integrated or jointly inferred. Multiplying separately normalized posterior maps without accounting for priors can double-count prior information.

A timing window is justified by emission physics, propagation and instrumental timing uncertainty. A nonzero arrival delay does not by itself measure a propagation-speed difference because intrinsic emission times can differ. For a small speed difference over known distance d, an approximate propagation delay scales as d times the inverse-speed difference, but cosmological propagation requires the corresponding model.

Localization regions are probability distributions, not guaranteed hard-edged boxes. Joint localization should combine compatible likelihood and prior information and account for coordinate, time and calibration conventions. False-coincidence rates depend on background event rates, search windows and the number of trials or follow-ups considered.

Selection effects alter the observed population. A sample detected by two instruments need not represent every event detected by either instrument, let alone the underlying source population. Nondetection can constrain a source model only after exposure, sensitivity, viewing geometry and expected emission are specified.

Gravitational waves constrain dynamical masses, orientations and distances through a waveform model; photons can constrain environment, host redshift and emission processes; neutrinos can probe particle acceleration or dense-core processes. None automatically supplies a complete mechanism alone. A joint interpretation should report model dependence, calibration errors, data provenance and alternative associations rather than converting temporal proximity into certainty.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="worked" title="A worked argument" layer="worked" fullOnly>

If prior odds for a common source are 1:100 and a justified joint likelihood ratio is 1000, posterior odds become 10:1, giving probability 10/11≈0.909 under this two-hypothesis model. This calculation is illustrative and does not certify any real event association.
