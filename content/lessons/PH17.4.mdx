<LessonSection id="theory" title="Derivation, proof and limitations" layer="theory" fullOnly>

For conservative dynamics, $m_i\ddot r_i=-\nabla_iU$. Pair symmetry can enforce internal momentum balance. A correct force routine still needs a stable, converged time step and consistent units/masses. Discontinuous cutoffs can create energy errors.

Velocity Verlet updates position using old acceleration and velocity with both old/new accelerations. It is a symplectic/time-reversible scheme for the admitted separable Hamiltonian setting, not an exact finite-step solution. A bounded energy oscillation and a systematic drift require different diagnostics.

Periodic boundaries, minimum-image assumptions and neighbour lists require a compatible cutoff and cell geometry. Long-range forces need appropriate methods rather than an arbitrary short truncation. Initial states and equilibration affect averages.

Thermostats/barostats change dynamics/ensembles and require their own sampling justification. Naively rescaling velocities can distort correlations. Statistical errors need autocorrelation analysis, and force-field error remains even after numerical convergence. Do not call a classical force-field trajectory a first-principles quantum result.


### Derivation and transfer

For a Lennard–Jones pair U=4ε(r^−12−r^−6) in σ=1 units, radial force is −dU/dr=24ε(2r^−13−r^−7). It vanishes at the potential minimum, while a zero potential at r=1 need not mean zero force.

The display probes instantaneous pair force. A molecular dynamics project additionally needs initial states, integrator, timestep tests, neighbor handling, boundaries and conserved-quantity diagnostics. An analytic force curve is not a completed many-particle trajectory simulation.

</LessonSection>
