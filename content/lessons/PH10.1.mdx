<LessonSection id="story" title="Why the question matters" layer="story">

The Klein–Gordon equation is a relativistic field equation; its conserved charge density is not an ordinary positive one-particle probability density.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="intuition" title="Build a mental model" layer="intuition">

<LessonSection id="concept" title="Definitions and hypotheses" layer="concept">

For a free scalar field in flat spacetime, the Klein–Gordon equation follows from E²=p²c²+m²c⁴ with a specified metric and operator convention.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="theory" title="Derivation, proof and limitations" layer="theory" fullOnly>

With signature (+−−−), $[\Box+(mc/\hbar)^2]\phi=0$, where □=c⁻²∂ₜ²−∇². Plane waves obey ω²=c²k²+m²c⁴/ℏ². Both frequency branches occur; discarding one by an informal rule does not provide a general interacting relativistic particle theory.

A complex scalar has a conserved U(1) current built from φ*∂^μφ−φ∂^μφ*. Its time component can have either sign, so it is not the Schrödinger positive probability density for arbitrary solutions. Quantum field theory interprets the corresponding charge and particle/antiparticle sectors consistently.

The equation is second order in time and needs field and time-derivative initial data with suitable regularity/boundary conditions. A real scalar has no independent complex phase charge of this type, although its energy and other symmetries still matter.

External interactions and particle production require a consistent field framework. Classical positive energy density and quantum charge density are different objects. The nonrelativistic limit can recover a Schrödinger-type envelope after factoring a rapid rest-energy phase under a controlled small-momentum/slow-envelope approximation.


### Derivation and transfer

A free Klein–Gordon plane wave obeys ω²=c²k²+(mc²/ℏ)². Positive- and negative-frequency solutions appear, and a relativistic quantum-field interpretation resolves issues with treating the naive density as a single-particle probability.

The display follows a positive-frequency branch in c=ℏ=1 units. It is not a field quantization or an assertion that a massive particle group velocity equals its phase velocity. The group velocity k/ω stays below one for positive mass.

</LessonSection>
