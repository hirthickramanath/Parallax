<LessonSection id="story" title="Why the question matters" layer="story">

Vector potential encodes the divergence-free magnetic field, while magnetization separates free and material currents. Gauge choices and material models simplify calculations without changing observables.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="intuition" title="Build a mental model" layer="intuition">

The curl of a gradient vanishes, so adding a gradient can change the potential arrows without changing B. Material dipole loops contribute extra currents that must be counted consistently.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="concept" title="Definitions and hypotheses" layer="concept">

A magnetic vector potential satisfies B=∇×A on a suitable domain. Magnetization M is magnetic dipole moment per volume, and H=B/μ₀−M in the standard macroscopic convention.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="theory" title="Derivation, proof and limitations" layer="theory" fullOnly>

The identity ∇·(∇×A)=0 makes a vector potential compatible with the magnetic Gauss law. A transformation $A\mapsto A+\nabla\chi$ leaves B unchanged where χ is sufficiently smooth. Global topology and singularities can complicate a single potential representation; a local vector formula does not automatically settle every global domain.

In magnetostatics, choosing Coulomb gauge ∇·A=0 converts ∇×B=μ₀J into $\nabla^2A=-\mu_0J$ under appropriate conditions. For a localized conserved current in unbounded vacuum, a corresponding solution is $A(r)=\mu_0\int J(r')/|r-r'|\,dV'/(4\pi)$ with suitable decay. Gauge choice does not add a new physical force.

For uniform B=B₀ez, one convenient potential is A=(−B₀y/2,B₀x/2,0). Another is A=(0,B₀x,0). Their curls agree, and their difference is the gradient of B₀xy/2. A field prediction must depend on B or the appropriate gauge-invariant quantity, not an arbitrary value of A at one point.

Magnetization contributes bound current $J_b=\nabla\times M$ and surface current $K_b=M\times n$ when the exterior magnetization is zero. Defining H gives ∇×H=Jfree in magnetostatics. A uniform M can produce no volume bound current but still a surface current, analogous to uniform electric polarization producing surface charge.

For a linear isotropic simple response, M=χmH and B=μH with μ=μ₀(1+χm). Ferromagnetic hysteresis, saturation, anisotropy and frequency response violate a universal constant-μ picture. Magnetic energy and force in matter require specified constraints and material response; the vacuum B²/(2μ₀) formula cannot be blindly substituted as a complete energy model for every magnetic medium.


### Derive, test and transfer

The vector potential satisfies B=curl A, so adding gradχ leaves B unchanged when mixed derivatives commute on the relevant smooth domain. This gauge freedom means a particular A value is not itself a unique local magnetic field observable. Physical fields and the boundary/topological setting must remain consistent under the transformation.

For a uniform z-directed field, one gauge is A=(−By/2,Bx/2,0). Computing its curl recovers Bz=B. In materials, H and magnetization separate source conventions in a macroscopic constitutive description; a scalar susceptibility is an approximation with limits. A local smooth curl identity does not settle every global flux/topology question.

</LessonSection>
