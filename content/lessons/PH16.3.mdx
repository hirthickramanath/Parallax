<LessonSection id="concept" title="Definitions and hypotheses" layer="concept">

The Schwarzschild exterior is a static spherical vacuum solution with scale r_s=2GM/c² and areal-radius coordinate r.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="theory" title="Derivation, proof and limitations" layer="theory" fullOnly>

One signature form is $ds^2=(1-r_s/r)c^2dt^2-(1-r_s/r)^{-1}dr^2-r^2d\Omega^2$. This exterior chart fails at r_s, but a suitable extension can cross a horizon regularly. The central curvature singularity is a different invariant issue.

For stationary clocks in the static exterior, proper time satisfies dτ=√(1−r_s/r)dt; such stationary observers require acceleration and do not remain timelike stationary inside the horizon. A redshift comparison must specify emitter and observer motion as well as gravitational location.

Timelike/null geodesics yield perihelion advance, light deflection and time-delay predictions with an admitted approximation. Weak-field light bending is approximately 4GM/(bc²). This is not the full strong-lensing angle for impact parameters near capture.

The metric applies outside a suitable spherical nonrotating source, not every rotating star or disc. Stable circular orbit limits, photon orbits and horizons are distinct radii. A coordinate plot of slowing infall is not proof that a falling observer never crosses the horizon in finite proper time.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="interactive" title="Investigate the claim" layer="interactive">

Compare radial null curves in Schwarzschild and a horizon-regular chart. Compute a weak-field light-bending example and state where the expansion fails.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="worked" title="A worked argument" layer="worked" fullOnly>

For GM=3,c²=2, r_s=3. At radius r=4r_s, a static clock has dτ/dt=√(3/4). The clock formula is tied to a static exterior observer, not arbitrary free fall.

</LessonSection>
