<LessonSection id="story" title="Why the question matters" layer="story">

Lagrangian mechanics organizes constrained dynamics through energy and virtual variations. Its power comes from choosing coordinates adapted to the problem, while still accounting for the actual constraint and force assumptions.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="intuition" title="Build a mental model" layer="intuition">

Instead of resolving every Cartesian force component, describe the allowed configurations and ask which path makes the action stationary.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="concept" title="Definitions and hypotheses" layer="concept">

For a regular conservative mechanical system, L=T−V in generalized coordinates and stationary fixed-endpoint action gives the Euler–Lagrange equations. Velocity-dependent potentials or nonconservative forces require appropriate extensions.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="theory" title="Derivation, proof and limitations" layer="theory" fullOnly>

For coordinates qi, the action is S=∫L(q,q̇,t)dt. Endpoint-zero variations give $d(\partial L/\partial\dot q_i)/dt-\partial L/\partial q_i=0$. This is stationarity, not a universal minimum principle. The chosen coordinates must describe the admitted configurations without imposing false independence at a coordinate singularity.

For a particle in a central plane potential, T=m(ṙ²+r²θ̇²)/2 and L=T−V(r). The angular equation gives d(mr²θ̇)/dt=0. The radial equation gives mr̈=mrθ̇²−V′(r). The outward-looking angular term arises from coordinate geometry and angular motion, not a new central repulsive interaction.

Canonical momenta are $p_i=\partial L/\partial\dot q_i$. They need not equal Cartesian mass times coordinate speed. In the polar example pθ=mr²θ̇ is angular momentum, while pr=mṙ is radial momentum. Dimensions follow the coordinate definition.

Generalized nonconservative forces can be included as d(Lq̇)/dt−Lq=Qi under the appropriate virtual-work formulation. A Rayleigh dissipation function quadratic in velocities models certain linear drag forces through Qi=−∂R/∂q̇i; it is not a universal recipe for every dissipative process.

Adding dF(q,t)/dt to L leaves the fixed-endpoint interior equations unchanged but shifts canonical momenta by ∂F/∂q. Derive observables from the complete formulation rather than identifying one gauge-dependent canonical expression with a measured mechanical quantity in every setting.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="worked" title="A worked argument" layer="worked" fullOnly>

For a small-angle pendulum with mass m and length ℓ, L≈mℓ²θ̇²/2−mgℓθ²/2 up to a constant. Euler–Lagrange gives θ̈+(g/ℓ)θ=0. For g=10 and ℓ=2, angular frequency squared is 5; mass cancels under the ideal assumptions.

</LessonSection>
