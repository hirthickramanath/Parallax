<LessonSection id="story" title="Why the question matters" layer="story">

Schrödinger and Heisenberg pictures redistribute time dependence without changing physical probabilities.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="concept" title="Definitions and hypotheses" layer="concept">

For a time-independent self-adjoint Hamiltonian, U(t)=exp(−iHt/ℏ). Schrödinger states evolve with U; Heisenberg observables evolve as U†AU.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="theory" title="Derivation, proof and limitations" layer="theory" fullOnly>

Unitarity preserves norms and transition probabilities. In the Schrödinger picture |ψ(t)⟩=U(t)|ψ(0)⟩ with fixed operators unless they have explicit time dependence. In the Heisenberg picture $A_H(t)=U^\dagger(t)A_S(t)U(t)$ and the reference state stays fixed. Substitution proves that expectation values in the two pictures agree.

Differentiation gives $dA_H/dt=(i/\hbar)[H_H,A_H]+(\partial A_S/\partial t)_H$. For a time-independent operator commuting with H, expectation is conserved under the stated domain assumptions. An explicitly time-dependent operator requires the extra derivative term even if a commutator happens to vanish.

For a time-dependent Hamiltonian, a simple exponential of the time integral works only when the relevant Hamiltonians commute at different times. Generally the propagator is time ordered and can be approximated by controlled short-time products. The interaction picture separates a chosen solvable part from the perturbation; the split is a calculation choice, not a new physical theory.

For a wave packet in a smooth potential, Ehrenfest gives d⟨x⟩/dt=⟨p⟩/m and d⟨p⟩/dt=−⟨V′(x)⟩ when valid. Replacing ⟨V′⟩ by V′(⟨x⟩) is exact for the corresponding linear force but not for every potential. Closed-system unitary evolution does not itself model every open-system dissipative state; reduced dynamics can require a density matrix and a justified environment model.


### Derivation and transfer

A superposition of two energies acquires relative phase ΔEt/ℏ. Measuring in the energy basis leaves populations unchanged, but another basis can show oscillating probabilities. Schrödinger and Heisenberg pictures place the same dynamics in states or operators and agree on observables.

The explorer uses an initially equal superposition and a complementary basis, giving P+=(1+cosΔt)/2. It does not describe spontaneous decay, dephasing or a collapse occurring merely because time passes.

</LessonSection>
