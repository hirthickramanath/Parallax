<LessonSection id="story" title="Why the question matters" layer="story">

Renormalization separates intermediate regulator choices from matched observables and organizes scale dependence and effective-theory uncertainty.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="intuition" title="Build a mental model" layer="intuition">

Start with the concrete worked example below. Track which quantities belong to the system, which are imposed by its surroundings, and which conclusions require a limiting approximation.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="concept" title="Definitions and hypotheses" layer="concept">

Regularization defines singular expressions; renormalization fixes parameters/operators through conditions so predictions at a stated order can be compared across scales.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="theory" title="Derivation, proof and limitations" layer="theory" fullOnly>

A cutoff or dimensional regulator changes intermediate integrals. Counterterms consistent with symmetry and power counting absorb dependence into defined parameters; simply deleting every large term is not a justified procedure. The renormalization condition specifies what measured or matched quantity fixes a parameter.

A running coupling obeys a beta function $\beta(g)=dg/d\ln\mu$ under this energy-scale convention. Coefficients and coupling definitions can be scheme dependent, while consistently matched observables agree to the admitted accuracy. A coupling is not a universal scale-independent number in every process.

For an EFT, higher-dimension operators are suppressed by a high scale under a stated power counting. Nonrenormalizable in the old finite-counterterm sense does not imply useless: it can be predictive to fixed order with finitely many needed coefficients. Extrapolation past the cutoff is not justified.

Perturbative series can be asymptotic, and large logarithms may require resummation. A regulator-independent answer still has truncation, input and model uncertainties. Naturalness arguments and speculative ultraviolet completions are not direct experimental measurements.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="interactive" title="Investigate the claim" layer="interactive">

Compare a regulated toy integral under two cutoffs after fixing the same low-energy condition. Track residual truncation dependence and the expansion parameter rather than declaring all uncertainty removed.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="worked" title="A worked argument" layer="worked" fullOnly>

For a toy beta law dg/dlnμ=.1 at effectively constant beta over a step Δlnμ=2, Δg≈.2. This linear approximation is not a universal QFT running law. A term suppressed as (E/Λ)² is .01 at E/Λ=.1.

</LessonSection>
