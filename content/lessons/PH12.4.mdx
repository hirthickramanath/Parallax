Why does a molecular spectrum contain separate rotational and vibrational scales? State a prediction and a reason before reading the argument.

<LessonSection id="story" title="Why the question matters" layer="story">

Nuclear motion on electronic potential surfaces produces coupled level patterns whose simple limits have explicit mass and rigidity assumptions.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="intuition" title="Build a mental model" layer="intuition">

Start with the concrete worked example below. Track which quantities belong to the system, which are imposed by its surroundings, and which conclusions require a limiting approximation.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="concept" title="Definitions and hypotheses" layer="concept">

A diatomic rigid rotor has E_J=ℏ²J(J+1)/(2I), while a harmonic vibration has E_v=ℏω(v+1/2) with reduced mass in ω.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="theory" title="Derivation, proof and limitations" layer="theory" fullOnly>

The moment of inertia is I=μr_e² in a two-nucleus rigid-geometry model. Rotational transition spacings grow with J for the corresponding dipole-allowed rotor lines. Homonuclear symmetry and nuclear-spin statistics alter which states/intensities occur; a classical rotor diagram alone does not determine all selection rules.

Near a stable potential minimum, $\omega=\sqrt{k/\mu}$ gives harmonic vibrational spacing. Anharmonicity, rotation-vibration coupling and electronic-surface changes alter high-excitation levels. A harmonic potential has no dissociation limit, so it cannot predict bond breaking without extension.

A permanent dipole is needed for elementary electric-dipole pure rotational activity in the stated approximation, while vibration requires a changing dipole. Raman response uses polarizability and different rules. Absence in one channel does not imply absence of molecular motion or of the molecule.

Born–Oppenheimer separation relies on scale structure and can fail near electronic degeneracies or strong nonadiabatic coupling. Spectral assignments need isotopologues, temperature populations, linewidths and instrument resolution. A supplied toy line list is not an observed molecular detection.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="worked" title="A worked argument" layer="worked" fullOnly>

Let B=ℏ²/(2I)=2 energy units. J=1 has energy 4 and J=2 energy 12, so the 2→1 energy difference is 8. Quadrupling I reduces B by four at fixed ℏ.

</LessonSection>
