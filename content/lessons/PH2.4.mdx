<LessonSection id="puzzle" title="Commit to a prediction" layer="puzzle">

Why is a momentum coordinate not simply an extra independent velocity? State a prediction and a reason before reading the argument.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="story" title="Why the question matters" layer="story">

Hamiltonian mechanics trades velocities for momenta through a Legendre transformation. The transformation requires regularity, and phase-space coordinates come with a geometric structure beyond a list of numbers.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="intuition" title="Build a mental model" layer="intuition">

For a regular kinetic model, momenta encode the velocities invertibly. The Hamiltonian reorganizes the same equations into paired first-order evolution equations.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="concept" title="Definitions and hypotheses" layer="concept">

When the velocity Hessian of L is invertible, define p=∂L/∂q̇ and solve for q̇(q,p,t). The Hamiltonian is H=p·q̇−L after that substitution.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="theory" title="Derivation, proof and limitations" layer="theory" fullOnly>

Differentiate H using the defining momentum relation. The q̇ differentials cancel, leaving $dH=\sum_i\dot q_i\,dp_i-\sum_i L_{q_i}\,dq_i-L_tdt$. Euler–Lagrange therefore gives Hamilton’s equations $\dot q_i=\partial H/\partial p_i$ and $\dot p_i=-\partial H/\partial q_i$. Explicit time dependence supplies ∂H/∂t=−∂L/∂t at corresponding variables.

For L=mq̇²/2−V(q), p=mq̇ and H=p²/(2m)+V. For a charged particle with L=mv²/2+qA·v−qφ, canonical momentum is p=mv+qA, and H=|p−qA|²/(2m)+qφ in this nonrelativistic model. Canonical and mechanical momenta must not be interchanged without the vector-potential term.

For smooth canonical Hamiltonian flow, phase-space divergence is ∑(∂²H/∂qi∂pi−∂²H/∂pi∂qi)=0. This yields Liouville volume preservation under the stated hypotheses. It does not mean every projected position-space area is conserved, nor that dissipative dynamics can be made Hamiltonian by simply renaming drag as a potential.

Energy conservation requires absence of explicit time dependence in H. A time-dependent canonical coordinate transformation can change the Hamiltonian by the appropriate generating term, so one must distinguish the conserved physical quantity from an arbitrary transformed expression.

If the velocity Hessian is singular, the naive Legendre inversion fails. Constraints, gauge redundancy and a constrained Hamiltonian formulation may then be required. A zero determinant is a structural warning, not a license to divide by it numerically.

</LessonSection>

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="worked" title="A worked argument" layer="worked" fullOnly>

For m=2, V=q²/2 and state q=3,p=4, H=16/4+9/2=8.5. Hamilton’s equations give q̇=p/m=2 and ṗ=−q=−3. Direct differentiation of H along this motion gives zero.

</LessonSection>
