<LessonSection id="story" title="Why the question matters" layer="story">

Radiation is identified by far-field energy flux, not merely by the presence of time-dependent fields near an observer.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="concept" title="Definitions and hypotheses" layer="concept">

The radiation zone contains field terms falling as 1/R whose integrated outward energy flux remains finite as the observation sphere grows; near-zone terms decay faster.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="theory" title="Derivation, proof and limitations" layer="theory" fullOnly>

Retarded fields of a localized time-varying source contain near, induction and radiation contributions. A 1/R radiation amplitude produces an energy flux density of order 1/R², which can integrate over an area proportional to R² to a finite outgoing power. Faster-decaying Coulomb-like terms do not alone yield that same asymptotic power.

For a nonrelativistic point charge with acceleration magnitude a, the Larmor result is $P=q^2a^2/(6\pi\epsilon_0c^3)$ in SI. Its dipole angular dependence is proportional to sin²θ relative to the acceleration direction in the instantaneous nonrelativistic approximation. Uniform straight motion has no such acceleration radiation in an inertial frame, although fields can still vary at a fixed observer as the charge passes.

For a localized source much smaller than a wavelength, electric-dipole radiation is governed by the second time derivative of p(t)=∫rρ dV. A monochromatic dipole’s time-averaged power scales as ω⁴ times dipole amplitude squared, with peak/RMS conventions stated. Magnetic dipole and electric quadrupole terms become necessary when leading dipole radiation vanishes or higher multipoles matter.

Relativistic radiation is beamed and requires the appropriate relativistic formula and retarded geometry. A bound charge inside matter also couples to material response, and a collection of charges can interfere coherently or incoherently. Adding individual powers is not always equivalent to adding fields and then forming the flux. The point-charge radiation model has short-distance and self-force limitations.


### Derive, test and transfer

For a nonrelativistic accelerating point charge in vacuum, the Larmor power is proportional to q²a² with the familiar SI coefficient q²/(6πε₀c³). Constant velocity does not generate this acceleration-radiation term, though static/velocity fields still exist. Far-zone radiation fields and near-zone energy storage have different distance scalings.

The power formula alone does not give an observed spectrum, polarization or angular pattern without a trajectory. Relativistic motion requires the appropriate generalization and can beam radiation strongly. Feeding a radiated-power estimate back as a local self-force is a separate radiation-reaction problem with subtleties; it is not justified by merely subtracting energy in an arbitrary time step.

</LessonSection>
