<LessonSection id="concept" title="Definitions and hypotheses" layer="concept">

Use work done by the system as positive: dU=δQ−δW. For a simple compressible equilibrium system of fixed composition, dU=T dS−p dV.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="theory" title="Derivation, proof and limitations" layer="theory" fullOnly>

Heat and work label energy transfer processes, not separate state functions stored in matter. For a quasistatic pressure-volume process, work is ∫p_ext dV; replacing external pressure by system pressure requires the appropriate mechanical-equilibrium limit. A free expansion into vacuum can have zero work even while the volume changes.

For a reversible heat transfer δQ_rev=T dS. The Clausius inequality gives dS≥δQ/T_b for a system exchanging heat with a single boundary reservoir at temperature T_b, with the full surroundings included when entropy production is assessed. An isolated system has nondecreasing total entropy, but the entropy of an open subsystem may decrease by exporting entropy. Reversibility is not equivalent to simply operating slowly if friction or finite gradients remain.

Legendre transformations give $F=U-TS$, H=U+pV and G=U−TS+pV. For fixed composition, $dF=-S\,dT-p\,dV$ and $dG=-S\,dT+V\,dp$. Thus at fixed T,V and appropriate allowed variations F is minimized in stable equilibrium; at fixed T,p the corresponding potential is G. These statements include reservoir contributions and are not unconstrained rules to minimize all energies simultaneously.

Equality of mixed derivatives produces Maxwell relations such as (∂S/∂V)_T=(∂p/∂T)_V in a smooth single phase. Stability imposes response constraints, including positive heat capacities for ordinary stable canonical states. Phase boundaries and nonadditive gravitating systems need care: ensemble-specific stability conclusions cannot be transferred blindly. A cyclic engine between reservoirs satisfies η≤1−T_c/T_h when temperatures are absolute and all heat/work exchanges are included.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="worked" title="A worked argument" layer="worked" fullOnly>

An engine absorbs 100 J and rejects 60 J in one cycle. Its internal-energy change is zero, so work output is 40 J and efficiency is .4. Reservoirs at 500 K and 300 K have Carnot bound .4; attaining that bound requires the reversible idealization, not merely matching two measured numbers.

</LessonSection>
