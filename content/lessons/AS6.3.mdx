<LessonSection id="concept" title="Definitions and hypotheses" layer="concept">

Ideal MHD combines fluid conservation with a magnetic field, using E+v×B=0 and neglecting specified kinetic and nonideal effects.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="theory" title="Derivation, proof and limitations" layer="theory" fullOnly>

Combining ideal Ohm’s law with Faraday yields $\partial_t\mathbf B=\nabla\times(\mathbf v\times\mathbf B)$ with ∇·B=0. Smooth ideal flow supports a flux-freezing theorem for material surfaces. Resistivity, sharp structures and departures from the fluid model can break the conditions needed for that conclusion.

Momentum includes J×B, which can be written as magnetic pressure and tension contributions under the divergence constraint. Magnetic pressure B²/(2μ₀) alone does not represent every force direction; bent field lines produce tension. An energy equation and EOS remain necessary closures.

A uniform background supports Alfvén disturbances with $v_A=B/\sqrt{\mu_0\rho}$ along the appropriate field geometry. Compressible MHD also has fast and slow magnetosonic branches. One scalar magnetic speed cannot describe every propagation direction and mode.

With magnetic diffusivity η_m, a resistive induction term appears and the magnetic Reynolds number UL/η_m compares advection and diffusion at chosen scales. Reconnection can concentrate in thin regions even when a large-scale number is high. Collisionless plasma, Hall terms, anisotropic pressure and kinetic resonances can require a model beyond one-fluid ideal MHD.


### Derive, test and transfer

A bent magnetic field supplies a restoring tension per volume of order $B^2/(\mu_0L)$. Dividing by density yields an acceleration scale, giving the Alfvén wave speed $v_A=B/\sqrt{\mu_0\rho}$. Increasing field strengthens the restoring force, while increased mass density adds inertia.

Ideal MHD treats the plasma as a conducting fluid with a common bulk description and approximately frozen magnetic flux. Resistive reconnection, ambipolar effects, kinetic scales and relativistic magnetization require extensions. Sound, fast and slow magnetosonic modes have different directional behavior; the Alfvén speed alone is not the speed of every disturbance. </LessonSection>
