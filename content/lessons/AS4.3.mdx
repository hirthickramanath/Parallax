Post-main-sequence evolution separates the energy/pressure response of the core and envelope, with mass and composition deciding the path.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="concept" title="Definitions and hypotheses" layer="concept">

A giant star can contain a contracting or degenerate core, shell burning and an extended envelope; a single uniform-temperature sphere is not an adequate model.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="theory" title="Derivation, proof and limitations" layer="theory" fullOnly>

When core hydrogen is depleted, changes in support and energy generation can drive core contraction while hydrogen burns in a surrounding shell. The envelope can expand and cool at the surface as luminosity and structure readjust. Core and envelope energies must be treated together; surface cooling does not imply every interior region cools.

Low-mass stars can ignite helium in a degenerate core through a helium flash, while higher-mass stars may ignite more smoothly. The outcome depends on mass, composition and prior evolution. Degeneracy weakens simple thermal expansion feedback, but the flash does not normally mean the entire star is disrupted.

On the asymptotic giant branch, hydrogen/helium shell burning surrounds a compact core, and thermal pulses plus mixing can alter surface abundances. Mass loss involves atmosphere dynamics, pulsation, radiation and dust in relevant regimes. A one-parameter steady wind law is an empirical/model closure rather than a complete first-principles explanation.

For an ideal steady spherical wind, $\dot M=4\pi r^2\rho v$ is a continuity relation, not an independent acceleration mechanism. Envelope removal can expose a hot core and produce an ionized nebula as conditions allow. Advanced burning, dredge-up and final remnants require mass/composition-dependent tracks, not a universal sequence identical for every star.


### Derive, test and transfer

Rearrange the surface luminosity relation to $T/T_0=(L/L_0)^{1/4}(R/R_0)^{-1/2}$. At fixed luminosity, an expanding envelope cools at the effective surface even if a contracting core heats. Surface and core temperatures must not be conflated when explaining giant evolution.

After core hydrogen exhaustion, shell burning, core contraction and envelope response occur on different physical scales. A parametrized surface relation does not solve their energy balance or predict a mass-loss rate. Winds depend on radiation, dust, pulsation and other circumstances. The investigation holds a chosen luminosity ratio fixed while moving radius; an evolutionary track instead determines both from a coupled stellar model. State this controlled distinction before attributing a plotted path to a real star.

</LessonSection>
