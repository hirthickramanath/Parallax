</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="theory" title="Derivation, proof and limitations" layer="theory" fullOnly>

In one rotating-frame convention, $H=\hbar(\Delta\sigma_z+\Omega\sigma_x)/2$. On resonance and without damping, a ground-state population oscillates to excitation as sin²(Ωt/2). Peak/RMS field and angular/cyclic frequency conventions affect Ω definitions.

The rotating-wave approximation discards rapidly oscillating terms only when coupling/detuning/time scales justify it relative to the carrier. Strong driving, large detuning or multilevel structure can require corrections. Dipole matrix elements and polarization determine which levels actually couple.

Spontaneous emission, dephasing and population feeding add open-system dynamics. A steady absorption line and a coherent pulse experiment need not have the same width/power dependence. Saturation and optical pumping redistribute populations.

Far-detuned coupling can produce light shifts and effective interactions in an admitted expansion, but photon scattering and multilevel effects can remain. Calling a drive off resonance does not mean no interaction. Compare experiment with the corresponding master equation and detector observable, not only a drawn two-state sinusoid.


### Derivation and transfer

For a coherent driven two-level atom with detuning Δ and resonant Rabi frequency Ω, the generalized frequency is sqrt(Ω²+Δ²). Excitation probability has amplitude Ω²/(Ω²+Δ²), so detuning suppresses complete transfer.

The display fixes Ω=1 and varies time and detuning under the rotating-wave approximation. It omits spontaneous emission, laser noise and motion. A fitted line width cannot be inferred from this closed-system oscillation alone.

</LessonSection>
