First-order transition amplitudes integrate the interaction-picture perturbation; Fermi’s golden rule approximates suitable transitions into a dense spectrum.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="theory" title="Derivation, proof and limitations" layer="theory" fullOnly>

For initial n, $c_m^{(1)}(t)=-(i/\hbar)\int_0^t e^{i\omega_{mn}t'}V_{mn}(t')dt'$ with ω_mn=(E_m−E_n)/ℏ in the stated convention. Probability is the squared amplitude only to the consistent perturbative order; normalization errors grow if transition probabilities cease to be small.

For harmonic driving, finite-time integrals give resonance envelopes with a width tied to observation duration. An isolated resonant two-level system can exhibit coherent Rabi oscillations and saturation, not an irreversible constant rate forever.

A continuum long-enough-time and weak-coupling approximation gives $\Gamma=(2\pi/\hbar)|V|^2\rho(E)$ with a density of final states per energy and the relevant matching condition. Very short times, finite recurrences, broad variation of matrix elements and depletion can invalidate it.

Selection rules constrain matrix elements of the actual interaction operator. Experimental rates include populations, linewidths and environment; a formally allowed transition can be weak. Keep peak/RMS driving conventions and energy/frequency density conversions explicit.


### Derivation and transfer

For an isolated resonantly driven two-level system in the rotating-wave approximation, transition probability is sin²(Ωt/2). It oscillates and remains bounded. Fermi's golden rule instead describes an appropriate weak-coupling continuum/long-time regime, not indefinite linear growth in an isolated two-state system.

The display is coherent resonant Rabi dynamics. Detuning, damping, pulse shape and rotating-wave validity require additional parameters. A rate approximation must identify the timescale over which it is used.

</LessonSection>
