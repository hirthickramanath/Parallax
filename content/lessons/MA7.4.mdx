<LessonSection id="story" title="Why the question matters" layer="story">

Hermite and Laguerre families solve specific self-adjoint problems. Confusing conventions or integration weights gives wrong normalizations even when a polynomial looks familiar.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="intuition" title="Build a mental model" layer="intuition">

A polynomial is only part of a mode. A decaying envelope and the geometry supply its weight, and square-integrability selects the admissible family.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="concept" title="Definitions and hypotheses" layer="concept">

Use physicists’ Hermite polynomials Hₙ and generalized Laguerre polynomials Lₙ^(α), n≥0. Their orthogonality measures are e⁻ˣ²dx on the line and xᵅe⁻ˣdx on the positive half-line, respectively, with α>−1 for the latter.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="theory" title="Derivation, proof and limitations" layer="theory" fullOnly>

Physicists’ Hermite polynomials satisfy $H_n''-2xH_n'+2nH_n=0$ and Rodrigues’ formula $H_n=(-1)^ne^{x^2}d^n(e^{-x^2})/dx^n$. Thus H₀=1, H₁=2x and H₂=4x²−2. The probabilists’ convention Heₙ has different scaling: Hₙ(x)=2ⁿᐟ²Heₙ(√2x). Naming a family without the convention can therefore change both coefficients and norms.

Repeated integration by parts, with Gaussian boundary terms vanishing, gives $\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-x^2}H_nH_mdx=\sqrt\pi\,2^nn!\,\delta_{nm}$. The normalized oscillator functions are Hₙ(x)e⁻ˣ²ᐟ² divided by π¹ᐟ⁴√(2ⁿn!). For the dimensionless Hamiltonian (−d²/dx²+x²)/2, substitution produces eigenvalues n+1/2. A physical oscillator adds its length and energy scales; x is not automatically a displacement measured in metres.

Generalized Laguerre polynomials satisfy $xy''+(\alpha+1-x)y'+ny=0$ and $L_n^{(\alpha)}=x^{-\alpha}e^x(n!)^{-1}d^n[e^{-x}x^{n+\alpha}]/dx^n$. In particular L₀^(α)=1 and L₁^(α)=α+1−x. For α>−1 their norm is Γ(n+α+1)/n! in the weight xᵅe⁻ˣ. The parameter α is fixed when comparing this orthogonal family.

For the three-dimensional Coulomb radial problem, separating angular modes and imposing regularity/normalizability leads to an exponential envelope times an associated Laguerre polynomial with scaled radial variable. The physical radial norm involves r²dr for R(r), or dr for the reduced function u=rR. These two radial functions are not interchangeable. Detailed energy quantization also requires the physical Hamiltonian and boundary assumptions, not only recognizing a polynomial.

Termination of an otherwise growing series and decay at infinity explain discrete solutions in the appropriate eigenproblem. Generic solutions of the underlying differential equation need not be normalizable polynomials times the chosen envelope. Near singular endpoints, the admissible domain of the operator must be stated. Orthogonality is a weighted integral statement, not a claim that two plotted functions never cross or never share the same value.


### Derivation and transfer

Using the physicists' convention, H0=1, H1=2x and H2=4x²−2. Hermite functions multiply these polynomials by a Gaussian, so the polynomial itself is not a normalizable oscillator wavefunction. Laguerre families use a different domain and weight.

An oscillator mode requires its normalization and physical length scale. The display isolates H2 exp(−x²/2) with an adjustable amplitude; varying that amplitude does not preserve probability normalization. State the polynomial convention before applying recurrence or orthogonality formulas.
