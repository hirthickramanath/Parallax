<LessonSection id="concept" title="Definitions and hypotheses" layer="concept">

A multimessenger association is a joint inference about astrophysical origin conditioned on detector responses, backgrounds, time windows and source models.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="theory" title="Derivation, proof and limitations" layer="theory" fullOnly>

Neutrinos interact weakly and can escape dense environments, but interaction probability and detector effective area depend on energy and flavour. Oscillation changes flavour composition during propagation; a detector does not directly count every emitted neutrino. Expected events require an integral such as $N=\int\Phi(E)A_{eff}(E)\,dE\,dt$ with a consistent flux convention.

A temporal coincidence must be compared with the background rate and chosen search window. A wider window includes more possible delayed-source models but also more accidental coincidences. Angular localization regions and catalogue completeness alter association probabilities.

A common event may produce different emission and propagation delays for photons, neutrinos and gravitational waves. Interpreting a delay as a fundamental speed difference requires a source-time model, distance and uncertainty treatment. Absence of one messenger can reflect sensitivity or beaming, not proof that it was never produced.

Joint likelihoods require conditional dependencies and shared nuisance parameters to be handled rather than multiplying correlated summaries as if independent. Trials, selection, nondetections and calibration matter. Synthetic examples demonstrate a method; actual event claims require validated data and community-quality review.


### Derive, test and transfer

For a Poisson event count with expected signal s and background b, $P(N=n)=e^{-(s+b)}(s+b)^n/n!$. The probability of at least one event is $1-e^{-(s+b)}$. This probability grows with exposure, but it includes background events and is not the probability that a detected event is astrophysical.

A source association uses direction, energy, timing, detector response and the background process. Searching many coincidence windows changes the trials accounting. Neutrino energy and flavor response depend on interaction cross sections and detector acceptance; counts alone do not directly measure a total emitted energy without a source model and distance. The display isolates counting behavior and explicitly treats its input rates as synthetic.

</LessonSection>
