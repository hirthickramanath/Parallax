<LessonSection id="story" title="Why the question matters" layer="story">

Angular separation organizes gravitational multipoles, sky maps and quantum angular modes. Regularity, periodicity and a normalization convention fix the basis.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="intuition" title="Build a mental model" layer="intuition">

An angular pattern must join consistently after one full turn and stay regular at the poles. These requirements eliminate many formal solutions of the separated equations.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="concept" title="Definitions and hypotheses" layer="concept">

Legendre polynomials Pℓ solve the regular axisymmetric angular equation on [−1,1], with integer degree ℓ≥0 and Pℓ(1)=1. Spherical harmonics Yℓᵐ are normalized angular eigenfunctions of the sphere Laplacian.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="theory" title="Derivation, proof and limitations" layer="theory" fullOnly>

Separating Laplace’s equation in spherical coordinates gives, for an axisymmetric angular factor with x=cosθ, $\frac d{dx}[(1-x^2)P']+\ell(\ell+1)P=0$. Regular endpoint solutions on the full sphere select nonnegative integer ℓ. Rodrigues’ formula is $P_\ell(x)=(2^\ell\ell!)^{-1}\frac{d^\ell}{dx^\ell}(x^2-1)^\ell$. It gives P₀=1, P₁=x and P₂=(3x²−1)/2.

The self-adjoint form yields orthogonality: multiply the equations for distinct degrees by the opposite solution, subtract and integrate. The endpoint term vanishes for the regular polynomial solutions, leaving $(\ell(\ell+1)-j(j+1))\int_{-1}^{1}P_\ell P_jdx=0$. Their squared norm is 2/(2ℓ+1). This argument needs endpoint conditions; arbitrary singular solutions do not inherit it automatically.

For integer m≥0 use the Condon–Shortley convention $P_\ell^m(x)=(-1)^m(1-x^2)^{m/2}d^mP_\ell/dx^m$, with m≤ℓ. The standard complex harmonic is $Y_\ell^m=\sqrt{\frac{2\ell+1}{4\pi}\frac{(\ell-m)!}{(\ell+m)!}}P_\ell^m(\cos\theta)e^{im\phi}$. Negative orders satisfy Yℓ⁻ᵐ=(−1)ᵐconj(Yℓᵐ) in this convention. Other phase conventions exist, so compare definitions before comparing signs.

On the unit sphere, $\Delta_{S^2}Y_\ell^m=-\ell(\ell+1)Y_\ell^m$. The surface measure is dΩ=sinθ dθ dφ, and the harmonics are orthonormal in that measure. There are 2ℓ+1 independent orders for each degree. For a real field the complex coefficients obey the corresponding conjugacy relation; a real-harmonic basis is an equivalent alternative with its own definitions.

Outside a bounded source in a source-free region, separated multipoles combine angular harmonics with radial factors rℓ and r⁻ˡ⁻¹. Boundary conditions and decay determine which radial branches are allowed. A truncated angular expansion is not a claim that unresolved small-scale structure is absent. Sky masks, uneven noise and a telescope beam can mix observed modes, so mathematical orthogonality on the full sphere does not guarantee uncorrelated measured coefficients.


### Derivation and transfer

The first Legendre polynomials are P0=1, P1=x and P2=(3x²−1)/2. Orthogonality on [−1,1] with unit weight makes ∫P2 dx=0. Spherical harmonics add angular phase, associated-polynomial order and normalization conventions.

A plotted polynomial is not a complete spherical harmonic. The display isolates a degree-two axisymmetric angular shape with a chosen amplitude; it does not imply arbitrary functions are represented by one mode or that normalization is unchanged when the amplitude moves.
