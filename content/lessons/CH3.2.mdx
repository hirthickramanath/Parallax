<LessonSection id="theory" title="Derivation, proof and limitations" layer="theory" fullOnly>

For a gas reaction A+B with a rate coefficient k, loss includes $k n_A n_B$ for distinct reactants. Identical pair counting and branching must be handled consistently. A stiff network can have fast transients and slow abundance evolution, requiring stable integrators and conservation checks.

Adsorption depends on collision flux, grain area and sticking; surface diffusion and binding determine encounters/desorption. Thermal, photon and cosmic-ray processes can release species. One dust temperature or grain size need not describe a full population.

When expected surface occupation per grain is below one, a deterministic product of average populations can fail to represent correlated rare events. Master-equation or stochastic methods may be needed. A rate coefficient inferred in one surface regime is not universally transferable.

Track elemental/charge budgets and uncertainty in poorly known reactions. Negative computed densities or mass drift can signal a solver/problem-definition error. A chemically plausible final abundance is not sufficient proof of a correct network history.


### Derive, test and transfer

A spherical grain with surface site density ns has approximately 4πa²ns adsorption sites. Doubling its radius quadruples site count, while its mass scales with radius cubed at fixed composition/density. Surface availability per unit dust mass therefore changes with the size distribution.

Gas-phase binary reaction rates scale as k nA nB in a simple dilute model, while grain-surface rates depend on adsorption, diffusion, occupancy and desorption. A large site count alone does not establish rapid formation: reactants must meet and survive. Sparse stochastic populations can defeat deterministic rate equations. The investigation is a geometric site budget rather than an executed chemical network.

</LessonSection>
