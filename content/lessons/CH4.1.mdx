<LessonSection id="story" title="Why the question matters" layer="story">

Lewis structures organize electron accounting but do not uniquely determine molecular wavefunctions or measurable bond orders.

<LessonSection id="concept" title="Definitions and hypotheses" layer="concept">

Formal charge assigns bonding electrons equally between bonded atoms; resonance contributors retain the same nuclear connectivity while redistributing an approximate electron-pair description.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="theory" title="Derivation, proof and limitations" layer="theory" fullOnly>

Count total valence electrons, including charge, before choosing connectivity and completing electron pairs. For an atom, $FC=V-N_{nonbonding}-B/2$, where B counts bonding electrons. Formal charges sum to the total molecular/ionic charge. They are bookkeeping assignments, not directly measured partial charges or oxidation states.

The octet rule describes many second-row main-group patterns, but electron-deficient, odd-electron and hypervalent species require qualifications. A drawing with fewer formal charges is not automatically a correct complete bonding theory. Electronegativity, nuclear geometry, orbital availability and delocalization affect which contributors are chemically useful.

Resonance contributors share the nuclear framework and differ in formal electron placement. The actual state is not a classical time sequence jumping between drawings. Equivalent contributors can predict equivalent bonds, but a simple average of drawn integer bond orders is only a qualitative model. Structural isomers instead change connectivity and are distinct chemical species.

Lewis counting should be checked against geometry, spectroscopic evidence and a more detailed electronic model when needed. Charge separation, solvent and counterions can alter stability without changing the bookkeeping rule. Do not equate a formal neutral atom inside a Lewis structure with zero local polarization.


### Derive, test and transfer

Formal charge assigns half of bonding electrons and all nonbonding electrons to an atom. Thus FC=valence electrons−nonbonding electrons−bond-order sum in an ordinary two-electron-bond Lewis picture. For nitrogen with three single bonds and one lone pair, 5−2−3=0; with four single bonds and no lone pair it is +1.

Formal charge is not an independently measured atomic charge. Resonance contributors share connectivity and describe one delocalized electronic state, not a molecule switching among separate classical structures. Octet, total-electron and total-charge constraints must all be checked; arithmetic alone does not establish that an arbitrary arrangement is chemically stable.

</LessonSection>

In one nitrate contributor, central N has five valence electrons, no nonbonding electrons and eight bonding electrons, giving FC=5−0−4=+1. A singly bonded O with six nonbonding and two bonding electrons has FC=6−6−1=−1. Three equivalent contributors preserve the same N–O connectivity.
