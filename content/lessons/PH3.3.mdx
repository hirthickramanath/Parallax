<LessonSection id="concept" title="Definitions and hypotheses" layer="concept">

The material derivative D/Dt=∂ₜ+v·∇ follows a fluid parcel. Mass conservation and momentum balance require a stated stress and equation of state.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="theory" title="Derivation, proof and limitations" layer="theory" fullOnly>

Mass balance is $\partial_t\rho+\nabla\cdot(\rho\mathbf v)=0$. Constant material density gives ∇·v=0 in the corresponding incompressible model. This does not require pressure to be constant; pressure adjusts to maintain the velocity constraint with boundaries and forcing.

For an incompressible Newtonian fluid of constant viscosity, $\rho D\mathbf v/Dt=-\nabla p+\eta\nabla^2\mathbf v+\rho\mathbf b$. Euler flow omits viscosity only when the intended regime justifies it. A compressible viscous model also needs bulk/shear stresses, energy evolution and a constitutive closure.

Bernoulli’s quantity p/ρ+v²/2+gz is constant along a streamline for steady inviscid barotropic flow with conservative forcing under the appropriate assumptions. It is not universally constant across all streamlines of a rotational flow or across a dissipative shock. No-slip walls create viscous boundary layers even when bulk Reynolds number is large.

Nondimensionalization yields Reynolds number Re=ρUL/η. It compares inertial and viscous terms at the chosen scales, not a universal transition temperature or a proof of turbulence. Geometry, disturbances and boundary conditions affect transition. A numerical solution must also respect mass balance and boundary fluxes; a plausible arrow plot is not validation.


### Derive, test and transfer

For steady incompressible fully developed Newtonian flow in a straight circular pipe, the axial Navier–Stokes equation reduces to η(1/r)d(r dv/dr)/dr=−G for a positive pressure-drop gradient G. Finite center velocity and no slip at radius R give v=G(R²−r²)/(4η).

Integrating 2πr v dr gives Q=πGR⁴/(8η), so modest radius changes strongly affect flux. This exact laminar solution does not establish stability at arbitrary Reynolds number. Entrance effects, turbulence, non-Newtonian rheology and wall slip can invalidate it. The investigation shows the parabolic profile for its stated ideal regime while keeping the governing pressure/viscosity balance explicit.

</LessonSection>
