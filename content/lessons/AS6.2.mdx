<LessonSection id="concept" title="Definitions and hypotheses" layer="concept">

A simple Strömgren model is a uniform pure-hydrogen static region with an ionizing photon rate Q and a stated case-B recombination coefficient α_B.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="theory" title="Derivation, proof and limitations" layer="theory" fullOnly>

Balancing supplied photons with volume recombinations gives $Q=4\pi R_S^3\alpha_Bn_en_p/3$ in the ionization-bounded ideal sphere. For fully ionized pure hydrogen n_e≈n_p≈n. Thus R_S∝Q¹ᐟ³n⁻²ᐟ³. Clumping changes the recombination integral because it depends on density products rather than only an average density.

Case B treats recombinations directly to the ground state through an on-the-spot ionizing-photon approximation. It is not valid for every optically thin or photon-leaking geometry. Dust can absorb ionizing radiation, and density-bounded regions can let photons escape rather than satisfying the full ideal volume balance.

The recombination time scale is approximately 1/(α_B n_e) when the coefficient and density are suitable. Heated gas can expand and drive a front, so a static Strömgren radius is a reference equilibrium scale, not the full time-dependent solution.

Nebular line ratios depend on electron temperature, density, abundances, ionizing spectrum and collisional/radiative processes. Critical densities and optical depths matter. A bright recombination line can constrain ionization under assumptions but is not a direct uncorrected elemental mass measurement.


### Derive, test and transfer

For uniform fully ionized hydrogen in equilibrium, recombinations inside a sphere consume $\alpha_B n_en_p(4\pi R^3/3)$ photons per time. Equating this to the absorbed ionizing rate Q gives $R_S=[3Q/(4\pi\alpha_Bn^2)]^{1/3}$. Density enters quadratically because recombination involves two particle densities.

Case B excludes direct recombinations to the ground state from the net distant-photon budget under the on-the-spot approximation. Dust absorption, clumping, escaping ionizing photons and a changing source alter the effective budget. Gas expansion can move the region away from the initial static sphere. A recombination line can trace the absorbed ionizing power only with these transfer and geometry assumptions; it is not an unconditional measurement of every emitted UV photon.

</LessonSection>
