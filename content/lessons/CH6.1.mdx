<LessonSection id="story" title="Why the question matters" layer="story">

Organic acid-base reasoning connects molecular structure with equilibrium. In astrochemistry, ionization and proton transfer can determine reaction pathways even when terrestrial solution pKa tables are inapplicable.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="intuition" title="Build a mental model" layer="intuition">

Compare the acid and its conjugate base in the same environment. A structural change matters through the free-energy difference, not simply because one drawing looks more comfortable.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="concept" title="Definitions and hypotheses" layer="concept">

A Brønsted acid donates a proton and a base accepts one. Resonance contributors encode one delocalized electronic state while preserving atomic positions and overall charge.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="theory" title="Derivation, proof and limitations" layer="theory" fullOnly>

For an acid HA in a specified medium, $K_a=a_Ha_A/a_{HA}$ and pKa=−log10Ka, using the adopted proton and standard-state conventions. Lower pKa means stronger acidity under those same conditions. Comparing numerical pKa values from different solvents, temperatures or phases can be invalid.

For HA+B⁻⇌A⁻+HB in one consistent solution convention, $K\approx10^{pK_a(HB)-pK_a(HA)}$ when the compared standard-state quantities match. Products favour the weaker acid and base pair. This is an equilibrium tendency, not a guarantee of a fast reaction: barriers, diffusion and competing pathways can govern the observed rate.

Resonance contributors differ in electron allocation, not nuclear connectivity. A valid contributor respects electron count, charge and the allowed valence description. The actual state is not a molecule jumping between drawings. Delocalization can stabilize a conjugate base, but acidity depends on both acid and base free energies, including solvation and geometry.

Inductive effects operate through the electronic response of bonds, while conjugation requires orbital overlap. Their magnitude and direction depend on distance, substituent and context. Formal charge is bookkeeping; it is not identical to a measured partial charge. A carbon bearing formal negative charge can have different stability and reactivity in different hybridization and solvent environments.

In a dilute gas, proton affinity and gas-phase reaction energetics provide different quantities from solution pKa. Low-density interstellar reactions may be controlled by ion–molecule capture, radiation and sparse collisions rather than a bulk-solution equilibrium. Keep electronic-structure reasoning, thermodynamic quantities and environmental kinetics as distinct parts of the argument.


### Derive, test and transfer

For HA⇌H⁺+A⁻, equilibrium gives pH=pKa+log10(aA/aHA) under the consistent activity convention. Replacing activities by concentration ratios is an ideal/dilute approximation. Equal conjugate acid/base activity gives pH=pKa, but that alone does not specify buffering capacity.

Resonance, induction, solvation and the stability of conjugate species affect organic acid-base trends. Comparing pKa values across solvents without conventions can be misleading. The display uses a simple ratio relation, not a complete high-concentration titration or water-autoprotolysis solver.

</LessonSection>
