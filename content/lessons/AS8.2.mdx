<LessonSection id="story" title="Why the question matters" layer="story">

Collisionless dynamics connects phase-space populations to gravity, but projection and orbital anisotropy create inference degeneracies.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="intuition" title="Build a mental model" layer="intuition">

Start with the concrete worked example below. Track which quantities belong to the system, which are imposed by its surroundings, and which conclusions require a limiting approximation.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="concept" title="Definitions and hypotheses" layer="concept">

The collisionless Boltzmann equation evolves a distribution function under a smooth mean gravitational potential; Jeans equations are its velocity moments with closure/assumption requirements.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="theory" title="Derivation, proof and limitations" layer="theory" fullOnly>

For a steady spherical nonstreaming tracer, $d(\nu\sigma_r^2)/dr+2\beta\nu\sigma_r^2/r=-\nu GM(r)/r^2$, with β=1−(σ_θ²+σ_φ²)/(2σ_r²). Tracer density ν need not equal total gravitating mass density. Projected data do not directly reveal every three-dimensional moment.

Different combinations of M(r) and anisotropy can produce similar projected dispersions, the mass–anisotropy degeneracy. Proper motions, multiple tracers and higher-order information can help but require their own data/model assumptions. A Jeans moment solution also must correspond to a physically nonnegative distribution function.

The virial theorem provides an integrated relation with surface and stationarity qualifications. A rough M∼Rσ²/G includes a geometry/structure coefficient and is not exact for every galaxy. Rotation and non-equilibrium tidal structure can invalidate naive pressure-support estimates.

Two-body relaxation is often slow in galaxies compared with their age, supporting a collisionless approximation, whereas dense star clusters can relax much faster. Close encounters, resonances and collective effects require separate time-scale checks. A numerical N-body model must distinguish physical relaxation from finite-particle artifacts.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="interactive" title="Investigate the claim" layer="interactive">

<LessonSection id="worked" title="A worked argument" layer="worked" fullOnly>

For a stated toy estimator M=Rσ²/G with coefficient one, R=3,σ=2,G=1 give M=12. Doubling σ at fixed R multiplies that estimate by four; it does not remove uncertainty in anisotropy or structure.

</LessonSection>
