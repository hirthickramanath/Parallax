<LessonSection id="concept" title="Definitions and hypotheses" layer="concept">

Hartree–Fock uses a determinant variational family; density-functional theory expresses ground-state energy through density under the relevant existence/representability assumptions.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="theory" title="Derivation, proof and limitations" layer="theory" fullOnly>

For HF, antisymmetry generates exchange while a single determinant omits correlation beyond its family. In an admissible same-Hamiltonian variational setting, $E_{trial}\ge E_0$. This bound does not transfer automatically to a different effective Hamiltonian or every approximate functional.

Kohn–Sham DFT uses an auxiliary noninteracting construction with the target density when representability and formal assumptions permit. The practical exchange-correlation functional is approximated. Its orbitals are calculation objects and their individual energies are not generally all exact excitation energies.

Self-consistency residual, basis convergence, numerical-grid convergence and functional/model adequacy are separate checks. Charge, spin state and geometry can select different solutions. A low residual in the wrong state is not a correct molecular prediction.

Compare methods with benchmark quantities and uncertainties appropriate to the chemical regime. Strong correlation, dispersion, charge transfer and open-shell states can challenge approximations differently. Selecting a functional after seeing one target and then calling that target independent validation is circular.


### Derive, test and transfer

For a normalized Gaussian exp(−a x²) in the harmonic oscillator with ħ=m=ω=1, the energy expectation is a/2+1/(8a). Its minimum at a=1/2 gives E=1/2. Varying a checks both normalization-dependent kinetic and potential contributions rather than assuming an arbitrary trial shape is exact.

The variational principle bounds ground-state energy for the specified Hamiltonian and admissible normalized states. Hartree–Fock restricts the state class to a determinant and omits correlation beyond that ansatz; approximate density functionals have their own errors. Kohn–Sham auxiliary orbital energy gaps are not automatically exact physical excitation energies. An internally consistent ground-state optimization is not a universal spectroscopy solver.

</LessonSection>
