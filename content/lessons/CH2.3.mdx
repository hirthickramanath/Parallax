<LessonSection id="puzzle" title="Commit to a prediction" layer="puzzle">

Why can two electronic-structure calculations agree numerically yet share the same model error? State a prediction and a reason before reading the argument.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="story" title="Why the question matters" layer="story">

HF and DFT have different formal variables and approximations; basis and iteration convergence are not independent scientific validation.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="intuition" title="Build a mental model" layer="intuition">

Start with the concrete worked example below. Track which quantities belong to the system, which are imposed by its surroundings, and which conclusions require a limiting approximation.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="concept" title="Definitions and hypotheses" layer="concept">

Hartree–Fock uses a determinant variational family; density-functional theory expresses ground-state energy through density under the relevant existence/representability assumptions.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="theory" title="Derivation, proof and limitations" layer="theory" fullOnly>

For HF, antisymmetry generates exchange while a single determinant omits correlation beyond its family. In an admissible same-Hamiltonian variational setting, $E_{trial}\ge E_0$. This bound does not transfer automatically to a different effective Hamiltonian or every approximate functional.

Kohn–Sham DFT uses an auxiliary noninteracting construction with the target density when representability and formal assumptions permit. The practical exchange-correlation functional is approximated. Its orbitals are calculation objects and their individual energies are not generally all exact excitation energies.

Self-consistency residual, basis convergence, numerical-grid convergence and functional/model adequacy are separate checks. Charge, spin state and geometry can select different solutions. A low residual in the wrong state is not a correct molecular prediction.

Compare methods with benchmark quantities and uncertainties appropriate to the chemical regime. Strong correlation, dispersion, charge transfer and open-shell states can challenge approximations differently. Selecting a functional after seeing one target and then calling that target independent validation is circular.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="interactive" title="Investigate the claim" layer="interactive">

Prepare a computational specification with geometry, charge, spin, method and basis. Compare independent convergence axes and retain a benchmark not used to choose the method.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="worked" title="A worked argument" layer="worked" fullOnly>

For one model with exact energy −12 and trial energy −10, the trial excess is 2. If a larger trial space gives −11, the excess falls to 1; that example illustrates a variational comparison, not a blanket guarantee for every DFT result.

</LessonSection>
