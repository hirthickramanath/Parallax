<LessonSection id="concept" title="Definitions and hypotheses" layer="concept">

Kerr geometry describes an ideal stationary rotating vacuum black hole; horizon, ergoregion and circular-orbit structures depend on mass and angular momentum.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="theory" title="Derivation, proof and limitations" layer="theory" fullOnly>

In geometric units G=c=1, define a=J/M and horizons $r_\pm=M\pm\sqrt{M^2-a^2}$ for |a|≤M in the ideal Kerr family. The ergoregion boundary is not generally the same as the horizon; it concerns the allowed stationary Killing motion.

The inner stable circular orbit depends on spin and orbital orientation and is not a material surface. Accretion emission also depends on stresses and radiative transfer, so locating a spectral inner radius is a model-dependent inference rather than direct horizon imaging.

Black-hole thermodynamics assigns entropy proportional to horizon area and a temperature tied to surface gravity under the relevant stationary/semi-classical assumptions. For Schwarzschild, $T_H=\hbar c^3/(8\pi Gk_BM)$. Larger mass means lower temperature; this is not ordinary thermal surface emission from solid matter.

Hawking radiation is a semiclassical prediction with quantum fields on a curved background, not a complete quantum-gravity resolution of information or singularities. Astrophysical observations constrain compact geometry through emission, motion and waves with model systematics. Do not label every philosophical issue experimentally settled.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="worked" title="A worked argument" layer="worked" fullOnly>

For geometric M=5,a=3, Kerr r_+=5+√16=9. In the Schwarzschild temperature scaling, doubling M halves T_H. Neither arithmetic fixes an actual accretion spectrum.
