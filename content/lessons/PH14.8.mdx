<LessonSection id="concept" title="Definitions and hypotheses" layer="concept">

Band topology assigns invariant data to occupied states under specified gaps, symmetries and dimensionality; an invariant is not defined independently of those conditions.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="theory" title="Derivation, proof and limitations" layer="theory" fullOnly>

For an ideal integer quantum Hall state, $\sigma_{xy}=C e^2/h$ with integer C in the applicable two-dimensional gapped setting. Longitudinal dissipation and finite-temperature/contact effects require experimental qualifications. A plateau is not obtained by inserting any arbitrary integer into every conductor.

Berry curvature integrates to a Chern invariant on an appropriate closed Brillouin manifold for a well-defined occupied bundle. Gauge-dependent eigenvectors can be patched while the integral is invariant. A gap closure or a change in protecting symmetry can alter classification.

Bulk-boundary correspondence predicts boundary phenomena under its hypotheses and boundary setting. Not every accidental edge band is topologically protected. Disorder and interactions can preserve some phases or require a classification beyond independent bands.

Time-reversal-protected topological insulators use different invariants from a Chern Hall state. Fractional Hall phases involve interactions/topological order and cannot be fully explained by one noninteracting integer-band picture. Compare transport, boundaries and gap/symmetry evidence together.


### Derivation and transfer

An ideal integer quantum Hall plateau has Hall conductance νe²/h for integer filling ν under the appropriate gapped transport conditions. Topological quantization concerns a robust invariant, whereas dissipative transport and sample contacts still require care.

The display steps a specified integer plateau index. It does not derive a Chern number from a Hamiltonian or simulate disorder, edge modes or a fractional Hall state. The longitudinal response is a separate observable.

</LessonSection>
