<LessonSection id="concept" title="Definitions and hypotheses" layer="concept">

The nonrelativistic hydrogenic model uses reduced electron–nucleus mass μ and central potential −Ze²/(4πε₀r), neglecting spin-dependent, relativistic and radiative corrections.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="theory" title="Derivation, proof and limitations" layer="theory" fullOnly>

Separate ψ=R(r)Yℓᵐ(θ,φ) and define u=rR. The radial equation is $-\hbar^2u''/(2\mu)+[\hbar^2\ell(\ell+1)/(2\mu r^2)-Ze^2/(4\pi\epsilon_0r)]u=Eu$. Its domain and normalization at zero/infinity are essential; the centrifugal term is an effective radial contribution rather than a new force inserted by hand.

For bound states, regularity and decay select terminating associated-Laguerre solutions with $E_n=-\mu Z^2e^4/[2(4\pi\epsilon_0)^2\hbar^2n^2]$. Labels obey n≥1, 0≤ℓ≤n−1 and −ℓ≤m≤ℓ. The spin-free spatial degeneracy is ∑(2ℓ+1)=n². A separate spin factor is not already included in that count.

The normalized wavefunction supplies a three-dimensional density. A radial shell probability uses r²|R|²dr after normalized angular integration; |R|² alone is not a radial probability density per unit r. An orbital is not a classical path with a hidden definite radius. Expectation, most-probable radius and turning radius need not coincide.

Emission or absorption transfers an energy difference hν, with selection rules depending on the coupling operator. Leading electric-dipole orbital rules include Δℓ=±1 and appropriate m changes, but parity, spin and state mixing matter in fuller models. Fine structure, nuclear motion, hyperfine interactions and QED corrections change the simple n-only spectrum; multielectron screening cannot be captured by merely reusing every hydrogen degeneracy.


### Derivation and transfer

For a nonrelativistic hydrogenic ion, En=−Ry Z²/n² with a reduced-mass-dependent Rydberg scale. Emission photon energy is the positive difference between the initial and final bound energies. Ionization starts at the continuum threshold zero.

The display uses Ry=1 and integer n. Fine structure, hyperfine coupling, external fields and finite nuclear size are omitted. A negative energy is relative to a chosen continuum zero, not a negative measured photon energy.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="worked" title="A worked argument" layer="worked" fullOnly>

Using E_n=−E₀/n², the photon energy for n=2 to n=1 is 3E₀/4. For n=3, the spin-free spatial state count is 1+3+5=9. The count includes distinct angular states, not nine different leading energies.
