<LessonSection id="story" title="Why the question matters" layer="story">

Particle production is constrained by invariant energy and conserved momentum. A stationary target wastes some lab energy on motion of the final centre of momentum, unlike a balanced collider.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="intuition" title="Build a mental model" layer="intuition">

At threshold the final particles can be at rest relative to one another in their centre-of-momentum frame. In the laboratory that whole final system may still have to move.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="concept" title="Definitions and hypotheses" layer="concept">

The total four-momentum norm determines the centre-of-momentum energy. In the formulas below, s denotes that energy squared: s=(Etotal)²−c²|ptotal|².

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="theory" title="Derivation, proof and limitations" layer="theory" fullOnly>

For incoming particles 1 and 2, conservation requires the same summed four-momentum before and after the collision. The energy-squared invariant is $s=m_1^2c^4+m_2^2c^4+2(E_1E_2-c^2p_1\cdot p_2)$. It is calculated in one frame but has the same value in every inertial frame.

For a target of mass m₂ initially at rest, E₂=m₂c² and p₂=0, so $s=m_1^2c^4+m_2^2c^4+2m_2c^2E_1$. If the final state has total rest-mass sum M and no unavoidable extra massless energy at threshold, its minimum allowed centre-of-momentum energy is Mc². Solving s=M²c⁴ gives the threshold projectile total energy. Subtract m₁c² to report kinetic rather than total energy.

This threshold is a necessary kinematic condition. Conserved charges, angular momentum, selection rules and a nonzero interaction amplitude must also allow the channel. If a massless final particle is mandatory, the ideal threshold can be an infimum approached as its energy tends to zero, needing careful process interpretation.

For equal opposing beams with equal energies E and opposite momenta, total momentum is zero and centre-of-momentum energy is 2E. For photons with energies E₁,E₂ and angle θ between their directions, $s=2E_1E_2(1-\cos\theta)$. Parallel photons have zero s and cannot create a massive pair in isolation; head-on photons maximize it at fixed energies.

For a two-body final state in its centre frame, momenta have equal magnitude and opposite directions. Energy conservation gives $E_3^*=(s+m_3^2c^4-m_4^2c^4)/(2\sqrt{s})$. The momentum then follows from its mass shell. Transforming to lab angles and energies requires another boost; the centre-frame isotropic distribution, if physically present, need not look isotropic in the laboratory.


### Derivation and transfer

For two equal-mass particles with opposite momenta in the center-of-momentum frame, total energy is 2sqrt(m²+p²) in c=1 units. A fixed-target collision puts energy into overall center-of-mass motion, so the usable invariant energy grows differently.

Threshold conditions apply to invariant energy and all conserved quantum numbers, not just a laboratory projectile energy. The display assumes a symmetric collider and does not calculate a cross section or detector acceptance.

</LessonSection>
