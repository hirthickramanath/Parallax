<LessonSection id="story" title="Why the question matters" layer="story">

Ladder operators derive the discrete spectrum algebraically and connect a formal commutator to normalization and physical scales.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="intuition" title="Build a mental model" layer="intuition">

<LessonSection id="concept" title="Definitions and hypotheses" layer="concept">

For H=p²/(2m)+mω²x²/2 with [x,p]=iℏ, define a=(mωx+ip)/√(2mℏω) and its adjoint a†.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="theory" title="Derivation, proof and limitations" layer="theory" fullOnly>

The canonical commutator gives [a,a†]=1 and $H=\hbar\omega(a^\dagger a+1/2)$. The number operator N=a†a has nonnegative expectation because ⟨ψ|N|ψ⟩=||aψ||². Commutators [N,a]=−a and [N,a†]=a† shift eigenvalues whenever the resulting vector is nonzero.

An indefinitely descending ladder would contradict nonnegativity. A normalizable lowest state satisfies a|0⟩=0, leading in position space to a Gaussian. Normalization gives a|n⟩=√n|n−1⟩ and a†|n⟩=√(n+1)|n+1⟩. Thus $E_n=\hbar\omega(n+1/2)$ for the stated full-line oscillator domain.

The ground-state widths are Δx=√(ℏ/(2mω)) and Δp=√(mℏω/2), saturating ΔxΔp=ℏ/2. The zero-point energy comes from kinetic and potential fluctuations, not an arbitrary independent heating mechanism. Physicists’ Hermite functions describe higher stationary states when the coordinate is scaled by √(ℏ/(mω)).

A coherent state satisfies a|α⟩=α|α⟩ and has a Poisson number distribution with mean |α|². Its centre follows the classical oscillator motion in this ideal quadratic Hamiltonian, while the state retains quantum fluctuations. Anharmonic systems do not retain the same equally spaced ladder or exact coherent-state propagation; adding a perturbation requires a controlled approximation.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="interactive" title="Investigate the claim" layer="interactive">

Calculate normalized low-number wavefunctions and compare node counts, widths and energy spacing. For a coherent-state number distribution with a chosen |α|², verify mean and variance. Do not identify a stationary number state’s zero mean position with absence of motion-related fluctuations.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="worked" title="A worked argument" layer="worked" fullOnly>

If ℏω=2, the ground energy is 1 and the n=3 energy is 7. Applying a† to |3⟩ produces coefficient √4=2 times |4⟩. The coefficient is an amplitude normalization, not a transition probability without an interaction model.

</LessonSection>
