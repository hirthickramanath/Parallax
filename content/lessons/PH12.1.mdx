<LessonSection id="puzzle" title="Commit to a prediction" layer="puzzle">

Why does one ideal hydrogen level split into several nearby observed frequencies? State a prediction and a reason before reading the argument.

<LessonSection id="story" title="Why the question matters" layer="story">

Fine, hyperfine and radiative effects operate at different scales and require consistent angular-momentum coupling.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="intuition" title="Build a mental model" layer="intuition">

Start with the concrete worked example below. Track which quantities belong to the system, which are imposed by its surroundings, and which conclusions require a limiting approximation.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="concept" title="Definitions and hypotheses" layer="concept">

Fine structure involves relativistic/spin-dependent electronic effects, while hyperfine structure couples nuclear and electronic degrees of freedom in a stated atomic model.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="theory" title="Derivation, proof and limitations" layer="theory" fullOnly>

The ideal Coulomb energy depends on n; fine structure introduces electronic total angular momentum j and terms from a consistent relativistic expansion. Radiative corrections such as the Lamb shift are additional physics, not a classical orbital-precession correction.

For a leading magnetic-dipole hyperfine model, $H_{hfs}=A\mathbf I\cdot\mathbf J/\hbar^2$ in an energy-valued A convention. With F=I+J, the energy is A[F(F+1)−I(I+1)−J(J+1)]/2. Other A conventions differ by ℏ factors and must not be mixed.

For I=J=1/2 the total F sectors are zero and one. Nuclear electric quadrupole contributions require the corresponding nuclear/electronic angular structure and vanish in relevant low-spin cases. Isotope shifts also reflect nuclear mass and size.

Actual line frequencies include fields, Doppler broadening, collisions and calibration. Precision spectroscopy compares a complete model with uncertainties; one elementary splitting formula is not a prediction of every measured feature or a new fundamental-constant determination.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="interactive" title="Investigate the claim" layer="interactive">

Enumerate coupled F labels and compute their degeneracies and shifts under the stated A convention. Add a weak field only after comparing its scale with the hyperfine gap.

<LessonSection id="worked" title="A worked argument" layer="worked" fullOnly>

For I=J=.5, the F=1 hyperfine shift is A/4 and F=0 shift −3A/4, giving a gap A. If A=4 energy units, upper shift is 1 and the gap is 4.

</LessonSection>
