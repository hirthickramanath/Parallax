<LessonSection id="story" title="Why the question matters" layer="story">

Occupation-number language organizes variable particle sectors and exchange statistics, but local fields remain operator-valued distributions.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="intuition" title="Build a mental model" layer="intuition">

<LessonSection id="concept" title="Definitions and hypotheses" layer="concept">

Fock space is a direct sum of appropriately symmetrized or antisymmetrized many-particle spaces; creation/annihilation operators change mode occupations.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="theory" title="Derivation, proof and limitations" layer="theory" fullOnly>

For boson modes, $[a_i,a_j^\dagger]=\delta_{ij}$ and same-type commutators vanish. Ladder normalization gives a†|n⟩=√(n+1)|n+1⟩. Fermion modes instead obey canonical anticommutators and allow occupations zero or one per complete mode.

A free Hamiltonian can be written as a sum of mode energies times number operators, with vacuum-energy terms treated according to the model/regularization. Mode labels refer to a chosen one-particle basis; changing basis transforms operators rather than creating physically distinguishable particle identities.

Field operators expand in mode functions and are generally distributions in spacetime. Products at the same point need a defined regularization/renormalized prescription. Replacing all operators by classical complex numbers loses commutation/statistical information except in controlled approximations.

Second quantization is a representation framework, not automatically an interacting solution. Interactions couple occupation states and can change particle numbers when allowed by the theory and conserved charges. Fermion ordering signs must be tracked consistently in basis states and operator products.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="interactive" title="Investigate the claim" layer="interactive">

Construct small truncated boson and exact fermion matrices, then check the appropriate algebra with explicit attention to boson truncation at its top state. Do not claim a finite truncated boson matrix obeys the infinite algebra everywhere.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="worked" title="A worked argument" layer="worked" fullOnly>

A boson creation operator acting on |3⟩ gives coefficient 2. A fermion mode cannot be raised from occupancy one to two. Two independent fermion modes span four occupation states before imposing any additional total-number constraint.

</LessonSection>
