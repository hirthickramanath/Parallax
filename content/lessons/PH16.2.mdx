<LessonSection id="theory" title="Derivation, proof and limitations" layer="theory" fullOnly>

The Einstein tensor is $G_{\mu\nu}=R_{\mu\nu}-g_{\mu\nu}R/2$. Its divergence vanishes through the contracted Bianchi identity, so a compatible matter solution obeys covariant stress-energy balance. This is not ordinary partial-derivative conservation of matter energy alone in arbitrary coordinates.

T contains energy density, momentum density, stresses and fluxes. In a weak static slow-motion limit, the appropriate metric perturbation recovers Poisson gravity with the usual assumptions. Setting every pressure term to zero is not valid for relativistic stars or cosmology.

Einstein equations have diffeomorphism redundancy and constraint/evolution structure. A coordinate/gauge choice is needed in calculation, and initial data must satisfy constraints. Not every arbitrarily drawn spatial metric and velocity field is allowed.

Vacuum means T=0 for the region, not necessarily zero curvature: gravitational waves and exterior tidal geometry can remain. A cosmological constant can be placed geometrically or modeled as an effective stress term with a consistent convention. Matching and boundary conditions choose a physical spacetime.


### Derivation and transfer

Einstein's equation relates spacetime curvature to stress-energy and cosmological constant under stated sign conventions. In four-dimensional vacuum with Rμν=Λgμν, contracting gives R=4Λ. Vacuum here excludes ordinary matter but need not imply zero curvature.

The display isolates this constant-curvature contraction. It is not a metric solver or a claim that all vacuum spacetimes are maximally symmetric; gravitational-wave and black-hole vacuum geometries need not be described by one scalar alone.

</LessonSection>
