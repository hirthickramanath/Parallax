<LessonSection id="concept" title="Definitions and hypotheses" layer="concept">

The microcanonical ensemble describes an isolated system with fixed energy, volume and particle number; a declared narrow energy shell specifies the accessible states.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="theory" title="Derivation, proof and limitations" layer="theory" fullOnly>

Let Ω count quantum states in the chosen energy shell. Boltzmann entropy is $S=k_B\ln\Omega$. Continuous classical phase space requires a dimensionless measure, conventionally including h^(3N) and N! for identical classical particles. The shell width and whether one uses a density or cumulative count must be stated; finite-system entropy conventions can differ.

For weakly coupled subsystems A and B with fixed total energy E, the count at allocation E_A is proportional to Ω_A(E_A)Ω_B(E−E_A). Its logarithmic derivative vanishes at an interior most-probable allocation when $\partial S_A/\partial E_A=\partial S_B/\partial E_B$. Defining 1/T=(∂S/∂E)_(V,N) gives equal equilibrium temperatures. The second derivative determines whether this stationary allocation is stable.

Macrostates with vastly larger multiplicity are overwhelmingly more probable when the equal-accessible-state postulate and relevant equilibration assumptions apply. This does not prove every trajectory explores every state on an experimentally accessible time. Ergodicity, mixing, conserved quantities and metastable barriers are dynamical questions, not consequences of writing a logarithm.

For independent distinguishable two-state sites, a macrostate with n excited sites has count C(N,n). The entropy of that macrostate uses this binomial count, while the total ensemble count includes all allowed macrostates. Small systems fluctuate visibly. Negative microcanonical heat capacities can occur in nonadditive systems such as some gravitating systems; canonical fluctuation positivity cannot simply rule those states out without matching ensembles and constraints.


### Derive, test and transfer

For independent weakly coupled subsystems whose accessible counts multiply, Ωtotal=ΩAΩB. Boltzmann entropy S=k lnΩ makes this multiplication additive: Stotal=SA+SB. The count must refer to the stated macrostates and constraints; mixing different coarse-graining conventions can shift constants and comparisons.

Thermodynamic temperature comes from the entropy derivative with respect to energy, not simply from the magnitude of S. Equalizing that derivative explains the most probable energy exchange under microcanonical constraints. Finite systems, indistinguishability and energy-shell definitions require care. The investigation is a discrete multiplicity budget, not an enumeration of a realistic many-body phase space.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="worked" title="A worked argument" layer="worked" fullOnly>

For four distinguishable two-state sites with exactly two excitations, Ω=C(4,2)=6 and S/k_B=ln6. With exactly one excitation Ω=4. The ratio of multiplicities is 6/4=1.5; equal site microstate weights do not imply equal macrostate probabilities.
