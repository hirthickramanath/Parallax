Why does a photon’s short local path not imply a short escape time from a star? State a prediction and a reason before reading the argument.

<LessonSection id="story" title="Why the question matters" layer="story">

Optically thick transport behaves diffusively, and opacity averages must match the physical transport regime.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="intuition" title="Build a mental model" layer="intuition">

Start with the concrete worked example below. Track which quantities belong to the system, which are imposed by its surroundings, and which conclusions require a limiting approximation.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="concept" title="Definitions and hypotheses" layer="concept">

Opacity per mass κ gives extinction per length κρ. Optical depth is an integral of κρ along a path, with absorption/scattering and spectral conventions stated.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="theory" title="Derivation, proof and limitations" layer="theory" fullOnly>

A mean free path is approximately $\ell=1/(\kappa\rho)$ in a homogeneous local model. A random walk over size R takes of order (R/ℓ)² steps, giving a rough diffusion time R²/(cℓ), up to geometry and transport coefficients. This differs from the straight crossing time R/c and assumes a suitable optically thick scattering/interaction picture.

In LTE optically thick radiative diffusion, $F_r=-4acT^3\nabla T/(3\kappa_R\rho)$. Combining spherical luminosity L_r=4πr²F_r gives dT/dr=−3κ_RρL_r/(16πac r²T³). The Rosseland mean is a weighted harmonic opacity average appropriate to diffusion, not a generic arithmetic average over observed spectral lines.

Absorption, emission and scattering must satisfy the model’s energy balance. Electron scattering, free–free, bound–free and lines contribute differently as ionization and composition change. Transport scattering opacities can differ from total scattering if angles are strongly forward peaked.

Near a photosphere or optically thin region the diffusion approximation fails and an angle/frequency-dependent transfer treatment is needed. LTE itself requires sufficient local thermal coupling; radiation and particle temperatures need not coincide everywhere. A grey opacity can be a controlled simplification but cannot reproduce every spectral feature.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="worked" title="A worked argument" layer="worked" fullOnly>

For κρ=4 in inverse-length units, the local mean free path is .25. With R=2 and c=1, the rough random-walk escape scale R²/(cℓ)=16, compared with crossing time 2. Numerical factors depend on the geometry and transport definition.
