<LessonSection id="concept" title="Definitions and hypotheses" layer="concept">

A Lie group is a smooth group with smooth multiplication/inversion. Its Lie algebra describes tangent generators and their bracket at identity.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="theory" title="Derivation, proof and limitations" layer="theory" fullOnly>

SO(3) consists of real three-by-three rotation matrices with RᵀR=I and determinant one. SU(2) consists of complex two-by-two unitary matrices with determinant one. Their Lie algebras are locally isomorphic, but SU(2) double covers SO(3); local generator relations do not determine global topology.

For angular momentum generators, $[J_i,J_j]=i\hbar\epsilon_{ijk}J_k$. Hermitian generators in physics and anti-Hermitian tangent matrices differ by factors of i and normalization. State a convention before comparing Lie-algebra structure constants.

A spin-half rotation is U=exp(−iθn·σ/2). At θ=2π it becomes −I, while its associated spatial vector rotation is identity. The spinor returns after 4π. A global sign leaves a single pure-state ray unchanged, while relative phases in an interference/composite experiment require full state accounting.

The exponential map provides local coordinates near identity under the appropriate theorem, but global surjectivity/injectivity differs among groups. Noncommuting rotations generate commutator effects at second order. A Lie-algebra approximation must retain an admitted small-angle regime.


### Derivation and transfer

For spin one-half, U(θ)=exp(−iθσz/2) has diagonal phases exp(∓iθ/2). A 2π rotation gives −I while 4π gives I. The map to spatial rotations is two-to-one, explaining why SO(3) and SU(2) differ globally despite closely related local algebras.

The real phase component plotted here is not a full spinor or observable probability. A global phase of one isolated state is unobservable, but relative phases in interference can matter.

</LessonSection>
