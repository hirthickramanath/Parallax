<LessonSection id="concept" title="Definitions and hypotheses" layer="concept">

Spectroscopic and computational evidence tests predictions of an electronic-structure model. Agreement with one observable is support under stated assumptions, not universal identification of the wavefunction.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="theory" title="Derivation, proof and limitations" layer="theory" fullOnly>

For a harmonic diatomic approximation, $\omega=\sqrt{k/\mu}$ and vibrational frequency depends on both force constant and reduced mass. A bond-length trend or frequency shift alone need not isolate bond order because environment, coupling and anharmonicity can also change the observable. IR intensity requires a changing dipole; Raman intensity involves polarizability response.

Rotational spectra constrain moments of inertia and geometry, while electronic and photoelectron spectra probe excitation or ionization energies with selection and relaxation effects. Isotopic substitution changes masses while mostly retaining the electronic potential at leading Born–Oppenheimer order, making it a useful model test. Nondetection can reflect sensitivity and selection rules rather than absence of a species.

A computational result must specify electronic method, basis, charge, spin state, geometry, convergence and environment. Hartree–Fock, density-functional and correlated wavefunction approximations treat exchange/correlation differently. A larger basis does not automatically fix a poor functional or wrong electronic state. Numerical convergence and physical-model accuracy are separate.

Compare predicted and measured quantities with units, calibration and uncertainties. Use more than one independent diagnostic and preserve provenance for any actual data. Fitting parameters to a spectrum and then using that same spectrum as independent validation overstates evidence. This lesson provides model calculations, not a new experimental detection or a benchmarked quantum-chemistry dataset.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="worked" title="A worked argument" layer="worked" fullOnly>

If force constant stays fixed and reduced mass quadruples, harmonic angular frequency halves. With k=8 and μ=2 in consistent units, ω=√4=2. An observed shift can therefore reflect isotopic mass without requiring the bond force constant to change.

</LessonSection>
