<LessonSection id="story" title="Why the question matters" layer="story">

Wave models connect string experiments, acoustic modes and field propagation. A displacement snapshot cannot distinguish left-moving and right-moving components.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="intuition" title="Build a mental model" layer="intuition">

A stretched string stores elastic energy in its slope and kinetic energy in its velocity. Both are required to determine later motion.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="concept" title="Definitions and hypotheses" layer="concept">

The one-dimensional homogeneous wave equation uₜₜ=c²uₓₓ has wave speed c>0. Its initial-value problem specifies displacement f(x) and velocity g(x), with boundary conditions on a finite interval.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="theory" title="Derivation, proof and limitations" layer="theory" fullOnly>

For a uniform taut string with tension T, linear density μ and small transverse slope, force balance yields $\mu u_{tt}=T u_{xx}$ and c²=T/μ. This derivation neglects bending stiffness, damping and nonlinear stretching. A model of a different medium requires its own constitutive assumptions even if the same PDE results.

On the infinite line, suitable data give d’Alembert’s formula $u(x,t)=[f(x-ct)+f(x+ct)]/2+(2c)^{-1}\int_{x-ct}^{x+ct}g(s)ds$. The first term splits the shape and the integral includes initial velocity. Only data in the interval [x−ct,x+ct] affect that point at time t. This finite domain of dependence differs from the ideal heat equation.

For fixed ends on 0&lt;x&lt;L, separation gives sin(nπx/L) and angular frequencies ωₙ=cnπ/L. Each modal amplitude is Aₙcosωₙt+Bₙsinωₙt, with Aₙ fixed by displacement and ωₙBₙ by velocity. Angular frequency in radians/time differs from frequency in cycles/time by 2π. Endpoint compatibility depends on the desired solution regularity.

The physical energy is $E=\frac12\int[\mu u_t^2+T u_x^2]dx$. Multiply the equation by uₜ and integrate by parts: dE/dt is the boundary power T[uₓuₜ] at the endpoints, absent forcing or damping. Fixed ends have zero endpoint velocity and no boundary work. Local displacements can vanish while energy resides in velocity; a flat instantaneous shape is not necessarily equilibrium.

A centered explicit time/space scheme has the standard Courant condition cΔt/Δx≤1 for this uniform one-dimensional problem. Its numerical dispersion can still distort a stable solution. Reflecting, free and absorbing boundaries have different physics; an arbitrary zero endpoint can produce reflections absent from the intended infinite domain. Superposition follows from linearity and does not extend unchanged to nonlinear waves.


### Derive, test and transfer

For u_tt=c²u_xx on [0,L] with fixed endpoints, a separated spatial sine mode has angular frequency ωn=cnπ/L. Its time amplitude is a linear combination of cosine and sine fixed by both initial displacement and initial velocity. One time initial condition is insufficient for a second-order evolution.

In the ideal undamped model, energy exchanges between kinetic and gradient terms while total energy is conserved. A standing mode does not carry a net one-way traveling wave through the boundaries. Dispersion, damping or nonuniform coefficients change the spectrum. A plotted phase slice illustrates an exact selected mode, not a general wavepacket or every boundary configuration.

</LessonSection>
