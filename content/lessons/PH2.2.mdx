<LessonSection id="story" title="Why the question matters" layer="story">

Generalized coordinates and ideal virtual work can eliminate unknown reaction forces. Eliminating them is a mathematical consequence of the allowed variation space, not proof that the support or tension vanished.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="intuition" title="Build a mental model" layer="intuition">

Allowed virtual displacements lie tangent to the constraint. An ideal normal reaction can do no virtual work along them while still supplying a large physical force.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="concept" title="Definitions and hypotheses" layer="concept">

A holonomic constraint is a relation among coordinates and possibly time. Ideal constraint reactions do zero virtual work for admitted virtual displacements under the stated mechanical principle.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="theory" title="Derivation, proof and limitations" layer="theory" fullOnly>

For constraints gα(q,t)=0 with independent coordinate gradients, local coordinate reduction can describe the constraint manifold with fewer independent variables. Dependence or singular points can break that local parameterization. A time-dependent holonomic constraint is rheonomous; a time-independent one is scleronomous.

In a Cartesian constrained problem, Lagrange multipliers can retain reaction forces. For ideal holonomic constraints, a consistent equation is $d(L_{\dot q_i})/dt-L_{q_i}=\sum_\alpha\lambda_\alpha\partial g_\alpha/\partial q_i$ with the convention chosen for the multipliers. Solve these equations together with gα=0; multiplier dimensions depend on constraint normalization.

For a particle constrained to a sphere, a multiplier force normal to the sphere supplies the needed radial acceleration. Replacing g by twice g changes the multiplier but not the physical reaction when the full product λ∇g is transformed consistently. The multiplier value alone is not a coordinate-independent force magnitude.

A velocity relation a(q,t)·q̇+b(q,t)=0 is not automatically an integrable position constraint. Rolling without slipping is a common source of nonholonomic restrictions. Directly treating every such relation as a variational holonomic constraint can lead to equations different from physical d'Alembert dynamics.

A moving ideal constraint can transfer energy even when its reaction does no virtual work in the relevant instantaneous variation class. Virtual displacements are not identical to the actual time displacement of a moving support. State the support motion and energy exchange before invoking conservation of the unconstrained mechanical energy.


### Variational derivation and transfer case

For a regular holonomic constraint $f(q,t)=0$, allowed virtual displacements at fixed time obey $\nabla f\cdot\delta q=0$. A multiplier reaction $\lambda\nabla f$ then has zero virtual work on those variations. Its numerical multiplier depends on how the same constraint equation is scaled; the physical reaction must be reconstructed from the product.

Actual velocities satisfy $\nabla f\cdot\dot q+f_t=0$. Therefore a moving ideal constraint can have power $\lambda\nabla f\cdot\dot q=-\lambda f_t$, which need not vanish. Zero virtual work is not zero actual energy exchange with moving apparatus. Counting constraints also requires independence and local regularity; dependent equations do not each remove a separate degree of freedom.

</LessonSection>
