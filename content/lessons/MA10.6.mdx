<LessonSection id="story" title="Why the question matters" layer="story">

Spacetime symmetry includes rotations, boosts and translations, and their composition controls relativistic state classification.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="concept" title="Definitions and hypotheses" layer="concept">

With metric η=diag(1,−1,−1,−1), the Lorentz group preserves ΛᵀηΛ=η. The Poincaré group also includes spacetime translations.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="theory" title="Derivation, proof and limitations" layer="theory" fullOnly>

The full Lorentz group includes disconnected parity/time-orientation components. The proper orthochronous subgroup preserves determinant and future time orientation. A boost along one direction can be parameterized by rapidity χ with β=tanhχ, so collinear rapidities add even though velocities do not add linearly.

Generators of noncollinear boosts have commutators proportional to rotations, with convention-dependent factors/signs. Thomas/Wigner rotations reflect this noncommutative structure. Treating every boost composition as one unchanged-axis Galilean velocity sum misses it.

Translations transform as spacetime vectors under Lorentz transformations, giving a semidirect rather than independent direct-product structure. Invariant momentum norm is $P^\mu P_\mu=m^2c^2$ for a massive on-shell particle in this signature. Energy and each momentum component individually depend on frame.

Relativistic quantum state representations involve mass and spin/helicity labels with little-group structure. Fields and particle states transform in related but distinct representations; a finite-dimensional spinor field matrix is not by itself the full unitary Hilbert-space representation. Curved-spacetime local frames do not imply one global Poincaré symmetry for an arbitrary geometry.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="interactive" title="Investigate the claim" layer="interactive">

<LessonSection id="worked" title="A worked argument" layer="worked" fullOnly>

For E=5,p=3 and c=1 in a one-dimensional massive example, m²=E²−p²=16 and m=4. Two collinear boosts each with rapidity χ add to 2χ, not generally twice the velocity.

</LessonSection>
