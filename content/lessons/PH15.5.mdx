<LessonSection id="story" title="Why the question matters" layer="story">

QED is a local gauge theory with renormalized parameters, infrared qualifications and observable-state definitions.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="intuition" title="Build a mental model" layer="intuition">

Start with the concrete worked example below. Track which quantities belong to the system, which are imposed by its surroundings, and which conclusions require a limiting approximation.

<LessonSection id="concept" title="Definitions and hypotheses" layer="concept">

QED couples a charged Dirac field to an abelian gauge field through a covariant derivative and a stated charge/sign convention.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="theory" title="Derivation, proof and limitations" layer="theory" fullOnly>

A common natural-unit structure is $\mathcal L=-F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}/4+\bar\psi(i\gamma^\mu D_\mu-m)\psi$. Local gauge transformations act consistently on matter and potential; gauge fixing removes redundant calculation variables rather than changing observables.

Ward identities connect parts of amplitudes/correlation functions and enforce gauge-consistent relations when the regulator/renormalization preserve the symmetry. Dropping arbitrary pieces of one order can spoil those relations. A physical observable should not depend on the chosen gauge parameter after consistent computation.

The coupling α=e²/(4πℏc) in a specified unit convention is a scale-defined renormalized quantity. Precision calculations include loop and real-emission contributions appropriate to the measurement. Bare parameters are not directly inserted as measured constants without a matching prescription.

Soft-photon infrared structure requires inclusive or otherwise well-defined observable constructions; an exact no-extra-photon exclusive idealization can be singular. Detector thresholds matter. QED is highly tested in its regime, but that is not a declaration that a finite tutorial amplitude describes strong fields, composite structure or every experimental correction.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="interactive" title="Investigate the claim" layer="interactive">

Check gauge consistency of a supplied elementary current-conservation calculation and distinguish a renormalized parameter input from a bare symbol. Define photon-energy acceptance before discussing an inclusive observable.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="worked" title="A worked argument" layer="worked" fullOnly>

In normalized units 4πℏc=1 and e=.2, α=.04. Doubling e gives four times this coupling parameter. These deliberately normalized values are not the measured physical fine-structure constant.

</LessonSection>
