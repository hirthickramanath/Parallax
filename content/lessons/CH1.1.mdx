Why does a component move even when the total temperature and pressure stay fixed? State a prediction and a reason before reading the argument.

<LessonSection id="story" title="Why the question matters" layer="story">

Chemical potential connects composition, diffusion and reaction equilibrium. Temperature and pressure alone do not determine the thermodynamic tendency when material can redistribute.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="intuition" title="Build a mental model" layer="intuition">

Think of chemical potential as the free-energy price of adding a small amount of one component while the stated other variables stay fixed.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="concept" title="Definitions and hypotheses" layer="concept">

For a simple homogeneous phase, μᵢ is the partial derivative of Gibbs energy with respect to amount nᵢ at fixed temperature, pressure and other component amounts.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="theory" title="Derivation, proof and limitations" layer="theory" fullOnly>

For a simple compressible multicomponent system, $dG=-S\,dT+V\,dp+\sum_i\mu_i\,dn_i$. Thus μ has energy-per-mole units when n is molar amount. It is a partial molar quantity, not generally the average G divided by total amount. For an extensive homogeneous phase, Euler's relation gives G=∑niμi, and Gibbs–Duhem constrains the component potentials: at fixed T,p, ∑ni dμi=0.

For a reaction extent ξ with dni=νi dξ, the fixed-T,p derivative is $\Delta_rG=\sum_i\nu_i\mu_i$. A permitted infinitesimal forward change lowers G when this is negative. At an unconstrained interior reaction equilibrium it is zero; boundaries, constrained reactions and nonequilibrium driving need separate treatment.

Represent component chemical potential as $\mu_i=\mu_i^\circ+RT\ln a_i$, with dimensionless activity and a specified standard state. This produces ΔrG=ΔrG°+RT lnQ and K=exp(−ΔrG°/RT) at equilibrium. A standard chemical potential is not a universal zero, and its convention changes together with activity.

Between two phases at common T,p that can exchange component i, a small transfer changes total G by (μi,2−μi,1)dn. Equilibrium therefore requires equality of the applicable chemical potentials. Charged species and external fields need electrochemical or other generalized potentials; plain μ alone does not encode all work terms.

A low Gibbs energy does not imply rapid equilibration. Reaction barriers, interfaces and transport can prevent the system reaching the thermodynamic minimum on the observed timescale. Local thermodynamic equilibrium can supply state functions for small regions while the complete system still carries fluxes.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="worked" title="A worked argument" layer="worked" fullOnly>

For a component whose activity rises from 1 to 2 at fixed temperature and standard state, chemical potential rises by RT ln2. In units of RT the increase is ln2≈0.693147. Increasing concentration alone gives this result only if the associated activity model supports it.

</LessonSection>
