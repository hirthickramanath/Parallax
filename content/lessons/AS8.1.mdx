<LessonSection id="story" title="Why the question matters" layer="story">

Galactic maps combine incomplete distance, velocity and extinction information; a survey’s selection changes the apparent populations.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="concept" title="Definitions and hypotheses" layer="concept">

The Milky Way has disc, bulge/bar and halo components with distinct but overlapping spatial, chemical and dynamical distributions.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="theory" title="Derivation, proof and limitations" layer="theory" fullOnly>

For an axisymmetric circular approximation, $\Omega(R)=v_c(R)/R$. A rotation curve is not identical to every observed star’s azimuthal velocity, because random motion, noncircular streams and selection contribute. Converting heliocentric measurements requires solar position/velocity conventions and a coordinate frame.

Distances from parallax, candles or population models carry different uncertainties. Dust extinction obscures some sight lines and changes colours; near/far geometry can be degenerate in kinematic-distance methods. A brightness map is not a direct mass-density map.

The thin/thick disc, bar, stellar halo and gaseous components are useful descriptions rather than perfectly disjoint universal categories. Abundance patterns and phase-space substructure preserve formation information, but mixing and selection complicate assigning every star a unique origin.

Survey footprints, magnitude limits, completeness and astrometric/radial-velocity quality shape a catalogue. Correcting selection requires a model of what could enter the sample. Use multiple tracers and retain calibration/provenance rather than interpreting an apparent deficit as an empty galactic volume.


### Derive, test and transfer

An exponential tracer profile $\rho(z)=\rho_0e^{-|z|/h}$ has vertical column density $\Sigma=\int_{-\infty}^\infty\rho dz=2\rho_0h$. Increasing scale height at fixed midplane density increases the column; holding column fixed instead reduces the midplane density. Specify the controlled normalization before comparing discs.

The Milky Way has multiple populations, gas, dust, a bulge and a halo; one exponential is not its complete mass distribution. A magnitude-limited sample also does not directly reveal volume density without completeness and extinction treatment. Converting a tracer distribution into gravitational mass needs dynamics and a mass-to-tracer relation. The investigation describes a geometric profile rather than solving vertical equilibrium.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="worked" title="A worked argument" layer="worked" fullOnly>

For circular speed 200 and radius 10 in compatible units, angular speed is 20 in speed-per-length units. At the same speed and radius 20 it is 10. A flat circular-speed curve therefore is not solid-body rotation.
