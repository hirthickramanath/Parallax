Why does an accelerating spherical source not emit every possible gravitational-wave mode? State a prediction and a reason before reading the argument.

<LessonSection id="story" title="Why the question matters" layer="story">

Gravitational radiation obeys stress conservation and tensor symmetry, with wave-zone and gauge qualifications.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="intuition" title="Build a mental model" layer="intuition">

Start with the concrete worked example below. Track which quantities belong to the system, which are imposed by its surroundings, and which conclusions require a limiting approximation.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="concept" title="Definitions and hypotheses" layer="concept">

Weak radiative metric perturbations have transverse-traceless tensor modes in an appropriate far-field flat-background approximation.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="theory" title="Derivation, proof and limitations" layer="theory" fullOnly>

Linearize g=η+h with |h|≪1 and choose a suitable gauge. Vacuum radiative perturbations satisfy a wave equation. Gauge components are not all independent physical polarizations; ordinary GR has two tensor polarizations for the familiar massless wave sector.

For a slow localized source, leading radiation depends on the second time derivative of a trace-free mass quadrupole, schematically $h\sim G\ddot Q/(c^4R)$. Conservation removes a naive isolated monopole/dipole radiation term of the same kind. Exact spherical motion does not radiate gravitational waves in the corresponding isolated GR setting.

Wave energy flux arises at an appropriate quadratic/averaged perturbative order and is not a coordinate-invariant local linear h energy density for every background. Binary inspiral converts orbital binding energy into radiation under model assumptions. Strong merger dynamics needs nonlinear evolution.

Detector response, polarization, propagation and source extraction all need consistent conventions. The quadrupole formula has slow-motion/weak-source limits and cannot replace a validated relativistic waveform in every compact merger phase.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="interactive" title="Investigate the claim" layer="interactive">

Compute a rotating quadrupole’s time derivatives and compare with an exactly spherical pulsation. Separate source generation, far-zone propagation and detector projection.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="worked" title="A worked argument" layer="worked" fullOnly>

With a schematic proportionality h=C/R and fixed source coefficient, doubling distance halves strain and quarters wave intensity. These amplitude/intensity scalings differ, while orientation can modify the coefficient.

</LessonSection>
