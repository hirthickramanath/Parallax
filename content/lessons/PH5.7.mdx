Use x⁰=ct and metric diag(1,−1,−1,−1). Electromagnetic fields form an antisymmetric tensor; invariant combinations constrain what frames can observe.

<LessonSection id="theory" title="Derivation, proof and limitations" layer="theory" fullOnly>

With A^μ=(φ/c,A), define $F^{\mu\nu}=\partial^\mu A^\nu-\partial^\nu A^\mu$. In this convention F⁰ⁱ=−E_i/c and Fⁱʲ=−εᵢⱼₖB_k. Maxwell’s sourced equation is $\partial_\mu F^{\mu\nu}=\mu_0J^\nu$ with J^μ=(cρ,J). Antisymmetry implies ∂_νJ^ν=0 when derivatives commute.

Under a Lorentz transformation, F′=ΛFΛᵀ in matrix component notation for the contravariant tensor. For a boost with frame velocity v, the familiar split has E′_parallel=E_parallel and E′_perp=γ(E+v×B)_perp, with B′_perp=γ(B−v×E/c²)_perp. Reversing the boost convention reverses the appropriate v sign; compare definitions rather than memorizing an unsigned cross product.

The scalars E·B and E²−c²B² are Lorentz invariants. If E·B≠0, no inertial frame can make either field vanish completely. If E·B=0 and E²>c²B², an electric-only frame can exist for the uniform local field classification; the opposite sign permits a magnetic-only one. A nonzero null field with both invariants zero cannot be transformed away, as a plane wave illustrates.

The four-force law can be written $dp^\mu/d\tau=qF^{\mu\nu}u_\nu$ in this convention. Its spatial and temporal parts reproduce the Lorentz force and power qE·v. Tensor covariance does not remove source dynamics or boundary conditions. Global curved-spacetime extensions require covariant derivatives and geometric structure beyond a flat-frame boost.


### Derive, test and transfer

Electric and magnetic fields are frame-dependent components of one antisymmetric electromagnetic tensor. A Lorentz transformation can mix them while preserving tensor invariants. With an explicit sign convention, useful scalar combinations include B²−E²/c² and E dot B. Different metric/tensor conventions alter component signs without changing an invariant physical statement.

A nonzero E dot B cannot be transformed to a frame with either field identically zero. If the dot product vanishes, the other invariant further constrains which simplified frame may exist. The display evaluates a stated orthogonal-field case; it is not a complete Lorentz-tensor transformation or permission to compare components in unrelated frames without transforming them.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="worked" title="A worked argument" layer="worked" fullOnly>

For perpendicular fields with |E|=3, c=2 and |B|=1, the invariant E²−c²B² is 9−4=5 and E·B=0. This is electric-dominated, not null. A vacuum plane wave instead satisfies E=cB and E·B=0.
