<LessonSection id="concept" title="Definitions and hypotheses" layer="concept">

The nearest-neighbour Ising model has spins s_i=±1 and energy H=−J∑⟨ij⟩s_i s_j−h∑s_i, with each bond counted once.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="theory" title="Derivation, proof and limitations" layer="theory" fullOnly>

For two spins in zero field with one bond, aligned states have energy −J and opposite states +J. The partition function is $Z=2e^{\beta J}+2e^{-\beta J}$. Local spin correlations encode the energy preference even when the finite symmetric system has zero mean magnetization at h=0. Spontaneous symmetry breaking requires an appropriate thermodynamic limit and ordering of the field limit.

The one-dimensional finite-range zero-field chain has no positive-temperature phase transition in the infinite limit. A finite domain wall costs a finite energy, while possible wall locations supply entropy. In contrast, the two-dimensional nearest-neighbour ferromagnet on the square lattice has a positive critical temperature. Dimension, range and lattice/model assumptions therefore cannot be omitted from a statement about Ising criticality.

For the connected correlation C(r)=⟨s(0)s(r)⟩−⟨s⟩², a correlation length describes exponential decay away from criticality in the relevant regimes. Near a continuous transition the length can diverge in the thermodynamic limit. Finite simulations round singular behaviour; a sharp-looking finite-data curve is not a literal finite-system nonanalyticity.

Renormalisation groups coarse grain and rescale, generating effective couplings rather than simply replacing every block by one unchanged microscopic interaction. Fixed points organize scale-invariant behaviour. Relevant perturbations grow, irrelevant ones decay and marginal ones require more analysis. Universality classes depend on symmetry, dimension, range and other relevant structures, not the claim that every system has the same exponents. Controlled approximations and finite-size scaling are necessary before inferring critical exponents numerically.


### Derive, test and transfer

For the infinite one-dimensional nearest-neighbor ferromagnetic Ising chain at zero field and positive T, spin correlations decay as [tanh(J/(kT))]^r. Writing this as exp(−r/ξ) gives ξ=−1/ln[tanh(J/(kT))] in lattice spacings. It stays finite for every positive T and diverges only as T approaches zero.

This chain is not the two-dimensional square-lattice problem and does not have its finite-temperature critical point. A renormalization argument must preserve the model, dimensionality and induced couplings consistently rather than assuming one exponent fits every Ising setting. The display illustrates exact correlation-length behavior for the specified chain, not a Monte Carlo simulation or a general critical phase diagram.

</LessonSection>
