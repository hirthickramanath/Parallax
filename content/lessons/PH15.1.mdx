Why is a fixed-particle Schrödinger model insufficient for general relativistic interactions? State a prediction and a reason before reading the argument.

<LessonSection id="story" title="Why the question matters" layer="story">

Relativity, locality and quantum statistics motivate fields with variable particle sectors rather than simply replacing kinetic energy in one wave equation.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="intuition" title="Build a mental model" layer="intuition">

<LessonSection id="concept" title="Definitions and hypotheses" layer="concept">

Quantum field theory describes quantum degrees of freedom distributed over spacetime, with particles emerging as suitable excitations/asymptotic states in an admitted background.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="theory" title="Derivation, proof and limitations" layer="theory" fullOnly>

Relativistic energy permits creation when interactions supply enough energy while conserving charges and momentum. A fixed-particle-number Hilbert space cannot describe every such process. For two equal masses at rest in the final state the threshold energy scale includes $2mc^2$, but lab-frame thresholds also depend on initial momentum conservation.

Local quantum fields are operator-valued distributions. Causal separation is implemented through appropriate commutators/anticommutators and observables, not by assigning a classical trajectory to every virtual intermediate term. Free-field excitations and interacting particle definitions require different qualifications.

An effective field theory describes a regime below a cutoff with symmetries, degrees of freedom and an ordered set of operators. It can predict systematically without claiming validity at every energy. Matching and uncertainty from omitted orders are part of the framework.

A Feynman diagram is a term-organizing device in an expansion, not an observed movie of virtual particles following paths. Nonperturbative phases, confinement and curved/time-dependent backgrounds can complicate a simple particle picture. QFT is a framework requiring a specified dynamics, state and measurement model.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="interactive" title="Investigate the claim" layer="interactive">

Compare a fixed two-level particle model and a Fock-space process with explicit conserved charges. Identify what additional sectors are needed rather than inserting a creation event into a wavefunction with fixed particle count.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="worked" title="A worked argument" layer="worked" fullOnly>

For m=3,c=1, two-rest-mass energy is 6. That arithmetic alone is not a complete laboratory reaction threshold because recoil and the initial state must also satisfy momentum conservation.

</LessonSection>
