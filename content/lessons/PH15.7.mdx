<LessonSection id="concept" title="Definitions and hypotheses" layer="concept">

A Yang–Mills theory uses a Lie-algebra-valued connection with a curvature containing a commutator term and specified coupling conventions.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="theory" title="Derivation, proof and limitations" layer="theory" fullOnly>

In a component convention, $F^a_{\mu\nu}=\partial_\mu A^a_\nu-\partial_\nu A^a_\mu+gf^{abc}A^b_\mu A^c_\nu$. The nonlinear term reflects the generator algebra, not an arbitrary added classical nonlinearity. Signs/factors differ with the covariant-derivative convention.

The action includes a gauge-invariant contraction of F with itself. Gauge fixing, constraints and ghosts in suitable quantization formalisms are calculation structures needed for a consistent redundant-variable treatment; they are not extra detected asymptotic matter particles.

For QCD, colour confinement and strong low-energy behaviour require nonperturbative understanding, while asymptotic freedom supports weak-coupling calculations at sufficiently high scales. A running perturbative formula cannot be trusted unaltered into strong coupling.

Representations and anomaly cancellation constrain consistent matter content. Local Lie algebra and global gauge-group structure need not encode identical topological data. Lattice and other methods add regulator/continuum-limit questions beyond an introductory diagram expansion.


### Derivation and transfer

Non-Abelian gauge generators do not commute, so field strength contains connection commutator terms in addition to derivatives. For Pauli generators σi/2, [T1,T2]=iT3. This algebraic structure is not reproduced by simply copying an Abelian potential three times.

The display shows the coefficient of a commutator for constant chosen algebra components. It is not a full spacetime Yang–Mills solution, gauge-invariant energy density or confinement calculation. Generator normalization and coupling convention are stated explicitly.

</LessonSection>
