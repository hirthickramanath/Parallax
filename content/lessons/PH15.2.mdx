An action S=∫L d⁴x is stationary under admissible field variations; derivatives and boundary terms determine the field equations.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="theory" title="Derivation, proof and limitations" layer="theory" fullOnly>

Vary a scalar and integrate derivative terms by parts. With suitable vanishing boundary variations, $\partial_\mu[\partial\mathcal L/\partial(\partial_\mu\phi)]-\partial\mathcal L/\partial\phi=0$. A boundary term cannot simply be discarded when its variation is not controlled.

In c=ℏ=1 and signature (+−−−), a real free scalar has L=(∂φ)²/2−m²φ²/2, giving (□+m²)φ=0. Canonical momentum for a chosen time slicing is π=∂L/∂φ̇=φ̇ in this model, not a universal formula for every constrained field.

Continuous variational symmetries yield Noether currents on shell. Translations supply an energy-momentum construction, but improvement terms and gauge constraints affect the convenient tensor form. Local currents can change by identically conserved terms without changing a suitably controlled total charge.

Gauge redundancy requires constraints rather than treating every component as an independent physical degree of freedom. Explicit symmetry breaking and boundary forcing alter conservation. Quantization and anomalies are further steps; a classical identity is not automatically every quantum Ward identity.

### Keep the boundary term in the variation

Write $\pi^\mu=\partial\mathcal L/\partial(\partial_\mu\phi)$. For a compact region, integration by parts gives

$$\delta S=\int [\mathcal L_{,\phi}-\partial_\mu\pi^\mu]\delta\phi\,d^4x+\int_{\partial V}\pi^\mu\delta\phi\,d\Sigma_\mu.$$

Vanishing variations on the boundary justify removing the second integral. Arbitrary interior variations then imply the field equation. For a fixed-coordinate transformation $\delta\phi=\epsilon\Delta\phi$ with constant parameter and $\delta\mathcal L=\epsilon\partial_\mu K^\mu$, the on-shell identity gives $j^\mu=\pi^\mu\Delta\phi-K^\mu$ and $\partial_\mu j^\mu=0$. This formula assumes a global transformation at fixed coordinates; spacetime transformations need the corresponding coordinate terms.

### Conservation includes flux

For a fixed spatial region $V$, the continuity equation implies $dQ_V/dt=-\int_{\partial V}\mathbf j\cdot\mathbf n\,dA$. A conserved local current need not give a constant charge inside an open region. Appropriate decay, periodic boundaries, or vanishing outward flux is required for a conserved integrated charge.


### Derivation and transfer

For a complex scalar field with a global phase symmetry, a Noether current can be formed from the field and its derivatives. Conservation is an on-shell statement with the specified Lagrangian and boundary conditions, not a property of every arbitrary input function.

The display uses a single harmonic complex mode and a declared current convention giving density proportional to 2ω|A|². It is an illustrative classical charge density, not a normalized single-particle probability or a full field solution with interactions.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="worked" title="A worked argument" layer="worked" fullOnly>

For a homogeneous scalar with L=φ̇²/2−m²φ²/2, φ̇=3 gives canonical momentum 3. With m=2,φ=1, Hamiltonian density is 9/2+4/2=6.5.

The massless real scalar has shift symmetry $\Delta\phi=1$ and $K^\mu=0$, so $j^\mu=\partial^\mu\phi$. Its divergence is $\Box\phi=0$ on shell. Add $-m^2\phi^2/2$ to the Lagrangian: the same candidate current now has divergence $-m^2\phi$. The mass term breaks shift symmetry; it does not invalidate Noether's theorem.

With the stated signature, the free Hamiltonian density is $\mathcal H=\tfrac12\dot\phi^2+\tfrac12|\nabla\phi|^2+\tfrac12m^2\phi^2$. The positive spatial-gradient contribution follows from the Legendre transform, despite the negative gradient term in the Lagrangian.

</LessonSection>
