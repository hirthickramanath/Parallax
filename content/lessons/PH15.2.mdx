<LessonSection id="story" title="Why the question matters" layer="story">

Classical field variational structure supplies the starting point for quantization, with boundary and improvement conventions explicit.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="intuition" title="Build a mental model" layer="intuition">

Start with the concrete worked example below. Track which quantities belong to the system, which are imposed by its surroundings, and which conclusions require a limiting approximation.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="concept" title="Definitions and hypotheses" layer="concept">

An action S=∫L d⁴x is stationary under admissible field variations; derivatives and boundary terms determine the field equations.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="theory" title="Derivation, proof and limitations" layer="theory" fullOnly>

Vary a scalar and integrate derivative terms by parts. With suitable vanishing boundary variations, $\partial_\mu[\partial\mathcal L/\partial(\partial_\mu\phi)]-\partial\mathcal L/\partial\phi=0$. A boundary term cannot simply be discarded when its variation is not controlled.

In c=ℏ=1 and signature (+−−−), a real free scalar has L=(∂φ)²/2−m²φ²/2, giving (□+m²)φ=0. Canonical momentum for a chosen time slicing is π=∂L/∂φ̇=φ̇ in this model, not a universal formula for every constrained field.

Continuous variational symmetries yield Noether currents on shell. Translations supply an energy-momentum construction, but improvement terms and gauge constraints affect the convenient tensor form. Local currents can change by identically conserved terms without changing a suitably controlled total charge.

Gauge redundancy requires constraints rather than treating every component as an independent physical degree of freedom. Explicit symmetry breaking and boundary forcing alter conservation. Quantization and anomalies are further steps; a classical identity is not automatically every quantum Ward identity.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="interactive" title="Investigate the claim" layer="interactive">

Derive the free scalar equation with an explicit boundary term. Then compare a Noether charge with and without boundary flux, keeping the action and variation domain fixed.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="worked" title="A worked argument" layer="worked" fullOnly>

For a homogeneous scalar with L=φ̇²/2−m²φ²/2, φ̇=3 gives canonical momentum 3. With m=2,φ=1, Hamiltonian density is 9/2+4/2=6.5.

</LessonSection>
