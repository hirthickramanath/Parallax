<LessonSection id="puzzle" title="Commit to a prediction" layer="puzzle">

Why can a catalyst speed a reaction without changing its equilibrium constant? State a prediction and a reason before reading the argument.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="story" title="Why the question matters" layer="story">

Surface chemistry combines adsorption, site availability and reaction dynamics. Catalysts offer pathways with different barriers while the same overall thermodynamic endpoints retain their equilibrium relation.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="intuition" title="Build a mental model" layer="intuition">

A surface has a finite number of sites. Binding reactants helps only if sites remain accessible and products can leave.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="concept" title="Definitions and hypotheses" layer="concept">

Adsorption coverage θ is the fraction of sites occupied in a specified model. Catalysis changes kinetics through a pathway while preserving the equilibrium free-energy difference for the same net reaction.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="theory" title="Derivation, proof and limitations" layer="theory" fullOnly>

For an ideal single-species Langmuir model, adsorption and desorption rates are proportional to p(1−θ) and θ. Balancing them gives $\theta=Kp/(1+Kp)$ when K uses reciprocal-pressure units in that convention. A thermodynamic equilibrium constant instead uses a dimensionless pressure ratio; the definition must be stated before comparing numerical K values.

The model assumes identical independent sites, one occupancy per site and no lateral interactions. At low Kp, θ≈Kp; at high Kp, θ approaches 1. Heterogeneous binding energies, multisite adsorption and interacting adsorbates can produce other isotherms.

A Langmuir–Hinshelwood pathway reacts adsorbed species; an Eley–Rideal description involves an incident species reacting with an adsorbate. Those labels alone do not fix a unique rate law. Competitive adsorption and site balance determine concentration dependence, and excessive binding can inhibit turnover by blocking sites or slowing product release.

A catalyst lowers an effective kinetic barrier by changing the mechanism, not the standard overall reaction Gibbs energy. Forward and reverse pathways remain consistent with the same equilibrium constant at fixed conditions. Comparing an apparent one-way rate under flow with an equilibrium constant confuses a driven reactor with equilibrium.

On interstellar grains, surface temperature, diffusion, stochastic low occupancy and irradiation can invalidate a continuum mean-coverage treatment. Rate-equation, stochastic or microscopic models should be chosen according to the number of particles and timescales. An ideal isotherm fit alone does not establish the unique surface mechanism.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="worked" title="A worked argument" layer="worked" fullOnly>

For a declared Langmuir convention with Kp=3, coverage is 3/4=0.75. At Kp=1 it is 0.5. Doubling pressure from Kp=3 to 6 raises coverage to 6/7, not to 1.5; saturation prevents linear scaling.

</LessonSection>
