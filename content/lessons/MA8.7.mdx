<LessonSection id="story" title="Why the question matters" layer="story">

Error analysis separates random spread, calibration offsets and model discrepancy. Fitting a curve is an inference problem whose assumptions determine both the reported parameters and their uncertainty.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="intuition" title="Build a mental model" layer="intuition">

A best-fit curve is the closest allowed model according to a chosen residual metric. It can be consistently displaced from reality if the metric or the model misses a systematic effect.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="concept" title="Definitions and hypotheses" layer="concept">

For a differentiable map y=f(x), first-order covariance propagation is JΣxJᵀ, where J is the Jacobian. Least squares minimizes a specified weighted quadratic residual under an adopted data model.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="theory" title="Derivation, proof and limitations" layer="theory" fullOnly>

For small perturbations δx, use δy≈Jδx. Taking covariances yields $\Sigma_y\approx J\Sigma_xJ^T$. Correlated inputs contribute off-diagonal terms. The approximation can fail for large uncertainty, strong nonlinearity, boundaries or multimodal distributions; direct propagation or a full likelihood may be required.

For observations y and model f(θ), Gaussian errors with known positive-definite covariance Σ give negative log likelihood, up to constants, $\tfrac12r^T\Sigma^{-1}r$ where r=y−f(θ). Independent unequal errors yield the usual weighted sum ∑ri²/σi². If covariance depends on parameters, its log-determinant term is not a disposable constant.

For a locally linear identifiable model with fixed covariance, a common parameter covariance approximation is $(J^T\Sigma^{-1}J)^{-1}$ when the matrix is invertible and the likelihood assumptions hold. Near degeneracy produces large or correlated uncertainties. An apparently small residual does not establish a unique parameter vector.

Residual plots should examine structure against time, wavelength, predictors and fitted value. A reduced chi-square near one is meaningful only under the error model and appropriate effective degrees of freedom; it does not independently prove correct physics. Outlier clipping after seeing residuals changes the procedure and may bias an inference unless modeled or assessed.

For y=ab with independent small uncertainties, fractional variances add. If a and b share a calibration, covariance can reinforce or cancel their errors depending on the derived quantity. A common additive bias does not shrink as 1/√n when measurements are repeated. Report calibration, model and sampling contributions rather than compressing all uncertainty into one unexplained error bar.


### Derivation and transfer

For independent measurements with known variances, inverse-variance weights give the minimum-variance linear unbiased mean. With covariance, generalized least squares uses the inverse covariance matrix instead; ignoring shared calibration errors falsely inflates precision.

The display gives the uncertainty of combining two independent measurements, not a fitted residual distribution. A small residual alone does not certify a model: examine systematic structure, degrees of freedom and the error assumptions. Propagation by linearization can fail for strong nonlinearities or large uncertainties.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="worked" title="A worked argument" layer="worked" fullOnly>

For independent a=2±0.1 and b=3±0.2 with small standard uncertainties, y=ab=6. Its propagated variance is b²σa²+a²σb²=9×0.01+4×0.04=0.25, giving standard uncertainty 0.5. Correlation would add 2ab Cov(a,b).
