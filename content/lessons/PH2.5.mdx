<LessonSection id="story" title="Why the question matters" layer="story">

Canonical transformations preserve the symplectic structure underlying Hamiltonian dynamics. Ordinary invertibility is insufficient; the position–momentum pairing must remain compatible.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="intuition" title="Build a mental model" layer="intuition">

A coordinate map can stretch q and p while preserving their paired area, or can distort that structure. Only the first kind is canonical under the appropriate condition.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="concept" title="Definitions and hypotheses" layer="concept">

The canonical Poisson bracket is $\{f,g\}=\sum_i(f_{q_i}g_{p_i}-f_{p_i}g_{q_i})$. A canonical transformation preserves the fundamental brackets of the new coordinates and momenta.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="theory" title="Derivation, proof and limitations" layer="theory" fullOnly>

For a smooth observable f, Hamilton’s equations imply $df/dt=\{f,H\}+\partial f/\partial t$. With no explicit time dependence, a vanishing bracket with H is the conservation condition. Brackets are antisymmetric, obey the product rule and satisfy the Jacobi identity, making them more structured than an arbitrary derivative notation.

New coordinates Q,P are canonical when their fundamental brackets satisfy $\{Q_i,Q_j\}=0$, $\{P_i,P_j\}=0$ and $\{Q_i,P_j\}=\delta_{ij}$ in the old variables under the appropriate local nonsingularity assumptions. In one dimension Q=aq,P=p/a for nonzero constant a is canonical; Q=aq,P=ap generally is not unless a²=1.

A type-2 generating function F₂(q,P,t) gives p=∂F₂/∂q and Q=∂F₂/∂P, with transformed Hamiltonian K=H+∂F₂/∂t after the coordinate substitution. Different generating-function types use different independent variables and signs; copying one type’s rule into another creates errors.

For angular momentum, Poisson brackets encode its role as a rotation generator, with $\{L_i,L_j\}=\epsilon_{ijk}L_k$ under the standard Cartesian convention. A conserved generator corresponds to a continuous canonical symmetry when its bracket with H vanishes and time dependence is handled consistently.

Global topology and coordinate singularities can prevent one generating function from covering an entire phase space. A local canonical chart remains useful without claiming it is a universal global parameterization. Numerical transformations should be tested against brackets or the symplectic matrix condition, not only against invertibility.


### Variational derivation and transfer case

The Poisson bracket is $\{f,g\}=\sum_i(f_{q_i}g_{p_i}-f_{p_i}g_{q_i})$. Canonical coordinates preserve the bracket relations, or equivalently the symplectic form. Unit Jacobian determinant is necessary but, for two or more degrees of freedom, not sufficient: scaling one canonical pair's area by 2 and another by 1/2 preserves total volume while violating their separate brackets.

For a type-2 generator, matching the differential of $F_2(q,P,t)$ gives $p=F_{2,q}$, $Q=F_{2,P}$ and $K=H+F_{2,t}$. The shear generated by $F_2=qP+\alpha q^2/2$ has $Q=q$, $P=p-\alpha q$ and preserves $dQ\wedge dP=dq\wedge dp$. Explicit time dependence must be retained in the transformed Hamiltonian rather than silently discarded.

</LessonSection>
