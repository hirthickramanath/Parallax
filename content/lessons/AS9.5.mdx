<LessonSection id="story" title="Why the question matters" layer="story">

Perturbation growth depends on component pressure, expansion and scale. Linear equations cannot be extrapolated unchanged into collapsed haloes.

</LessonSection>

Density contrast δ=(ρ−ρ_bar)/ρ_bar measures a perturbation relative to the background in a stated slicing/gauge convention.

<LessonSection id="theory" title="Derivation, proof and limitations" layer="theory" fullOnly>

For pressureless subhorizon linear perturbations in a suitable Newtonian regime, $\ddot\delta+2H\dot\delta-4\pi G\bar\rho\delta=0$. Expansion damps peculiar motion while gravity amplifies overdensities. Radiation, pressure and relativistic horizon-scale effects require extended equations and explicit gauge conventions.

A linear growth factor D(t) describes a mode family only when scale dependence can be neglected in the admitted model. Matter-radiation transfer, neutrino free streaming and baryonic effects can make observed structure scale dependent. Initial amplitude and growth history are not the same parameter.

The power spectrum P(k) describes a two-point statistic with a Fourier normalization and dimensions. Nonlinear collapse couples modes and changes the distribution, so δ≫1 lies outside linear perturbation theory. N-body/hydrodynamic calculations need resolution, force, initial-condition and subgrid validation.

Galaxies are biased tracers of matter. Redshift-space distortions, survey windows and selection alter clustering estimates. Weak lensing probes differently weighted structure and has its own shape/redshift systematics. Compare multiple probes through consistent covariance rather than equating observed galaxy power with total matter power unconditionally.


### Derive, test and transfer

For pressureless subhorizon linear perturbations, gravity competes with expansion damping through $\ddot\delta+2H\dot\delta-4\pi G\rho\delta=0$ under the stated background approximation. In a matter-dominated flat background its growing solution is proportional to a, while a separate decaying mode also exists.

Smallness is essential: when the density contrast approaches order unity, nonlinear mode coupling and collapse invalidate the linear superposition. Radiation pressure and dark energy alter growth, and the observed tracer field has its own bias and selection. The display chooses a pure growing matter-era mode rather than deriving a full late-time matter power spectrum.

</LessonSection>
