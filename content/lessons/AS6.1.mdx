<LessonSection id="concept" title="Definitions and hypotheses" layer="concept">

The interstellar medium contains ionized, atomic and molecular components with different thermodynamic and dynamical conditions; phase labels are approximate descriptions.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="theory" title="Derivation, proof and limitations" layer="theory" fullOnly>

In a simple gas-pressure comparison, $p=nk_BT$. A cold dense region and a hot dilute region can have similar thermal pressures despite very different temperatures. Magnetic, turbulent and cosmic-ray pressures can be comparable, so equal thermal pressure alone does not establish full equilibrium.

A local energy model includes heating Γ and radiative cooling often expressed as n²Λ(T) for an optically thin collisional regime. This scaling is not universal for all densities, ionization states or trapped lines. Thermal balance solves heating=cooling, while stability depends on derivatives under a stated pressure/density constraint.

Ionization, molecular chemistry, dust shielding and external radiation couple phase structure to time-dependent history. A gas may be out of ionization or thermal equilibrium after a shock or rapid heating. Column density and volume density are different quantities, so an integrated sight line can mix several phases.

Emission/absorption lines, continuum and dust maps probe different weighting functions. Filling fraction is not the same as mass fraction: a hot dilute phase can occupy much volume while containing little mass. Retain line excitation, optical-depth and selection assumptions when inferring phase inventories.


### Derive, test and transfer

For thermal gas, $P=nkT$. At fixed thermal pressure, n is inversely proportional to T, so a warm dilute phase and a cold dense phase can have similar pressure. This algebra alone does not prove thermal coexistence or stability: a heat-gain and cooling function must permit an equilibrium and determine its response to perturbations.

Total interstellar stress can include magnetic, turbulent and cosmic-ray components. The particle density in P=nkT is not automatically the hydrogen-nucleus density used in an observational tracer. Ionization and composition change their conversion. The interactive curve holds one thermal-pressure value fixed, allowing a controlled compression/temperature comparison while excluding the full multiphase cooling physics.

</LessonSection>
