Why does orbital distance change a planet’s year more strongly than its circular speed? State a prediction and a reason before reading the argument.

<LessonSection id="story" title="Why the question matters" layer="story">

Planetary orbits connect two-body mechanics with perturbations, tides and resonances; the Solar System is not an exact collection of independent circles.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="intuition" title="Build a mental model" layer="intuition">

Start with the concrete worked example below. Track which quantities belong to the system, which are imposed by its surroundings, and which conclusions require a limiting approximation.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="concept" title="Definitions and hypotheses" layer="concept">

In the Newtonian two-body approximation, semimajor axis and total mass determine period through Kepler’s third law.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="theory" title="Derivation, proof and limitations" layer="theory" fullOnly>

The relative orbit satisfies $P^2=4\pi^2a^3/[G(M+m)]$. Semimajor axis is not the instantaneous star–planet distance for an eccentric orbit. Circular speed is √(GM/r) when the central-mass approximation applies, and local escape speed is √(2GM/r) without additional potentials.

Orbital energy and angular momentum determine eccentricity and orientation in an isolated inverse-square model. Perturbing planets exchange orbital elements and can produce resonances or long-term secular evolution. A near-rational period ratio is suggestive but not by itself proof of resonant libration.

Tidal forces depend on spatial gradients of gravity and material response. Dissipation can synchronize spin or alter eccentricity while exchanging angular momentum between orbit and rotation. A tide is not simply the total gravitational pull at the planet’s centre.

Thermal histories, impacts and composition also shape planetary systems. A point-mass orbit model cannot predict geology or climate by itself. Numerical long-term trajectories need step-size and conserved-quantity checks, and chaotic sensitivity can limit precise far-future orbital phases.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="worked" title="A worked argument" layer="worked" fullOnly>

At fixed central mass, increasing semimajor axis by four increases period by eight. In a circular central potential the escape-to-orbital speed ratio is √2. Those ratios do not imply a four-times-more-distant eccentric planet always has one instantaneous speed.

</LessonSection>
