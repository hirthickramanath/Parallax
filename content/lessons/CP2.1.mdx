<LessonSection id="story" title="Why the question matters" layer="story">

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="concept" title="Definitions and hypotheses" layer="concept">

A finite-difference method replaces derivatives by grid stencils. Consistency, stability and convergence are distinct properties of a stated scheme and problem.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="theory" title="Derivation, proof and limitations" layer="theory" fullOnly>

For uₜ=D uₓₓ on a uniform one-dimensional grid, forward Euler and a centred second difference give $u_j^{n+1}=u_j^n+r(u_{j+1}^n-2u_j^n+u_{j-1}^n)$ with r=DΔt/Δx². Taylor expansion gives first-order time and second-order space consistency for smooth solutions. Holding Δt fixed while halving Δx multiplies r by four.

A Fourier mode has amplification G=1−4r sin²(θ/2); requiring |G|≤1 for every grid mode yields 0≤r≤1/2 for this problem. This is not the stability bound for every PDE or every grid. A centred explicit wave scheme instead has its Courant ratio cΔt/Δx. Boundary closures and multidimensional stencils can change the constraint.

Backward Euler solves a linear system at each step and is unconditionally stable for the standard linear diffusion model, but that does not make arbitrarily large steps accurate. Crank–Nicolson is second order in time yet can retain oscillatory high-frequency transients. Solve residuals, matrix conditioning, boundary enforcement and time-step error must all be monitored.

Use an exact mode u=sin(πx/L)exp[−D(π/L)²t] for verification. Refine grids with a consistent time-step strategy and compute an explicit norm of the error at the same physical time. A small solver residual measures the discrete equation, not its agreement with the continuum. Conservation tests apply only to boundary conditions where the corresponding quantity should be conserved. Label the Fourier-mode bench’s analytic curves separately from an actual finite-difference calculation.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="worked" title="A worked argument" layer="worked" fullOnly>

With D=1, Δx=.2 and Δt=.01, r=.25. Halving Δx without changing Δt gives r=1 and violates the explicit diffusion bound. Reducing Δt by four restores r=.25. This corrects stability but still requires convergence checks for accuracy.

</LessonSection>
