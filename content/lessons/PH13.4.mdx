<LessonSection id="concept" title="Definitions and hypotheses" layer="concept">

The Standard Model is a quantum field theory with specified gauge representations, matter multiplets and interactions, conventionally summarized by SU(3)×SU(2)×U(1) with global-structure qualifications.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="theory" title="Derivation, proof and limitations" layer="theory" fullOnly>

Quarks carry colour and participate in strong interactions, while leptons have different gauge representations. Electric charge is related to electroweak generators by a convention such as $Q=T_3+Y$; another hypercharge normalization uses Y/2, so compare definitions before assigning numbers.

Gauge bosons, fermions and the scalar Higgs sector transform differently. Antiparticles have conjugate charges/representations with the appropriate field interpretation. Quark confinement means an isolated free-quark detector trajectory is not the generic observed hadronic final state.

Three matter generations repeat representation patterns with different masses/mixing. Neutrino oscillations establish physics requiring masses/mixing beyond the simplest original massless-neutrino formulation, and gravity is not included as a complete quantum interaction in the Standard Model.

Masses and coupling parameters need measurement/renormalization definitions; a table of values without scale/convention is incomplete. Dark matter, baryon asymmetry and quantum gravity remain open problems. No single chart should be presented as explaining all cosmic content.


### Derivation and transfer

Conserved energy-momentum constrains particle production thresholds, but quantum numbers and interaction channels determine whether a reaction is allowed and how strongly it occurs. Pair production requires enough invariant energy for both masses, with other conservation requirements respected.

The display shows a two-body equal-mass center-of-momentum kinetic budget. It is not a Standard Model amplitude or a rule that a single free photon can create a massive pair in vacuum without another participant.

</LessonSection>
