<LessonSection id="story" title="Why this matters" layer="story">

Alternating circuits expose the importance of phase, while Maxwell’s displacement current connects changing electric fields with electromagnetic propagation. A current in a lead need not mean charges crossed the capacitor dielectric.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="intuition" title="Build the picture" layer="intuition">

In a capacitor the current leads voltage because it depends on the voltage’s rate of change. An inductor resists rapid current change, producing the opposite phase relation.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="concept" title="State the model" layer="concept">

For sinusoidal steady states, phasors represent amplitudes and relative phases. They do not replace transient initial conditions.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="theory" title="Derive and qualify the result" layer="theory" fullOnly>

For an ideal capacitor, $I=C\,dV/dt$. With angular frequency ω and phasor convention $e^{i\omega t}$, $Z_C=1/(i\omega C)$. For an ideal inductor V=L dI/dt and $Z_L=i\omega L$. A resistor has real impedance R. In a series RLC circuit, $Z=R+i(\omega L-1/(\omega C))$ and $|I|=|V|/|Z|$.

The ideal series resonance occurs at $\omega_0=1/\sqrt{LC}$ where reactive terms cancel. Finite R limits current. Real components have parasitic capacitance, resistance, radiation and nonlinearity, so unlimited resonant amplification is not physical. Energy oscillates between capacitor electric energy and inductor magnetic energy.

For sinusoidal voltage and current, mean real power is $V_{\rm rms}I_{\rm rms}\cos\phi$. RMS values of a pure sine are peak values divided by √2. An ideal capacitor or inductor has zero average real power in steady periodic operation, although instantaneous energy transfers occur. RMS current is relevant to resistive heating even when average signed current is zero.

Maxwell's corrected Ampère law includes displacement current density ∂D/∂t. In vacuum, combining it with Faraday's law produces transverse waves with $c=1/\sqrt{\mu_0\epsilon_0}$. For a plane wave, E and B are perpendicular and E=cB. Energy flux is $\mathbf S=\mathbf E\times\mathbf B/\mu_0$.

These vacuum relations change in matter: phase speed, dispersion and absorption require material response. A lumped-element circuit model also fails when the circuit size becomes comparable to wavelength; propagation delay then needs transmission-line or field treatment.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="worked" title="A worked example" layer="worked" fullOnly>

For peak sinusoidal voltage 10 V across a 5 Ω resistor, Vrms=10/√2 V. Mean power is Vrms²/R=10 W, not 20 W. At ω=1000 rad/s, an ideal 100 μF capacitor has reactance magnitude 10 Ω.
