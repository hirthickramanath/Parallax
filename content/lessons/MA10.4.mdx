<LessonSection id="concept" title="Definitions and hypotheses" layer="concept">

A linear representation is a homomorphism from a group to invertible operators on a vector space. An invariant subspace is preserved by every represented group element.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="theory" title="Derivation, proof and limitations" layer="theory" fullOnly>

A representation satisfies $D(gh)=D(g)D(h)$. Equivalent representations are related by one invertible basis change, not by changing each matrix independently. A reducible representation has a proper nonzero invariant subspace; an invariant complementary subspace is an additional issue in general settings.

For a finite group over complex vector spaces, averaging an inner product over the group produces an invariant positive inner product. Orthogonal complements of invariant subspaces are then invariant, giving complete reducibility. This argument relies on finite-group/characteristic-zero assumptions and cannot be copied to every noncompact infinite group without qualifications.

The character χ(g)=TrD(g) is basis invariant and constant on conjugacy classes. Finite-dimensional complex finite-group irreducible characters obey an orthogonality relation with a normalized sum over G. Characters can decompose such representations, but do not universally classify arbitrary infinite-dimensional nonunitary representations.

If a Hamiltonian commutes with every symmetry operator, it preserves symmetry sectors. Schur’s lemma constrains operators intertwining irreducible complex representations under its hypotheses. Degeneracy follows in the appropriate irreducible sector, while accidental degeneracy and additional labels can remain. An allowed matrix element still needs its actual operator and states.


### Derivation and transfer

A representation maps group elements to invertible linear maps while preserving products. Its character is the trace, invariant under a change of basis. For a planar rotation, trace R(θ)=2cosθ and determinant one.

Equal character values for selected elements do not prove arbitrary representations equivalent without the appropriate group and completeness assumptions. The display is a real two-dimensional rotation representation, not a decomposition algorithm for all groups.

</LessonSection>
