<LessonSection id="story" title="Why the question matters" layer="story">

Kinetic equations and fluctuation-response relations connect microscopic motion to transport, with assumptions that distinguish equilibrium from driven matter.

<LessonSection id="concept" title="Definitions and hypotheses" layer="concept">

The Boltzmann equation evolves a one-particle phase-space distribution through streaming, forces and a modeled collision integral. Equilibrium fluctuation-dissipation relations connect specified correlations to linear response.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="theory" title="Derivation, proof and limitations" layer="theory" fullOnly>

For a dilute classical gas the kinetic equation is $\partial_t f+\mathbf v\cdot\nabla_x f+(\mathbf F/m)\cdot\nabla_v f=C[f]$. Its collision term uses a scattering model and a molecular-chaos approximation for incoming particles. Integrating with collision invariants yields number, momentum and energy balances when the modeled collisions conserve those quantities. Closing moment equations requires further constitutive assumptions.

For suitable elastic-collision kernels and boundary conditions, the H theorem gives nonincreasing ∫f ln f from the collision contribution. This is a statement within the kinetic approximation, not a direct proof that time-reversible microscopic equations violate reversibility. Correlations neglected by molecular chaos and chosen initial conditions matter. Boundary-driven or forced states can maintain entropy production and nonthermal distributions.

For one-dimensional overdamped Brownian motion, dx=μF dt+√(2D)dW in a classical equilibrium bath. With mobility defined as velocity per applied force, $D=\mu k_BT$ is the Einstein relation. It ensures the correct equilibrium density under a conservative force with suitable boundaries. A mobility defined with another convention, such as electrical mobility, changes the parameter conversion.

A Green–Kubo expression relates diffusion to the equilibrium velocity autocorrelation: D=∫₀∞⟨v_x(t)v_x(0)⟩dt when the integral and diffusive long-time limit exist. More general fluctuation-dissipation formulas depend on the observable, conjugate perturbation and spectral convention; quantum versions differ from classical limits. Strong driving, aging and nonstationary systems do not automatically obey the equilibrium relation. A numerical correlation must include sampling, truncation and uncertainty analysis before it becomes a transport coefficient.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="worked" title="A worked argument" layer="worked" fullOnly>

With mobility μ=2 in consistent units and k_BT=3, D=6. A one-dimensional free diffusive displacement then has variance 2Dt=24 at t=2. This variance is not the squared mean displacement, which remains zero in the unbiased equilibrium example.

</LessonSection>
