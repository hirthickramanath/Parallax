<LessonSection id="story" title="Why the question matters" layer="story">

Free scalar quantization connects canonical algebra, particle excitations and vacuum fluctuations under a normalization convention.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="intuition" title="Build a mental model" layer="intuition">

<LessonSection id="concept" title="Definitions and hypotheses" layer="concept">

At equal time a canonical scalar field and momentum obey [φ(x),π(y)]=iδ³(x−y) in ℏ=1 units, interpreted distributionally.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="theory" title="Derivation, proof and limitations" layer="theory" fullOnly>

Expand the free field in positive/negative-frequency modes with creation and annihilation operators. Normalizations are chosen so $[a_{\mathbf k},a^\dagger_{\mathbf q}]$ equals the chosen momentum delta measure. Factors of 2ω and (2π)³ depend on convention; using one mode measure with another commutator changes predictions.

Each free mode contributes oscillator excitations with energy ω_k=√(k²+m²) in natural units. Normal ordering subtracts a chosen vacuum constant in this setting but does not by itself resolve gravitational vacuum-energy questions or every interacting divergence.

Correlation functions describe amplitudes and fluctuations; a propagator is a distribution with boundary/time-ordering prescription. A time-ordered propagator is not identical to a retarded classical response kernel, though they are related constructions.

Local products and coincident correlations require regularization and renormalized definitions. Finite-volume and lattice cutoffs make computations controllable but introduce regulator dependence that must be assessed. The free construction is not a full solution of an interacting scalar theory.

### Fix one normalization and derive its oscillator algebra

For a real free field in a periodic spatial box of volume $V$, use discrete momenta and

$$\phi(t,\mathbf x)=\sum_{\mathbf k}\frac{a_{\mathbf k}e^{-i\omega_kt+i\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf x}+a^\dagger_{\mathbf k}e^{i\omega_kt-i\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf x}}{\sqrt{2V\omega_k}},\qquad [a_{\mathbf k},a^\dagger_{\mathbf q}]=\delta_{\mathbf k\mathbf q}.$$

Here $\omega_k=\sqrt{|\mathbf k|^2+m^2}$ and $\pi=\dot\phi$. Substituting both expansions into the equal-time commutator gives $iV^{-1}\sum_{\mathbf k}e^{i\mathbf k\cdot(\mathbf x-\mathbf y)}$, the periodic delta distribution. The two positive contributions each supply half the result. With a momentum cutoff it is a truncated delta kernel, not an exact point delta.

Substitution into the quadratic Hamiltonian gives $H=\sum_{\mathbf k}\omega_k(a^\dagger_{\mathbf k}a_{\mathbf k}+1/2)$. Normal ordering replaces it by the excitation Hamiltonian. The expansion requires a positive mode frequency: a massless zero mode needs separate treatment rather than division by zero.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="worked" title="A worked argument" layer="worked" fullOnly>

For m=3 and |k|=4, a free quantum has ω=5. Two quanta in that same free mode contribute excitation energy 10 above the chosen vacuum reference. Bose occupation allows this without treating them as labelled distinguishable particles.

For one oscillator with frequency 5, a state with occupation three has total oscillator energy $5(3+1/2)=17.5$ and excitation energy 15. These are different reference conventions for the same level spacing. The vacuum term for this one oscillator is 2.5; summing it over an unbounded field spectrum is not a finite operation justified by this example.

</LessonSection>
