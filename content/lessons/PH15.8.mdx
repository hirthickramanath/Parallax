<LessonSection id="theory" title="Derivation, proof and limitations" layer="theory" fullOnly>

A simple illustrative potential is $V(\phi)=-\mu^2|\phi|^2+\lambda|\phi|^4$ with λ>0. Its minima satisfy |φ|²=μ²/(2λ) in this convention. Different real-field/v normalization conventions change displayed factors and must be tracked.

Coupling the scalar covariant derivative to gauge fields produces gauge-boson mass terms in the expanded description while retaining the underlying gauge-invariant structure. Gauge redundancy is not an ordinary observable global symmetry that is broken in exactly the same sense as a ferromagnet’s physical orientation.

In the electroweak model, W/Z become massive while an electromagnetic combination remains massless. Fermion Yukawa couplings generate corresponding mass parameters after the scalar background, with their values requiring inputs. The mechanism does not predict every fermion mass from one universal number.

A physical scalar excitation and its measured couplings test the model, while vacuum stability, hierarchy and cosmological implications require scale-dependent higher-order assumptions. An introductory Mexican-hat drawing does not establish a complete thermal history or a unique ultraviolet completion.


### Derivation and transfer

For the real radial potential V=λ(φ²−v²)²/4, minima are at ±v and the curvature there is 2λv². The radial small-oscillation mass scale is sqrt(2λ)v in the stated canonical units. A gauge Higgs mechanism also involves gauge fields and degrees-of-freedom accounting.

The display isolates this scalar curvature benchmark. It does not identify the two signs as distinct observable vacua in every gauge theory or derive the whole electroweak spectrum from one curve.

</LessonSection>
