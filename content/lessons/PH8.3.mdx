<LessonSection id="concept" title="Definitions and hypotheses" layer="concept">

A normalized pure state is a ray in Hilbert space. Observables are self-adjoint operators with spectral probabilities; a basis changes the representation, not the state.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="theory" title="Derivation, proof and limitations" layer="theory" fullOnly>

For an orthonormal discrete basis |n⟩, write $|\psi\rangle=\sum_nc_n|n\rangle$ with ∑|cₙ|²=1. A projection-valued measurement with projectors P_a has probability ⟨ψ|P_a|ψ⟩. Degenerate outcomes project onto a subspace rather than automatically onto one uniquely chosen basis vector within it.

Expectation is ⟨A⟩=⟨ψ|A|ψ⟩ and variance is ⟨A²⟩−⟨A⟩² when these quantities exist. For operators with appropriate domains, Robertson’s inequality is $\Delta A\Delta B\ge\tfrac12|\langle[A,B]\rangle|$. Commuting self-adjoint observables require suitable spectral/domain compatibility for unrestricted simultaneous-measurement statements in infinite dimensions.

An ideal projective measurement has a state-update rule conditioned on its outcome. More general measurements use positive-operator-valued effects and need additional information to specify the state update. It is therefore insufficient to give outcome probabilities and assume a unique physical disturbance mechanism.

Mixed states are described by a positive trace-one density operator ρ, with p(a)=Tr(ρP_a). A mixed state is not merely a different global phase of one pure state. A composite system can be entangled, and tracing over one factor produces a reduced mixed state even when the joint state is pure. No basis change alone removes genuine entanglement across a fixed subsystem division.


### Derivation and transfer

For an orthonormal measurement basis, Born probabilities are squared amplitude magnitudes and sum to one for a normalized state. The state cosθ|0>+sinθ|1> has P1=sin²θ. A relative phase affects measurements in another basis even if these two populations stay fixed.

The display computes a pure-state population and does not sample random outcomes. An observable needs a self-adjoint operator and a measurement prescription; multiplying arbitrary coefficients without normalization does not define probabilities.

</LessonSection>
