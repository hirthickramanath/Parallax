<LessonSection id="story" title="Why the question matters" layer="story">

Astronomy software carries units, frames, epochs and quality flags. A table of plausible coordinates is not enough to establish a reproducible scientific sample.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="intuition" title="Build a mental model" layer="intuition">

Start with the concrete worked example below. Track which quantities belong to the system, which are imposed by its surroundings, and which conclusions require a limiting approximation.

<LessonSection id="concept" title="Definitions and hypotheses" layer="concept">

Astropy supplies unit-aware quantities, coordinate/time transformations and scientific table/FITS tools; astroquery provides interfaces to external astronomical data services whose versions and policies must be recorded.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="theory" title="Derivation, proof and limitations" layer="theory" fullOnly>

Attach units before combining physical quantities. A unit-aware distance divided by time can reject an incompatible operation rather than silently treating parsecs as metres. Unit conversion is not an uncertainty model: correlated calibration and measurement errors still require explicit propagation. Record whether a quantity is a flux density, integrated flux, magnitude or instrumental count.

For a constant small-angle proper motion, $\Delta\theta=\mu\Delta t$ within that approximation. A coordinate needs a frame and often an epoch and proper-motion model. ICRS, Galactic and instrument coordinates are not interchangeable angle columns. Matching directions at different epochs can fail for high-proper-motion sources; crowded fields need a statistical association model, not only a nearest-neighbour radius. Angular separation and physical separation also require different information.

FITS headers and tables can encode units, calibration and provenance, but missing or outdated metadata must be checked. Masked values, sentinel flags and nondetections are different states. A parallax inversion d=1/ϖ is a nonlinear inference with selection and uncertainty issues; negative noisy parallaxes are not simply negative physical distances to delete without a model.

For remote queries save service, catalogue release, exact query, retrieval date, response metadata and a checksum of the retained result. Respect row limits, pagination and service terms; an incomplete query result can bias a sample. Cache immutable inputs for reproducibility and distinguish local synthetic fixtures from externally retrieved observations. No live survey data are bundled or claimed by this lesson.


### Derivation and transfer

A catalog quantity carries units, reference frame, time convention and uncertainty. For positive high-signal-to-noise parallax in arcseconds, distance in parsecs is approximately 1/p; the nonlinear transform becomes biased and problematic at low signal-to-noise, including negative measured parallaxes.

The display is an ideal unit-conversion benchmark, not an Astropy/astroquery request or a real catalog analysis. A real project must retain query provenance, selection cuts, coordinate frames, masks and uncertainty treatment. Never silently replace a missing or negative measurement with a fabricated positive distance.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="worked" title="A worked argument" layer="worked" fullOnly>

A source moving .2 arcseconds per year shifts 2 arcseconds over ten years under a constant angular proper-motion approximation. A .5-arcsecond cross-match radius can therefore miss it if positions are compared at different epochs without propagation. For a well-measured positive parallax of .05 arcseconds, the elementary reciprocal estimate is 20 parsecs, subject to the inference qualifications.

</LessonSection>
