<LessonSection id="story" title="Why the question matters" layer="story">

CMB anisotropies combine primordial perturbations, plasma evolution, projection and foreground/instrument effects.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="intuition" title="Build a mental model" layer="intuition">

<LessonSection id="concept" title="Definitions and hypotheses" layer="concept">

The CMB temperature field can be expanded in spherical harmonics; a statistical angular spectrum summarizes correlations under stated isotropy and ensemble assumptions.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="theory" title="Derivation, proof and limitations" layer="theory" fullOnly>

Write ΔT/T=∑aℓmYℓm and $C_\ell=\langle|a_{\ell m}|^2\rangle$ in a statistically isotropic model. An observed finite sky supplies one realization, with cosmic variance even before instrument noise. Masks and beams mix or suppress estimated modes and must be modeled.

Before recombination, the coupled photon-baryon fluid supports acoustic oscillations. The observed characteristic angle is approximately θ_s=r_s/D_M when both sound horizon r_s and transverse distance D_M use a consistent comoving convention. Mixing a comoving ruler with physical D_A loses a redshift factor.

Baryon loading, gravitational driving, diffusion damping, optical depth and primordial parameters shape different peak features. Polarization adds independent structure through Thomson scattering geometry. Lensing remaps anisotropies; foreground dust and synchrotron require frequency-dependent separation.

Parameter inference fits a correlated theoretical spectrum with calibration/foreground nuisance parameters and a chosen cosmological model. A good fit constrains that model; it does not independently measure every parameter without degeneracies. A plotted theoretical spectrum is not a newly observed all-sky map.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="worked" title="A worked argument" layer="worked" fullOnly>

If a supplied comoving sound scale is 2 and comoving transverse distance 100, θ_s=.02 radians. Halving that ruler at fixed distance halves the angle. This geometric toy value is not a measurement of the actual acoustic scale.

</LessonSection>
