<LessonSection id="story" title="Why the question matters" layer="story">

Line formation couples atmospheric structure, atomic populations and radiative transfer, creating degeneracies that require calibrated models.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="concept" title="Definitions and hypotheses" layer="concept">

A stellar atmosphere model relates temperature, density, composition and radiation to emergent intensity; effective temperature is defined through total flux, not every local gas temperature.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="theory" title="Derivation, proof and limitations" layer="theory" fullOnly>

For a plane-parallel static LTE atmosphere, the source function equals Bν(T) when the relevant thermal assumptions hold, and transfer along optical depth gives the emergent intensity as a weighted integral. The Eddington–Barbier approximation relates Iν(0,μ) to a source value around τν≈μ for a sufficiently smooth source, not a delta-function formation depth.

Effective temperature satisfies $F=\sigma_{SB}T_{eff}^4$ and L=4πR²F for a spherical radiating surface. A colour temperature or fitted line-excitation temperature need not equal T_eff when opacity, reddening and nonequilibrium effects matter. Limb darkening arises because different angles sample different source-function layers in a stratified atmosphere.

Boltzmann excitation and Saha ionization give level populations only in the appropriate LTE regime with electron density, temperature and partition functions. A line strength also depends on oscillator strength, broadening, saturation and radiative transfer. Equivalent width can increase nonlinearly with abundance; the curve of growth separates regimes under model assumptions.

Non-LTE, 3D motion, magnetic splitting, blends and instrumental response can bias a simplistic abundance fit. Continuum placement and extinction require uncertainties. A spectral match is an inference conditional on an atmosphere/atomic-data model, not a direct count of atoms from one dark pixel. Retain provenance for actual spectra; the lesson provides no new measured stellar observations.


### Derive, test and transfer

Integrating grey plane-parallel radiative equilibrium with the Eddington boundary approximation gives $T^4=(3/4)T_{eff}^4(\tau+2/3)$. At τ=2/3 this temperature equals Teff. At the boundary τ=0 it is finite, reflecting the approximate radiative boundary condition rather than a zero-temperature surface.

A stellar spectrum samples a range of depths because opacity depends on wavelength. A line can form higher or lower than the nearby continuum, so a single photospheric radius and temperature do not describe every feature. LTE connects source functions and populations to local temperature only when collisions and other conditions justify it. The investigation is deliberately grey; using it to infer individual absorption-line strengths would exceed its assumptions.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="worked" title="A worked argument" layer="worked" fullOnly>

If effective temperature doubles at fixed radius, luminosity increases by 16. If radius doubles at fixed effective temperature, luminosity increases by four. Neither ratio implies that a selected absorption-line depth scales by the same factor.
