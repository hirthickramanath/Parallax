<LessonSection id="story" title="Why the question matters" layer="story">

Interior composition, thermal state and atmospheric loss create degeneracies that require multiple observables.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="intuition" title="Build a mental model" layer="intuition">

<LessonSection id="concept" title="Definitions and hypotheses" layer="concept">

A planetary structure model couples gravity, EOS, thermal evolution and composition. A simple atmosphere scale height applies only under its hydrostatic/isothermal assumptions.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="theory" title="Derivation, proof and limitations" layer="theory" fullOnly>

Hydrostatic interiors obey dp/dr=−Gmρ/r² together with mass continuity and an EOS. Different mixtures of rock, metal, ice and gas can produce similar total mass/radius pairs. Temperature, compression and age affect radius, so bulk density is not a unique composition label.

For an isothermal ideal atmosphere with approximately constant gravity, $H=k_BT/(\bar m g)$ and density falls exponentially in the simplified column. Real atmospheres have temperature gradients, chemistry, clouds and variable gravity. The scale height is not a rigid upper boundary.

Escape depends on energy deposition and particle distributions. Jeans escape and hydrodynamic escape are different regimes; stellar irradiation, magnetic interaction and planetary gravity affect loss. One energy-limited scaling is a model with efficiency and geometry assumptions, not a universal exact erosion rate.

Convection, conduction, radiation and phase transitions shape interior evolution and dynamos. Atmospheric retrieval from transmission/emission spectra is conditional on reference radius, temperature, abundances, cloud and instrument models. A narrow spectral feature does not uniquely map to a global inventory without those assumptions.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="interactive" title="Investigate the claim" layer="interactive">

Compare synthetic mass-radius models with different compositions and add an atmosphere. Calculate scale heights at stated conditions, then vary temperature and mean molecular mass separately to expose retrieval degeneracy.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="worked" title="A worked argument" layer="worked" fullOnly>

With k_BT=6, mean particle mass 2 and g=3 in consistent units, H=1. Halving gravity at fixed temperature/composition doubles H. A larger scale height changes an atmospheric signal but does not by itself prove more total atmospheric mass.

</LessonSection>
