<LessonSection id="concept" title="Definitions and hypotheses" layer="concept">

In a periodic single-particle potential, Bloch states have a plane-wave factor times a lattice-periodic function. Bands and gaps arise from the periodic electronic problem and occupancy.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="theory" title="Derivation, proof and limitations" layer="theory" fullOnly>

A periodic lattice permits $\psi_{n\mathbf k}(\mathbf r)=e^{i\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf r}u_{n\mathbf k}(\mathbf r)$ in the ideal translation-symmetric description. Band index n and crystal wavevector label states; crystal momentum is not identical to an isolated particle’s mechanical momentum in every setting.

A completely filled band separated by a gap can be insulating at zero temperature in the simple independent-electron model because nearby available states and a net response cannot be inferred from delocalization alone. Partially filled bands or overlapping bands commonly yield metallic behaviour, but correlation, disorder and topology can require a more sophisticated classification.

Group velocity is (1/ℏ)∇_kE_n and local band curvature defines an effective mass tensor where a quadratic expansion is appropriate. Effective mass is a response parameter, not a literal change of bare electron rest mass. Holes describe missing occupations near filled bands and can act as positive-charge carriers in an effective model.

At finite temperature, intrinsic semiconductor carrier populations depend exponentially on gap scales in a suitable nondegenerate regime, while dopants shift occupations and introduce states. Conductivity also requires mobilities and scattering, not only carrier number. Tight-binding, nearly-free-electron and density-functional models have distinct approximations; Mott insulation illustrates why filling alone is not universally sufficient.


### Derive, test and transfer

In a one-orbital nearest-neighbor tight-binding chain, a simple band is E(k)=ε−2tcos(ka). Its width is 4|t|, connecting orbital hopping to energy dispersion. The periodic k dependence also shows why one unit-cell representation should not be confused with free-particle quadratic energy at all wave numbers.

Metallicity depends on filling, band crossings, interactions and symmetry, not just the presence of a plotted band. Finite-temperature carriers and scattering control transport. The display chooses ε=0 and dimensionless ka; it neither computes a material's band structure nor guarantees conduction from the band shape alone.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="worked" title="A worked argument" layer="worked" fullOnly>
