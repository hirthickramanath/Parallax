<LessonSection id="story" title="Why the question matters" layer="story">

Polarization separates free charge from bound charge in matter. Auxiliary fields can simplify equations, but their source conventions must remain explicit.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="intuition" title="Build a mental model" layer="intuition">

Molecular dipoles redistribute charge slightly. Their averaged bound charges partly alter the field produced by the externally supplied free charges.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="concept" title="Definitions and hypotheses" layer="concept">

Polarization P is dipole moment per volume. In a macroscopic electrostatic description, bound volume charge is −∇·P and D=ε₀E+P obeys ∇·D=ρfree.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="theory" title="Derivation, proof and limitations" layer="theory" fullOnly>

For a polarized body, bound volume charge is $\rho_b=-\nabla\cdot P$ and bound surface charge is $\sigma_b=P\cdot n$ at a boundary with exterior polarization zero. These follow by summing dipole contributions and converting the total divergence. Uniform P gives zero volume bound charge but can still give nonzero surface bound charge.

For a linear homogeneous isotropic dielectric, P=ε₀χeE, giving $D=\epsilon E$ with ε=ε₀(1+χe). This is a constitutive approximation, not a universal identity for anisotropic, nonlinear, dispersive or strongly inhomogeneous matter. In an anisotropic medium, permittivity is generally a tensor.

A pillbox at an interface gives $n\cdot(D_2-D_1)=\sigma_{\rm free}$, with n from medium 1 to 2. Electrostatic circulation gives continuity of tangential E in the absence of a singular non-electrostatic layer. Normal E can jump even when no free surface charge is present if permittivity changes. The ordinary total-charge Gauss law still holds for E.

For a parallel-plate capacitor fully filled by a uniform dielectric with relative permittivity εr, capacitance increases by εr in the negligible-fringing model. If isolated charge is fixed, voltage and stored field energy decrease by 1/εr. If attached voltage is fixed, charge increases by εr and field energy increases; mechanical work must include the source exchange.

For a reversible linear nondispersive medium under appropriate conditions, electrostatic energy density is E·D/2. Real dielectric losses, hysteresis and frequency dependence require additional material and energy accounting. A macroscopic constitutive law cannot be extrapolated to arbitrarily strong fields or down to a molecular point without checking its averaging assumptions.


### Derive, test and transfer

Macroscopic polarization P is dipole moment per volume. For a linear homogeneous isotropic dielectric, P=ε₀χE and D=ε₀E+P=εE under the stated constitutive model. Gauss' law for D counts free charge, whereas the total electric field responds to both free and bound charges.

Bound charge follows ρb=−div P and σb=P dot n at an interface. A uniform polarization can have zero bulk bound density while retaining opposite bound surface charges. Relative permittivity is not automatically one scalar at every frequency or field strength: anisotropy, dispersion, loss and nonlinear response require more structure. The investigation holds the simple linear constitutive relation fixed while varying its inputs.

</LessonSection>
