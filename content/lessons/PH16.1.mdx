<LessonSection id="puzzle" title="Commit to a prediction" layer="puzzle">

Why can gravity disappear locally from one frame without tidal effects vanishing? State a prediction and a reason before reading the argument.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="story" title="Why the question matters" layer="story">

The equivalence principle relates free fall and local inertial physics, while curvature distinguishes gravity across a finite region.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="intuition" title="Build a mental model" layer="intuition">

Start with the concrete worked example below. Track which quantities belong to the system, which are imposed by its surroundings, and which conclusions require a limiting approximation.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="concept" title="Definitions and hypotheses" layer="concept">

A freely falling local frame can remove suitable connection coefficients at a point; curvature and finite-region tidal observables need not vanish.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="theory" title="Derivation, proof and limitations" layer="theory" fullOnly>

For timelike free fall in an appropriate test-body regime, $u^\nu\nabla_\nu u^\mu=0$. Coordinate acceleration can be nonzero in a chosen chart even though an ideal accelerometer measures no nongravitational proper acceleration.

A metric defines spacetime intervals and light cones. Signature and coordinates must be stated; a coordinate speed of light need not equal the locally measured c in arbitrary coordinates. The local equivalence statement is not a global transformation removing every gravitational field.

Tidal separation depends on curvature through geodesic deviation. A sufficiently extended apparatus can detect relative acceleration between nearby freely falling bodies. The principle does not say all finite gravitational effects are indistinguishable from uniform acceleration in flat spacetime.

Test universality and coupling assumptions need experimental context. Charged bodies, pressure forces and self-gravity can depart from ideal test-geodesic motion. Spacetime geometry plus matter dynamics and initial/boundary conditions are needed for predictions, not the equivalence principle alone.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="worked" title="A worked argument" layer="worked" fullOnly>

If a supplied local tidal coefficient maps separation ξ=2 to relative acceleration −6, its coefficient is −3 in consistent units. Doubling ξ doubles that leading small-separation response, not every finite separation effect.

</LessonSection>
