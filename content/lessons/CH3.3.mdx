<LessonSection id="theory" title="Derivation, proof and limitations" layer="theory" fullOnly>

For small line-of-sight speed in an explicitly chosen convention, $\Delta\nu/\nu_0\approx-v/c$ for receding motion. Relativistic/cosmological cases need the appropriate full relation. Rest-frequency uncertainties and reference frames can compete with inferred small velocity shifts.

Rotational level populations depend on temperature/density and radiative/collisional excitation. LTE population diagrams have assumptions about optical depth, filling fraction and one temperature. Non-LTE modelling needs transition and collision data with provenance.

Line blending, hyperfine structure, isotopologues and instrumental resolution can mimic or hide a feature. Multiple expected lines, relative strengths and nondetections constrain an assignment. Interferometric filtering and beam dilution change apparent emission spatially.

A column density estimate adds partition functions and transfer assumptions; it is not the same as a line’s raw peak. Public line catalogues must be versioned and checked. This tutorial supplies synthetic line examples and claims no new interstellar molecule.


### Derive, test and transfer

For a rigid linear rotor, neighboring transition frequencies scale as 2B(J+1) when B is expressed in frequency units. Molecular identity inference uses several line positions and relative strengths, not a single approximately matching frequency. Isotopic changes and hyperfine structure can add recognizable patterns.

Observed frequencies also include redshift and local velocity corrections. Cosmological νobs=νrest/(1+z) is distinct from a first-order local Doppler shortcut. Optical depth and non-LTE excitation complicate abundance from intensity. The ladder display is a normalized spectral-position model, not a catalog match or a full line-radiative-transfer calculation.

</LessonSection>
