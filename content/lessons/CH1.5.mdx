<LessonSection id="story" title="Why the question matters" layer="story">

Statistical thermodynamics connects microscopic levels with macroscopic averages. Different translational, rotational, vibrational and electronic spacings become thermally active at different temperatures.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="intuition" title="Build a mental model" layer="intuition">

Temperature weights accessible states rather than switching every possible motion on equally. The spacing between energy levels matters as much as the number of coordinates.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="concept" title="Definitions and hypotheses" layer="concept">

For a canonical system at temperature T, the partition function is Z=∑gᵢexp(−βEᵢ), with β=1/(kBT), using consistently counted microstates or degeneracies.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="theory" title="Derivation, proof and limitations" layer="theory" fullOnly>

Canonical probabilities are $p_i=g_i e^{-\beta E_i}/Z$ when level i groups gi degenerate states. Counting each microstate separately instead removes gi. The mean energy follows $U=-\partial\ln Z/\partial\beta$ at fixed external parameters, and Helmholtz free energy is F=−kBT lnZ. Differentiating gives heat capacities in the corresponding ensemble.

For independent distinguishable subsystems the partition functions multiply. A dilute classical gas of N identical noninteracting particles uses Z≈qᴺ/N! under the Maxwell–Boltzmann approximation, with appropriate quantum-state counting. The factor is not a universal replacement for Bose or Fermi statistics at quantum degeneracy.

Molecular q is often approximately factorized into translational, rotational, vibrational and electronic factors when couplings permit. A harmonic vibrational mode has spacing ℏω and partition factor $q_v=e^{-\beta\hbar\omega/2}/(1-e^{-\beta\hbar\omega})$. Zero-point energy contributes an energy offset; heat-capacity changes follow population of excited states rather than merely the existence of the mode.

For a two-level system with energies 0 and ε and equal degeneracy, excited probability is 1/(1+eᵝᵋ). At low T it is suppressed; at high T it approaches 1/2. Unequal degeneracies change that limit. A high-temperature classical equipartition result needs energy spacings small compared with kBT and the right quadratic form.

Energy-zero conventions shift free energies and partition functions consistently; reaction or phase comparisons must retain compatible references. A molecular spectrum can constrain level spacings, while predicting bulk thermodynamics also requires state counts, interactions and ensemble assumptions.


### Derive, test and transfer

A molecular partition function counts translational, rotational, vibrational and electronic states under a specified separability approximation. For an ideal harmonic vibration with its ground energy set to zero, qvib=Σexp(−nθ/T)=1/(1−exp(−θ/T)). Restoring zero-point energy multiplies by a Boltzmann factor and changes absolute energy bookkeeping while leaving relative level populations consistent.

At sufficiently high temperature a simple rigid-rotor factor scales as T/(σθrot), with symmetry number σ preventing overcounting equivalent orientations. Nuclear-spin statistics, anharmonicity and rotation-vibration coupling require refinements. State degeneracy belongs in the sum; a mode frequency alone is not a complete molecular thermodynamic model.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="worked" title="A worked argument" layer="worked" fullOnly>

For a two-level system with ε/(kBT)=ln3 and equal degeneracy, the excited-state probability is 1/(1+3)=0.25. If the upper level has degeneracy 3 and the lower 1 at the same gap, the excited probability instead becomes 3/3 divided by 1+3/3, or 0.5.
