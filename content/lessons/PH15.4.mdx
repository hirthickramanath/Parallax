Why must amplitudes be added before probabilities in a diagram expansion? State a prediction and a reason before reading the argument.

<LessonSection id="story" title="Why the question matters" layer="story">

Perturbative diagrams encode interference, symmetry factors and virtual integrations rather than independent alternative probabilities.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="intuition" title="Build a mental model" layer="intuition">

Start with the concrete worked example below. Track which quantities belong to the system, which are imposed by its surroundings, and which conclusions require a limiting approximation.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="concept" title="Definitions and hypotheses" layer="concept">

A perturbative S-matrix expansion organizes interactions between specified asymptotic states under the theory’s regulator and normalization conventions.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="theory" title="Derivation, proof and limitations" layer="theory" fullOnly>

The Dyson series uses $S=T\exp[-i\int H_I(t)dt]$ in ℏ=1 notation. Wick contractions organize terms into propagators and vertices. The time ordering and combinatorial factors are part of the expansion, not decorations to omit from a sketch.

For indistinguishable paths to one final state, total probability uses |∑M_i|² with interference. Summing |M_i|² is appropriate only after a justified incoherent/orthogonal distinction. Internal lines carry integrated/summed variables and need not obey external on-shell conditions.

Phase space, incident flux, spin sums/averages and identical-particle factors convert amplitudes into rates/cross sections. A diagram’s coupling power identifies an order, but loop integrals can contain divergences and large logarithms. A lowest-order result requires an admitted regime and uncertainty from neglected effects.

Unitarity, symmetry and conservation constrain the expansion. A selected incomplete set of terms can violate gauge invariance or an order-consistent probability balance. Nonperturbative phenomena need methods beyond drawing more unvalidated weak-coupling terms.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="interactive" title="Investigate the claim" layer="interactive">

Compute a two-amplitude interference example and track normalization separately from a phase-space conversion. Identify external and internal variables in a supplied elementary diagram without calling the internal line an observed trajectory.

<LessonSection id="worked" title="A worked argument" layer="worked" fullOnly>

Two contributions M₁=1 and M₂=−1 give total probability scale zero, whereas adding separate squared magnitudes would give two. For M₂=i instead, the total magnitude squared is two.

</LessonSection>
