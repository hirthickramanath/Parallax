<LessonSection id="story" title="Why the question matters" layer="story">

<LessonSection id="concept" title="Definitions and hypotheses" layer="concept">

Angular momentum operators obey [J_i,J_j]=iℏε_ijkJ_k. Joint eigenstates of J² and J_z have labels j,m and eigenvalues ℏ²j(j+1),ℏm.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="theory" title="Derivation, proof and limitations" layer="theory" fullOnly>

Define J±=J_x±iJ_y. Commutators give [J_z,J±]=±ℏJ± and [J²,J±]=0. Norm positivity yields ladder factors $\hbar\sqrt{j(j+1)-m(m\pm1)}$. Highest and lowest states terminate the ladder, so m runs from −j to j in unit steps and 2j is a nonnegative integer.

For orbital L=r×p acting on single-valued scalar spatial wavefunctions, ℓ is a nonnegative integer and the angular functions are spherical harmonics. Intrinsic spin can have half-integer j; it is an independent quantum rotational degree of freedom rather than a literal rigid-body surface speed. The magnitude √(j(j+1))ℏ exceeds the largest component jℏ except for j=0.

Adding two angular momenta produces total J=J₁+J₂ with allowed j from |j₁−j₂| to j₁+j₂ in unit steps. Product and coupled bases are connected by Clebsch–Gordan coefficients. State counts must agree: summing 2j+1 over the coupled sectors equals (2j₁+1)(2j₂+1). A selection rule is a matrix-element statement with an interaction symmetry, not only a table of allowed labels.

For spin half, J_i=ℏσ_i/2 with Pauli matrices. Different components do not have simultaneous sharp values in a general state. Rotation operators include exp(−iθn·J/ℏ), and spinors change sign under a 2π rotation while projective probabilities remain unchanged. Relative phases in composite processes still matter.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="worked" title="A worked argument" layer="worked" fullOnly>

For j=1, J²/ℏ²=2 and there are three m values. Combining two spin-half systems gives a j=1 triplet and j=0 singlet, with 3+1=4 states, matching the two-by-two product space.

</LessonSection>
