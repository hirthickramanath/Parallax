<LessonSection id="story" title="Why the question matters" layer="story">

Interferometers measure differential tidal strain and infer source parameters through relativistic waveform and detector-response models.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="concept" title="Definitions and hypotheses" layer="concept">

Gravitational-wave strain is a dimensionless relative deformation in the appropriate weak-wave detector description; the measured signal includes antenna response, noise and calibration.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="theory" title="Derivation, proof and limitations" layer="theory" fullOnly>

A weak long-wavelength detector estimate is $\Delta L\sim hL$ for a suitably aligned arm, with tensor polarization and geometry setting the exact response. General-relativistic waves are not ordinary pressure waves in a material medium. Gauge coordinates and observable light-travel/differential measurements must be distinguished.

For a slowly evolving leading-order circular compact binary, the chirp mass is $\mathcal M=(m_1m_2)^{3/5}/(m_1+m_2)^{1/5}$. It strongly controls the leading frequency evolution. Mass ratio, spins, eccentricity, distance and orientation add structure and degeneracy; a chirp mass alone does not identify both individual masses.

Matched filtering compares calibrated strain against a waveform family using a noise-weighted inner product. A high match is conditional on signal/noise and model assumptions, not a guarantee of a unique astrophysical interpretation. Nonstationary glitches, trials factors and calibration uncertainty require statistical treatment.

A detector network constrains sky location, polarization and consistency through time delays and response patterns. Distance inference is correlated with inclination and redshift/cosmology. Coalescence-waveform models must cover the regime being used; a Newtonian inspiral formula is not a full merger/ringdown solution. Keep public event data distinct from synthetic tutorial signals.


### Derive, test and transfer

At leading order a quasi-circular binary inspiral has $\dot f\propto\mathcal M^{5/3}f^{11/3}$, where $\mathcal M=(m_1m_2)^{3/5}/(m_1+m_2)^{1/5}$ is the chirp mass. A rising frequency speeds up the chirp sharply, giving a characteristic phase evolution that constrains the mass combination.

Observed masses are redshifted relative to source-frame masses; distance and orientation affect amplitude, while spin and higher-order effects complicate inference. The leading power law fails near merger, where strong-field waveforms are required. A detection is based on calibrated strain and a statistical search, not on a plotted chirp resemblance alone. The investigation compares controlled ratios within the adiabatic approximation rather than generating a detector-ready template.

</LessonSection>
