<LessonSection id="concept" title="Definitions and hypotheses" layer="concept">

A computational mini-project defines species/state, geometry, method, basis, target observables, convergence and independent comparison before drawing conclusions.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="theory" title="Derivation, proof and limitations" layer="theory" fullOnly>

Choose one target such as a diatomic equilibrium curve or isotope frequency ratio. Record charge, spin and Hamiltonian/functional assumptions. If an energy difference is $\Delta E=E_B-E_A$, both energies must share a consistent reference and units.

Scan or optimize geometry with convergence checks and avoid mixing electronic states between points. Verify small known cases and test basis/grid dependence separately from algorithm residual. A smooth fit can hide a state switch or unconverged point.

Compare with a benchmark not used to tune the calculation, including uncertainty and condition differences. A harmonic frequency and a measured anharmonic band centre are different quantities. If no independent data are available, state that limitation rather than inventing an experiment.

Deliver source code/configuration, input/output provenance, plots with units and a concise discrepancy analysis. Protect credentials and irrelevant private files. A completed tutorial calculation is not independent scientific certification, and this lesson provides a rubric rather than claiming a real project was executed.


### Derive, test and transfer

A computational mini-project should specify geometry, Hamiltonian or electronic method, basis, convergence thresholds, units and reference data before interpreting a result. A lower numerical energy across unrelated approximations is not automatically a better value for the same physical problem.

Central finite differences [f(x+h)−f(x−h)]/(2h) have second-order truncation error for sufficiently smooth f, but reducing h indefinitely amplifies floating-point cancellation. The Gaussian variational investigation is an analytic benchmark with a known exact answer, not a completed molecular calculation. A real project must retain input files, software versions, failed convergence attempts and a reproducible comparison target.

</LessonSection>
