<LessonSection id="theory" title="Derivation, proof and limitations" layer="theory" fullOnly>

A foliation writes spacetime in lapse/shift form, and the evolution system depends on formulation and gauge choices. Physical observables must be extracted with those conventions accounted for. Gauge stability is not identical to physical correctness.

Hamiltonian/momentum constraints should hold in the continuum. A diagnostic norm can be written $\|C\|_2=[V^{-1}\int C^2dV]^{1/2}$ with a stated geometric weight. Numerical truncation produces residuals; their convergence and propagation matter more than a single arbitrary small number.

Finite boundaries, mesh refinement and singular-region treatment can contaminate waves. Excision and puncture methods are distinct strategies with conditions, not instructions to delete unknown physical content arbitrarily. Wave extraction at finite radius needs error/control when approximating asymptotic radiation.

Verification uses exact solutions, symmetry limits, resolution studies and conservation/balance checks. Validation against observations adds source/measurement modelling. A simulation is not a new experiment, and expensive computation alone is not evidence of convergence. This introduction defines a reproducible study design rather than claiming a merger calculation was run.


### Derivation and transfer

A numerical relativity calculation must specify formulation, initial constraints, gauge, discretization, boundaries and diagnostics. A convergent discretization error does not by itself show that constraints are satisfied or that the continuum model matches the physical question.

The display uses an exact second-order error benchmark E=C h². It is not an executed Einstein-equation simulation. A real result needs multiple resolutions, constraint monitoring, waveform extraction and archived inputs/software sufficient to reproduce the run.

</LessonSection>
