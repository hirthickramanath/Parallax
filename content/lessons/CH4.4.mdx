<LessonSection id="concept" title="Definitions and hypotheses" layer="concept">

For a Hamiltonian invariant under a molecular symmetry operation, states can be organized into symmetry types; a matrix element vanishes when its transformed integrand lacks the totally symmetric component.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="theory" title="Derivation, proof and limitations" layer="theory" fullOnly>

If an operation R leaves H invariant and two states have opposite reflection parity, then $\langle a|H|b\rangle=-\langle a|H|b\rangle=0$. This elementary example shows why energy similarity alone does not guarantee mixing. For general groups, products of representations and their symmetry content determine the allowed couplings.

Symmetry-adapted linear combinations of equivalent ligand orbitals can be built by applying group operations or projection operators. Only matching symmetry sectors couple in an exact symmetry-preserving model. Within one sector, energy mismatch and overlap still control the strength; allowed does not mean large.

For a two-level same-symmetry Hamiltonian with equal diagonal energy ε and real coupling V, eigenvalues are ε±|V|. Different diagonal energies give an avoided crossing when coupling is nonzero. If exact symmetry forces V=0, states in different sectors can cross. Distortions or external fields can break the symmetry and enable previously forbidden mixing.

Selection rules for spectroscopy use the symmetry of the perturbation operator, such as a dipole component, as well as both states. An electronic transition forbidden in an ideal approximation can acquire weak intensity through vibrations, spin-orbit coupling or symmetry breaking. A qualitative group label does not replace a quantitative intensity or energy calculation.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="worked" title="A worked argument" layer="worked" fullOnly>

Two equal-energy basis states with ε=3 and coupling V=1 have energies 2 and 4, a splitting of 2. If an exact symmetry instead forces V=0, both remain at 3 in this two-state model. A zero matrix element must be justified by the model symmetry rather than assumed from a sketch.

</LessonSection>
