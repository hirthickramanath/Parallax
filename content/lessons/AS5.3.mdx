<LessonSection id="concept" title="Definitions and hypotheses" layer="concept">

An X-ray binary contains a compact accretor with a companion; a gamma-ray burst is a transient phenomenological class with multiple physical channels and beaming-dependent energetics.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="theory" title="Derivation, proof and limitations" layer="theory" fullOnly>

Accretion luminosity can be parameterized $L=\eta\dot Mc^2$, but the partition among disc, hot plasma, surface/boundary emission and jets depends on state and accretor. A neutron-star surface and a black-hole horizon impose different boundary physics; spectral similarities do not erase those differences.

Short variability suggests a causally connected scale roughly R≲cΔt in a simple nonrelativistic source, modified by Doppler factor, geometry and cosmological time dilation. It is a conditional scale constraint, not exact imaging. Absorption and reprocessing can change observed timing and colours.

Gamma-ray burst prompt spectra, afterglows and host context constrain relativistic outflows. Duration classes correlate with channels but are not perfect progenitor labels. Isotropic-equivalent energy assumes emission over 4π; a beaming correction needs a jet geometry and can be uncertain.

High compactness can make pair-production opacity important, motivating relativistic source models under stated spectral assumptions. Shock microphysics, magnetic reconnection and radiation efficiencies remain model dependent. Do not infer a unique compact-object mass, progenitor or radiative process solely from a bright transient light curve.


### Derive, test and transfer

A coherently variable region cannot communicate a change faster than its causal crossing time. For a relativistically moving region at redshift z, $\Delta t_{obs}\sim(1+z)\Delta t'/\delta$. A source size constraint is then $R'\lesssim c\delta\Delta t_{obs}/(1+z)$ under the stated coherence assumptions.

Treating an observed timescale as an exact radius can fail when multiple zones vary, an engine sets the clock, or geometric light-travel effects dominate. GRB compactness arguments additionally combine luminosity, high-energy photons and pair opacity; variability alone does not uniquely determine a Lorentz factor. X-ray binary spectra also require geometry, absorption and emission mechanisms. The investigation separates the timing scale from these additional inferences.

</LessonSection>
