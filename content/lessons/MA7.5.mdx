<LessonSection id="story" title="Why the question matters" layer="story">

Asymptotic methods estimate integrals and differential-equation solutions in regimes where exact expressions are unavailable or hard to interpret. Their validity depends on scale separation and on avoiding unhandled transition points.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="intuition" title="Build a mental model" layer="intuition">

At a large parameter, only a narrow part of an integral may contribute appreciably. Near a turning point, however, the presumed local scale changes and a formerly small correction can dominate.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="concept" title="Definitions and hypotheses" layer="concept">

An asymptotic expansion has a remainder smaller than the last retained scale in a specified limit; it need not converge as an infinite series. Laplace, stationary-phase and WKB approximations use distinct hypotheses and conventions.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="theory" title="Derivation, proof and limitations" layer="theory" fullOnly>

For an interior nondegenerate maximum x₀ of a smooth real φ with φ″(x₀)&lt;0, positive large λ localizes $\int a(x)e^{\lambda\phi(x)}dx$ near x₀ under suitable dominance and integrability assumptions. Expanding the exponent to quadratic order and scaling y=√λ(x−x₀) yields $I(\lambda)\sim a(x_0)e^{\lambda\phi(x_0)}\sqrt{2\pi/[\lambda|\phi''(x_0)|]}$ when a(x₀)≠0. Endpoint maxima, several comparable maxima, a vanishing amplitude or degenerate curvature require modified leading analysis.

For an oscillatory real integral with an interior nondegenerate stationary point, cancellation away from the stationary point gives a leading contribution $a(x_0)e^{i\lambda\phi(x_0)}\sqrt{2\pi/[\lambda|\phi''(x_0)|]}e^{i\pi\operatorname{sgn}(\phi''(x_0))/4}$ under appropriate support/smoothness hypotheses. Endpoints can contribute separately. Multiple stationary points interfere; taking the absolute value of each contribution before summing destroys the phase information.

Complex steepest descent deforms an admissible contour through contributing saddles so the exponential decays away from them along the chosen local directions. The deformation must respect singularities, branches and endpoint sectors. Not every algebraic saddle contributes to a given original contour. Stokes transitions concern changes in asymptotic contributions as parameters vary; an abrupt change in a chosen representation does not necessarily mean the exact function jumps.

For $\varepsilon^2y''+q(x)y=0$ with smooth positive q away from zeros, a leading WKB pair is $q^{-1/4}\exp[\pm i\varepsilon^{-1}\int^x\sqrt q\,ds]$. Substitution fixes the phase derivative squared to q and the amplitude transport to q⁻¹ᐟ⁴. A useful smallness measure is ε|q′|/q³ᐟ²≪1, together with control of higher variations and the domain. For q&lt;0 the leading forms are exponential with |q|; a physical boundary condition chooses the combination.

At a simple turning point q=0 the leading WKB amplitude diverges and the scale test fails. A local Airy-type analysis and justified matching replace that approximation. Boundary quantization and connection phases require the actual endpoint/turning-point configuration; there is no universal rule obtained by dropping the matching step. An asymptotic series can be divergent yet useful when truncated near its smallest term. Fix the parameter regime, compare successive truncations and use available remainder estimates rather than equating a formal expansion with a convergent exact answer.

</LessonSection>
