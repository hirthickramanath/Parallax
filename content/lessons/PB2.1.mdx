<LessonSection id="story" title="Why this matters" layer="story">

Pressure measurements underpin atmospheres, interiors and laboratory flow. A fluid at rest cannot sustain a static shear stress like an elastic solid; its internal stress is isotropic in the simple model.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="intuition" title="Build the picture" layer="intuition">

Imagine balancing a thin horizontal slab of fluid. The bottom pressure must support the top pressure plus the slab’s weight.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="concept" title="State the model" layer="concept">

Pressure is normal force per area. Hydrostatic equilibrium balances a pressure gradient against gravitational body force.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="theory" title="Derive and qualify the result" layer="theory" fullOnly>

Take z positive upward in a stationary fluid with mass density ρ. The vertical forces on area A and thickness dz yield $dp/dz=-\rho g$. For constant density and g, $p=p_0+\rho gh$ at depth h below a surface. Absolute pressure includes p₀; gauge pressure subtracts the chosen ambient reference. Shape does not enter, although wall forces contribute to the balance of an entire container.

Integrating pressure forces over an immersed closed surface gives an upward buoyant force equal to the weight of displaced fluid in uniform gravity. A floating body displaces its own weight, not necessarily its own volume of liquid. Buoyancy depends on the surrounding density; compression and stratification complicate the simple expression.

Mass conservation in steady flow through a tube gives $\rho Av=$ constant. Incompressible flow makes Av constant. Along a streamline, steady inviscid incompressible motion without pumps or dissipative loss gives Bernoulli's relation $p+\rho v^2/2+\rho gz=$ constant. It follows by work-energy balance on a moving fluid element. The relation is not a universal equality between arbitrary points across a viscous turbulent flow.

Viscosity transports momentum and dissipates mechanical energy. For slow fully developed laminar flow in a cylindrical tube, Poiseuille flow scales as $Q=\pi R^4\Delta p/(8\eta L)$ with no-slip walls and a Newtonian fluid. The fourth-power dependence holds only within those assumptions. Reynolds number Re=ρvL/η compares inertia to viscosity; a critical transition depends on geometry and disturbances.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="worked" title="A worked example" layer="worked" fullOnly>

In freshwater with density 1000 kg/m³, depth 2 m and g=9.8 m/s², gauge pressure is 19,600 Pa. The absolute pressure is about 120,900 Pa if the surface is at 101,300 Pa. Confusing gauge and absolute pressure gives an incorrect gas compression calculation.

</LessonSection>
