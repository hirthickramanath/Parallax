<LessonSection id="concept" title="Definitions and hypotheses" layer="concept">

A distribution is a continuous linear functional on a stated test-function space. The delta at a acts by δ_a[φ]=φ(a).

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="theory" title="Derivation, proof and limitations" layer="theory" fullOnly>

A regular distribution integrates a locally integrable function against a smooth compactly supported test function. Delta is not represented by an ordinary locally integrable point spike. It can be approximated by normalized narrow kernels in a weak limit, but that does not assign a finite or infinite pointwise value suitable for multiplication.

Distributional differentiation is defined by $T'[\phi]=-T[\phi']$, generalizing integration by parts without boundary terms for compact support. The derivative of a step is a delta in the corresponding whole-line convention, and the second derivative of |x| is 2δ.

For nonzero real a, the scaling rule is $\delta(ax)=\delta(x)/|a|$ under change of variables. Multiplication by a smooth function is well defined through test-function multiplication; arbitrary products of singular distributions are not generally defined. Writing δ² without a regularization/renormalized framework is not ordinary distribution algebra.

Green functions and weak PDE solutions use distributions to encode point sources and discontinuities. Boundary sources require their own domain and trace conventions. Weak convergence is convergence of actions on all test functions in the stated space, not uniform or pointwise convergence of the approximating kernel.


### Derivation and transfer

The delta distribution is defined by its action δ[φ]=φ(0), not by a finite height at zero. Its derivative acts as δ′[φ]=−φ′(0). A Gaussian approximation has fixed unit area while its height rises and width shrinks.

The display uses a normalized Gaussian of positive width. Convergence is distributional against appropriate test functions, not pointwise convergence to an ordinary function with a finite value at zero. Products of distributions are not automatically defined.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="worked" title="A worked argument" layer="worked" fullOnly>

The whole-line integral of δ(2x) times a test function with φ(0)=3 is 3/2. The factor is absolute: δ(−2x) gives the same result. For |x|, the first derivative jumps by two, producing 2δ in its second weak derivative.
