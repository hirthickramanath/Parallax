<LessonSection id="story" title="Why the question matters" layer="story">

Thermal history couples expansion, interactions and entropy transfer. Freeze-out is a competition of rates, not a universal fixed temperature.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="concept" title="Definitions and hypotheses" layer="concept">

A species stays near equilibrium while its interaction/relaxation rate sufficiently exceeds the expansion rate and relevant gradients; decoupling requires a specified process and distribution.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="theory" title="Derivation, proof and limitations" layer="theory" fullOnly>

For adiabatically expanding decoupled radiation without an entropy-transfer change, $T\propto a^{-1}$. When species annihilate or transfer entropy, the temperatures of coupled and decoupled sectors evolve differently. A single T∝a⁻¹ extrapolation across every transition misses these changes.

Weak reactions maintain a neutron/proton ratio near thermal balance before their effective rate drops relative to H. Neutron decay and the deuterium bottleneck then influence the abundance available when light-nucleus formation proceeds. Rates, baryon density and expansion history all enter a BBN network.

If most surviving neutrons end in helium-4, a simple estimate for helium mass fraction is $Y\approx2r/(1+r)$ with neutron/proton ratio r at the relevant synthesis time. It neglects smaller nuclei and detailed network effects; precision inference must include rates and observational abundance systematics.

Recombination is electron binding and reduced free-electron scattering, distinct from BBN nuclear synthesis. Neutrino decoupling and later photon last scattering are also different events. Constraints on extra radiation or new interactions are model comparisons with uncertainties, not direct readings of one primordial element ratio.


### Derive, test and transfer

A freely propagating photon wavelength stretches as a, so its energy scales as 1/a. Number density scales as a^−3 in a fixed comoving volume; radiation energy density therefore scales as a^−4. An equilibrium-shaped photon spectrum consequently retains its form with T proportional to 1/a when no entropy transfer changes the relation.

Species decoupling and particle–antiparticle annihilation redistribute entropy, so distinct species need not share one temperature afterward. Primordial nucleosynthesis depends on expansion, nuclear reaction rates and baryon density, not merely on the photon temperature scaling. The investigation tests the clean redshifting limit and does not integrate a reaction network or infer abundances.

</LessonSection>
