</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="theory" title="Derivation, proof and limitations" layer="theory" fullOnly>

The first energy coefficient is $E_n^{(1)}=\langle n|V|n\rangle$. The first state correction involves V_mn/(E_n⁰−E_m⁰) for m≠n with a normalization/gauge convention. Small denominators can invalidate an apparently weak perturbation.

The second energy coefficient is $E_n^{(2)}=\sum_{m\ne n}|V_{mn}|^2/(E_n^{(0)}-E_m^{(0)})$ in a discrete complete-basis setting with the needed convergence. A ground-state second-order correction is nonpositive when every coupled denominator is negative. Continuum contributions require integrals and appropriate normalization.

Near degeneracy, first diagonalize the relevant subspace rather than divide by a tiny gap. A perturbation series can be asymptotic rather than convergent; comparing orders and an exactly soluble control helps delimit usefulness.

Physical approximations also require the correct unperturbed domain and symmetry. An operator can be numerically small on selected states yet not be bounded on the entire Hilbert space. Report the gap, matrix elements and λ regime alongside an approximate energy.


### Derivation and transfer

For a nondegenerate level, the first-order shift is the diagonal matrix element of the perturbation. Off-diagonal mixing contributes at second order with denominators En−Em. Small denominators signal that the assumed expansion may fail.

For [[0,g],[g,Δ]], the lower exact eigenvalue is (Δ−sqrt(Δ²+4g²))/2, with small-g shift −g²/Δ. The display compares an exact two-state avoided mixing, not a universal error bound for many-state perturbation theory.

</LessonSection>
