<LessonSection id="story" title="Why the question matters" layer="story">

Accelerator measurements combine luminosity, acceptance, backgrounds and reconstruction; a response matrix can mix the true physical distribution.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="concept" title="Definitions and hypotheses" layer="concept">

Expected signal counts are related to cross section through integrated luminosity and an efficiency/acceptance model over the specified phase space.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="theory" title="Derivation, proof and limitations" layer="theory" fullOnly>

A one-bin relation is $N_{sig}=\mathcal L_{int}\sigma\epsilon$ when the definitions and response factor justify it. Background subtraction, branching fractions and fiducial restrictions must be matched. A count rate and integrated count use different luminosity units.

Tracking, calorimetry, timing and particle identification probe different interactions. Momentum curvature needs a magnetic-field geometry and charge sign, while deposited calorimeter energy need not equal total energy for every particle. Calibration and material budgets affect response.

A multi-bin response matrix maps truth to reconstructed distributions with inefficiency and migration. Naive inversion can amplify statistical noise; regularization introduces assumptions and needs closure/sensitivity tests. Simulated detector response is not automatically measured calibration.

Statistical significance differs from parameter precision and discovery from exclusion. Trials, nuisance parameters and systematic covariance belong in inference. This lesson gives analysis principles, not operational accelerator construction or a claim of a new particle from synthetic events.


### Derivation and transfer

In a uniform magnetic field perpendicular to momentum, a charged track has curvature radius r=p/(|q|B). Bending direction identifies a charge sign only with a known field direction and tracking convention. Energy loss, multiple scattering and detector resolution alter measured tracks.

The display uses |q|=1 and a transverse momentum magnitude. It is not a full accelerator lattice, event reconstruction or detector-efficiency simulation. Neutral particles do not acquire the same curvature formula.

</LessonSection>
