<LessonSection id="story" title="Why the question matters" layer="story">

Monte Carlo estimates need uncertainty and mixing diagnostics, especially when posterior distributions have distant modes or narrow degeneracies.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="concept" title="Definitions and hypotheses" layer="concept">

Independent Monte Carlo estimates expectations by sample averages. Markov chain Monte Carlo constructs a chain with a target invariant distribution; invariance alone does not guarantee rapid convergence.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="theory" title="Derivation, proof and limitations" layer="theory" fullOnly>

For independent samples X_i with finite variance σ², the mean estimator has variance σ²/N. This produces the familiar N⁻¹ᐟ² standard-error scaling, not a deterministic error bound for every realization. Infinite-variance tails and biased sampling can invalidate that simple uncertainty estimate.

For a symmetric proposal q(y|x)=q(x|y), Metropolis acceptance is $\alpha=\min(1,\pi(y)/\pi(x))$. A nonsymmetric proposal needs the Hastings factor q(x|y)/q(y|x). Work with log densities and compare log acceptance to log of a uniform variate to avoid overflow. A normalization constant common to numerator and denominator cancels, but support restrictions do not.

Detailed balance is a sufficient route to invariance for the stated kernel. Irreducibility, aperiodicity and other convergence conditions still matter. Rejections keep the current state as a chain sample; discarding all repeated states changes the sampling measure. Warm-up or adaptation must be distinguished from the retained stationary sampling scheme.

For a stationary correlated chain, the integrated autocorrelation time τ=1+2∑ρ_k gives effective sample size roughly N/τ when the sum and variance assumptions hold. Multiple dispersed chains, trace plots, rank-based convergence diagnostics and effective sample sizes expose some failures but cannot prove every hidden mode was found. Report priors, likelihood, proposal, seeds and uncertainty; synthetic inference exercises are not observational discovery.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="interactive" title="Investigate the claim" layer="interactive">

Sample a one-dimensional normal target, then a separated mixture. Compare short-step and long-step proposals using traces, rejection rates and effective sample sizes. Retain rejected states, run dispersed starts and label all targets/samples synthetic.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="worked" title="A worked argument" layer="worked" fullOnly>

If an approximately stationary chain has N=1000 and τ=5, its effective sample size is about 200. A symmetric proposal with target ratio π(y)/π(x)=.2 is accepted with probability .2. Calling all 1000 draws independent would understate uncertainty.

</LessonSection>
