<LessonSection id="concept" title="Definitions and hypotheses" layer="concept">

Intermolecular forces include electrostatic multipole interactions, induction, dispersion and short-range exchange effects; hydrogen bonding is a directional interaction whose description depends on the molecular context.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="theory" title="Derivation, proof and limitations" layer="theory" fullOnly>

A permanent dipole creates an orientation-dependent interaction with another multipole. Rotational thermal averaging can change the distance and temperature dependence, so an aligned dipole formula is not automatically the free energy of a freely rotating liquid. Induction occurs when a field polarizes a partner.

Correlated electronic fluctuations generate London dispersion even for nonpolar species. In the nonretarded well-separated regime its leading attraction often has $U=-C_6/r^6$ with C₆>0. Retardation, geometry, many-body screening and overlap can modify this form. It is not a short-distance formula valid down to coincident nuclei.

A Lennard–Jones 12–6 potential is a convenient empirical model combining a steep repulsive term with dispersion-like attraction. The repulsion exponent 12 is not a universal derivation of Pauli exclusion. Its minimum is at r=2¹ᐟ⁶σ and depth −ε for U=4ε[(σ/r)¹²−(σ/r)⁶]. Mixing rules require validation for the species and environment.

Hydrogen bonding often involves an X–H donor and an acceptor electron-density region with strong geometry dependence. Electrostatics, induction, exchange and charge-transfer language describe parts of a coupled electronic interaction rather than universally separable observables. Networks, solvent competition and zero-point motion matter; counting hydrogen atoms alone does not predict boiling points or binding strength.


### Derive, test and transfer

A Lennard–Jones toy potential U=4ε[(σ/r)¹²−(σ/r)⁶] combines a steep repulsive term with a longer-range attraction. Setting its derivative to zero gives rmin=2^(1/6)σ and Umin=−ε. The parameter σ is a zero crossing, not the equilibrium separation.

The inverse-sixth attraction has a dispersion interpretation in an appropriate nonretarded regime, while the inverse-twelfth repulsion is a convenient approximation rather than an exact universal electron-overlap law. Hydrogen bonding and anisotropic multipoles require additional structure. The display is a pair potential, not a full liquid simulation or a universal molecular interaction.

</LessonSection>
