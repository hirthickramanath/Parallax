<LessonSection id="story" title="Why the question matters" layer="story">

Multivalued formulas occur in waves, asymptotics and complex response. A branch is part of the mathematical definition, and a physical boundary or causality condition can select one.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="intuition" title="Build a mental model" layer="intuition">

A phase can increase continuously along a path even when a single principal angle jumps. A cut selects one sheet of a description; it does not remove the underlying winding phenomenon.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="concept" title="Definitions and hypotheses" layer="concept">

A branch of a multivalued expression is a single-valued holomorphic choice on a stated domain. Analytic continuation extends compatible holomorphic germs through overlapping open regions.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="theory" title="Derivation, proof and limitations" layer="theory" fullOnly>

For z=reⁱᶿ≠0, logarithm values are ln r+i(θ+2πk) for integer k. A holomorphic logarithm on a domain satisfies exp(Log z)=z and has derivative 1/z. A standard principal branch uses Arg z in (−π,π) and removes the nonpositive real axis. At a point on the excluded cut, that branch is not defined even though another local logarithm may exist there away from zero.

A logarithm exists on a simply connected domain avoiding zero, and more generally the winding integral of dz/z must vanish for every closed curve in the domain. On the punctured plane the unit-circle integral is 2πi, so no global single-valued holomorphic logarithm exists. A chosen cut is a domain convention; moving it changes where that branch description fails, while the branch point at zero remains.

Define a square-root branch through $\sqrt z=\exp[\tfrac12\operatorname{Log}z]$. Continuing once around zero increases a continuously tracked logarithm by 2πi and changes the square root by a factor eⁱπ=−1. After two loops the square root returns, but the logarithm has increased by 4πi. A principal-branch evaluation at each sampled point is not the same operation as continuously tracking a value along the entire path.

The identity theorem says that two holomorphic functions on a connected domain agreeing on a set with an accumulation point inside the domain agree everywhere there. This gives uniqueness where compatible continuations overlap. Agreement only at isolated points or at an accumulation point outside the domain is insufficient. A Taylor series can stop at its convergence boundary while analytic continuation succeeds through another chain of overlapping disks; convergence of one series and existence of continuation are distinct.

Path dependence of continuation around singularities is monodromy. Under the hypotheses of the monodromy theorem, continuation along all paths in a simply connected domain becomes single valued. One must establish the allowed paths and absence of obstructions rather than simply calling a formula analytic. In physical applications, branch cuts, outgoing-wave choices and causal prescriptions must follow the model; a software library’s default principal branch does not by itself establish the correct physical condition.


### Derivation and transfer

Writing a continued logarithm along z=r exp(it) gives ln r+it. A full positive circuit changes the continuation by 2πi even though z returns to its starting point. A principal branch instead imposes a cut and restricts the argument interval.

The continuous angle display follows a path on the covering surface; it is not the principal value after wrapping. No single-valued logarithm exists on the entire punctured plane. Branch conventions must be specified before comparing values or powers.

</LessonSection>
