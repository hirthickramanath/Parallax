Why can a partial differential equation describe whole families of classical trajectories? State a prediction and a reason before reading the argument.

<LessonSection id="story" title="Why the question matters" layer="story">

Hamilton–Jacobi theory trades trajectory equations for an action function. Action–angle coordinates simplify suitable integrable bound motion but do not apply globally to every Hamiltonian system.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="intuition" title="Build a mental model" layer="intuition">

An action surface supplies momentum through its spatial slope. Its constants label trajectories; suitable periodic motion can be encoded by actions that remain fixed and angles that advance.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="concept" title="Definitions and hypotheses" layer="concept">

Hamilton’s principal function S(q,t) satisfies ∂S/∂t+H(q,∂S/∂q,t)=0 in the appropriate local smooth setting. A complete integral contains enough independent constants to reconstruct the motion.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="theory" title="Derivation, proof and limitations" layer="theory" fullOnly>

A time-dependent canonical construction can seek a transformed Hamiltonian zero, leaving constants as new momenta. Its generating equation is $\partial_tS+H(q,\partial_qS,t)=0$. Recover old momenta from p=∂qS; derivatives with respect to the integration constants supply the corresponding new coordinates. One isolated solution S without enough independent parameters need not recover every trajectory.

For autonomous H, separation S=W(q)−Et yields H(q,∂qW)=E. In one dimension p(q;E) from the energy equation can be integrated to obtain W on a selected branch. Turning points and multiple trajectories can require different local branches; a single-valued classical function is not guaranteed everywhere.

For a periodic degree of freedom, define action $J=(2\pi)^{-1}\oint p\,dq$. In suitable action–angle coordinates the integrable Hamiltonian is H(J), with J̇=0 and θ̇=∂H/∂J. Angles are periodic coordinates; actions have angular-momentum dimensions. Some conventions omit 1/(2π), which changes the paired frequency relation.

For a harmonic oscillator H=p²/(2m)+mω²q²/2, the phase ellipse area is 2πE/ω, so J=E/ω and H=ωJ. The angle advances at ω under this convention. Near a separatrix the period can diverge, making simple global action–angle descriptions singular.

Several degrees of freedom need enough independent commuting integrals and the applicable regular compact invariant structure for the standard integrable-torus construction. Generic coupled or chaotic motion does not automatically admit a global set of conserved actions. Adiabatic invariance is an approximation with timescale and separatrix restrictions, not a blanket conservation law for any slowly described parameter change.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="worked" title="A worked argument" layer="worked" fullOnly>

For a harmonic oscillator with E=6 and ω=3 in compatible units, J=2. With the chosen 1/(2π) convention, ∂H/∂J=3 gives the angular frequency. Doubling E at unchanged ω doubles the action.

</LessonSection>
