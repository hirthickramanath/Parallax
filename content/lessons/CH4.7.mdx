<LessonSection id="story" title="Why the question matters" layer="story">

Coordination geometry and ligand interactions change d-level splitting and compete with electron pairing, affecting spectra and magnetism.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="concept" title="Definitions and hypotheses" layer="concept">

Crystal-field models split metal d orbitals through an idealized ligand environment; ligand-field/MO models include covalent mixing and must state geometry and electron-count conventions.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="theory" title="Derivation, proof and limitations" layer="theory" fullOnly>

In an ideal octahedral field, t₂g orbitals lie lower and e_g higher, separated by Δ_o. Relative to the d-level barycentre, $E_{t_{2g}}=-2\Delta_o/5$ and $E_{e_g}=3\Delta_o/5$. The degeneracy-weighted shift sums to zero, preserving the chosen reference; the splitting is not a net count of total metal–ligand binding energy.

For suitable d counts, competition between Δ_o and pairing costs determines high-spin versus low-spin occupations. Δ_o alone is not enough without a consistent pairing/covalency model. Tetrahedral and square-planar fields have different patterns and cannot reuse an octahedral diagram unchanged.

Ligand-field theory treats donor/acceptor mixing with metal orbitals. π donation and backbonding alter orbital energies and electron distribution. Oxidation state is a formal electron-accounting convention, distinct from measured metal charge. The spectrochemical ordering is empirical and contextual, not a universal scalar intrinsic to an isolated ligand under every condition.

Electronic spectra, magnetic moments, geometry and vibrational shifts test these models together. Spin-only moments neglect orbital and spin-orbit contributions, which can be significant. Jahn–Teller distortions and excited-state effects can lower ideal symmetry. Do not infer a unique bonding mechanism from one absorption peak or a single formal d count.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="worked" title="A worked argument" layer="worked" fullOnly>

For an octahedral low-spin d⁶ occupation t₂g⁶e_g⁰, the model crystal-field contribution is 6×(−.4Δ_o)=−2.4Δ_o before separate pairing accounting. For high-spin d⁶ with t₂g⁴e_g² it is −.4Δ_o, illustrating why a consistent pairing comparison is required.

</LessonSection>
