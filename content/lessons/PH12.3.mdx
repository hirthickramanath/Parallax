<LessonSection id="concept" title="Definitions and hypotheses" layer="concept">

Zeeman and Stark effects are magnetic- and electric-field shifts under a stated atomic Hamiltonian and coupling regime.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="theory" title="Derivation, proof and limitations" layer="theory" fullOnly>

In a weak magnetic field within a suitable LS-coupled level, $\Delta E=\mu_Bg_Jm_JB$. The Landé factor combines spin and orbital contributions with an adopted electron magnetic convention. Neglecting the small anomalous correction, an L=0,S=1/2,J=1/2 level has g≈2.

When field coupling competes with fine/hyperfine structure, the weak-field coupled basis may fail. Paschen–Back-type regimes require a different diagonalization, not extrapolating the same linear weak-field labels indefinitely. Avoided crossings and mixing can change transition strengths.

An electric dipole perturbation is −d·E. A nondegenerate parity eigenstate often has zero first-order permanent electric dipole expectation, giving a leading quadratic Stark response. Degeneracies or broken inversion symmetry can enable linear effects. The polarizability sign/value is state and convention dependent.

Real spectra also include gradients, broadening and calibration. Identify whether B/E values are uniform and whether transition frequencies reflect differences of shifted states. A single-state energy shift is not automatically the full measured transition shift.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="worked" title="A worked argument" layer="worked" fullOnly>

With μ_BB=2,g=2,m=.5, a weak-field shift is 2. The m=−.5 partner shifts by −2, giving separation 4. These numbers assume the weak-field basis remains valid.

</LessonSection>
