<LessonSection id="story" title="Why the question matters" layer="story">

A covariant derivative compares geometric objects at neighbouring points, and a metric-compatible torsion-free connection has a uniquely determined form.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="concept" title="Definitions and hypotheses" layer="concept">

A connection defines covariant differentiation; the Levi–Civita connection is the unique torsion-free connection compatible with a specified nondegenerate metric.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="theory" title="Derivation, proof and limitations" layer="theory" fullOnly>

For a vector, $\nabla_i v^j=\partial_i v^j+\Gamma^j_{ik}v^k$. For a covector the connection term has the corresponding minus sign. These terms compensate for local basis changes; Christoffel symbols themselves do not transform as tensor components.

For Levi–Civita geometry, metric compatibility and torsion freedom give $\Gamma^k_{ij}=\tfrac12g^{k\ell}(\partial_i g_{j\ell}+\partial_jg_{i\ell}-\partial_\ell g_{ij})$. A nondegenerate metric is required to form its inverse. Lorentzian and positive-definite metrics use the same formula but different physical norm properties.

In flat polar coordinates ds²=dr²+r²dθ², nonzero symbols include Γ^r_θθ=−r and Γ^θ_rθ=Γ^θ_θr=1/r. Nonzero connection coefficients in this chart do not imply intrinsic curvature. A locally chosen basis can remove suitable connection components at a point, not generally curvature throughout an open region.

Other connections can include torsion or nonmetricity, so the Levi–Civita choice is an assumption justified by the theory being used. A scalar’s first covariant derivative equals its partial derivative, but its second derivative involves connection terms because the first derivative is a covector.


### Derivation and transfer

A covariant derivative adds connection terms to ordinary component derivatives so the result transforms tensorially. In flat polar coordinates, Γrθθ=−r and Γθrθ=1/r are nonzero. Nonzero Christoffel symbols alone therefore do not imply curvature.

The display probes one connection coefficient in a flat chart. Its coordinate dependence is expected. Curvature requires the appropriate derivatives and products of connection coefficients, not the magnitude of one coefficient.

</LessonSection>
