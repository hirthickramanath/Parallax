<LessonSection id="story" title="Why the question matters" layer="story">

Nonlinearity can couple oscillation frequencies and amplify initial uncertainty. Numerical divergence must be separated from physical sensitivity.

<LessonSection id="concept" title="Definitions and hypotheses" layer="concept">

A nonlinear dynamical system has state evolution ẋ=f(x,t) not reducible to linear superposition. A Lyapunov exponent measures asymptotic perturbation growth under a stated trajectory and norm convention.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="theory" title="Derivation, proof and limitations" layer="theory" fullOnly>

For the weakly nonlinear oscillator $\ddot x+\omega_0^2x+\alpha x^3=0$, multiplying by ẋ gives conserved energy ẋ²/2+ω₀²x²/2+αx⁴/4. A negative quartic coefficient alone is not a globally confining potential; a local weak-amplitude approximation needs an admitted range or higher stabilizing terms.

A perturbative nearly sinusoidal analysis yields a leading amplitude-dependent frequency shift proportional to αA² for the regular weak-nonlinearity regime. Resonant forcing and damping can produce multiple responses, hysteresis and bifurcations. Linear superposition of two solutions is generally no longer a solution.

For a smooth system, a small perturbation obeys the tangent equation δẋ=Df(x(t),t)δx. A largest Lyapunov exponent can be defined by a long-time growth rate of perturbation norm, with rescaling needed in practical calculations before finite separation saturates. Positive finite-time growth alone is not a proof of asymptotic chaos; transients and numerical instability can imitate it.

Chaotic behaviour requires the relevant bounded aperiodic dynamics and sensitivity qualifications, not merely a complicated plot. Poincaré sections and parameter sweeps can reveal structure. Verify convergence, conserved quantities when applicable and step-size dependence before assigning a computed exponent to the mathematical system. Predictability limits concern finite uncertainty, not a failure of deterministic evolution.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="worked" title="A worked argument" layer="worked" fullOnly>

If a perturbation grows from .01 to .04 during time 2 in a local exponential-growth window, the estimated finite-time rate is ln4/2=ln2. This is a finite-window diagnostic; it is not automatically the infinite-time largest exponent.

</LessonSection>
