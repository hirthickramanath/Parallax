<LessonSection id="story" title="Why the question matters" layer="story">

Green, Stokes and divergence theorems connect local derivatives with global boundary integrals. They unify conservation-law calculations but require regular fields and compatible orientations.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="intuition" title="Build a mental model" layer="intuition">

Partition a region into small cells. Contributions on shared internal boundaries cancel in opposite orientations, leaving only the external boundary.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="concept" title="Definitions and hypotheses" layer="concept">

The integral theorems relate circulation to curl and outward flux to divergence for fields with suitable continuous derivatives on appropriate regions and their neighbourhoods.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="theory" title="Derivation, proof and limitations" layer="theory" fullOnly>

For a planar region with positively oriented piecewise smooth boundary, Green's theorem gives $\oint_{\partial D}P\,dx+Q\,dy=\iint_D(Q_x-P_y)\,dA$ under standard smoothness assumptions. Positive orientation keeps the region on the left while traversing its boundary; holes require opposite orientation on inner boundary components.

Stokes' theorem generalizes this to an oriented smooth or piecewise smooth surface S: $\oint_{\partial S}F\cdot dr=\iint_S(\nabla\times F)\cdot n\,dS$. The boundary direction follows the right-hand convention from the chosen normal. Changing both orientations preserves equality; changing only one reverses just one side and breaks it.

The divergence theorem for a bounded region V with suitable closed outward boundary gives $\iint_{\partial V}F\cdot n\,dS=\iiint_V\nabla\cdot F\,dV$. It is a flux theorem, not a circulation theorem. A field singular within the region does not meet the ordinary smooth-field hypotheses; removing a small surrounding region introduces an additional inner boundary.

For F=(−y,x,0), curl is (0,0,2). A counterclockwise unit circle in the xy-plane has circulation 2π, matching curl flux 2 times disk area π. For F=(x,y,z), divergence is 3, and a radius-R sphere has outward flux 3 times its volume, or 4πR³.

The field r/|r|³ has zero divergence away from the origin but flux 4π through a centred sphere. Applying the divergence theorem over the full ball while ignoring the singular origin is invalid. On a punctured shell, outer and inner flux contributions cancel as the ordinary theorem predicts; a distributional formulation can represent the missing point source explicitly.

These identities are exact mathematical statements under their hypotheses. Interpreting a measured flux as evidence for a source requires a field model, boundary data and uncertainty treatment. An unresolved hole or discontinuity can matter more than an algebraic simplification.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="worked" title="A worked argument" layer="worked" fullOnly>

On the unit cube [0,1]³ with F=(x,y,z), the three faces at coordinate 0 contribute zero and the three at coordinate 1 each contribute 1. Total flux 3 agrees with volume integral of divergence 3 over unit volume.

</LessonSection>
