<LessonSection id="concept" title="Definitions and hypotheses" layer="concept">

An astrochemical abundance is a species number density or fraction under a specified reference convention; a spectral intensity is a different observable requiring excitation/transfer modelling.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="theory" title="Derivation, proof and limitations" layer="theory" fullOnly>

For a simple species X, $dn_X/dt=P_X-L_Xn_X$ when production P and first-order loss L are the chosen closures. A steady value P/L requires those quantities to remain effectively fixed and a relaxation time short enough for the setting.

Ultraviolet photons, cosmic rays, ions and dust shielding alter reaction/destruction rates. Cold diffuse, shielded molecular and hot shocked regions support different networks. Low kinetic temperature does not mean no chemistry: ion-molecule processes, photons and surfaces can matter.

Abundance fractions require a denominator such as total hydrogen nuclei, H₂ or all particles. A quoted factor can be wrong by conventions even when both raw species counts are correct. Line-of-sight column averages can mix several physical components.

Molecular detection requires multiple consistent transitions, rest-frequency/provenance, excitation and line-blend checks. A reaction network predicting a species does not demonstrate its observation. Retain unknown rates and environmental uncertainty rather than inventing a complete chemical inventory.


### Derive, test and transfer

For two molecular levels in local thermodynamic equilibrium, the population ratio is (g1/g0)exp(−ΔE/(kT)). Degeneracy multiplies a ratio before normalization, so a ratio can exceed one without being a probability above one. A total column abundance requires a partition sum and a line/transfer model.

Interstellar gas can be too dilute for collisions to maintain LTE; radiation pumping and optical depth then change line inference. Detecting a molecular transition also needs accurate frequencies, velocity context and blending checks. The display uses one unit-degeneracy ratio as a controlled example and does not claim to infer an actual survey abundance.

</LessonSection>
