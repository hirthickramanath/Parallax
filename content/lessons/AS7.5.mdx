<LessonSection id="concept" title="Definitions and hypotheses" layer="concept">

A radiative equilibrium temperature follows a stated absorption/emission redistribution model; habitability and biological interpretation add atmospheric, geological and observational assumptions.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="theory" title="Derivation, proof and limitations" layer="theory" fullOnly>

For a spherical uniformly reradiating blackbody with Bond albedo A, $T_{eq}=T_*\sqrt{R_*/(2a)}(1-A)^{1/4}$ in the simple zero-internal-heat model. Day-side-only emission, nonblack emissivity, eccentric orbit and internal heat change the relation. T_eq is not automatically surface temperature.

An atmosphere can alter surface energy balance through absorption, scattering and convection. Water inventory, pressure, chemistry, escape and stellar activity affect possible liquid water. A habitable-zone boundary is a model-dependent insolation criterion, not a direct biological detection.

A putative biosignature must be compared with abiotic production, sinks and atmospheric history. Disequilibrium can be suggestive but needs a source/maintenance model. A spectral feature can be confused by clouds, instrument systematics and overlapping molecules; nondetection also depends on sensitivity.

Population claims require selection and false-positive/false-negative analysis. Evidence for an atmospheric species, evidence for a habitable environment and evidence for biology are separate inference steps. This course adds prerequisite physical reasoning, not a broad biology curriculum or a claim that one observed molecule proves life.


### Derive, test and transfer

Absorbed stellar power for a spherical planet is $(1-A)\pi R^2F$, while uniform thermal reradiation is $4\pi R^2\sigma T_{eq}^4$ for unit emissivity. Balancing gives $T_{eq}=[(1-A)F/(4\sigma)]^{1/4}$. The planet radius cancels because interception and emission areas scale together.

A surface temperature can differ greatly through greenhouse opacity, circulation, clouds and internal heat. A putative biosignature needs atmospheric context, chemical disequilibrium and abiotic alternatives; equilibrium temperature alone does not establish biology or liquid water. </LessonSection>
