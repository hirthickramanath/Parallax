<LessonSection id="concept" title="Definitions and hypotheses" layer="concept">

The Cauchy stress tensor maps an oriented surface normal to traction: t=σn. Infinitesimal strain is the symmetric displacement gradient.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="theory" title="Derivation, proof and limitations" layer="theory" fullOnly>

For displacement field u, $\varepsilon_{ij}=(\partial_i u_j+\partial_j u_i)/2$ removes the infinitesimal rigid-rotation part of the gradient. This is a small-deformation measure; finite rotations and large strains require an appropriate nonlinear kinematic description.

Traction depends on surface orientation through σn. Momentum balance is ρa_i=∂_jσ_ij+ρb_i in the ordinary Cauchy continuum. Angular momentum balance gives symmetric stress in the absence of couple stresses or additional internal rotational structure. Sign conventions for tensile versus compressive pressure must be stated.

For homogeneous isotropic linear elasticity, $\sigma=\lambda\operatorname{tr}(\varepsilon)I+2\mu\varepsilon$. Lamé coefficients describe bulk and shear response; elastic stability imposes positive shear and bulk moduli in the usual three-dimensional model. A uniaxial stress experiment differs from a constrained uniaxial strain experiment because transverse boundary conditions change the response.

Anisotropic crystals need a higher-rank stiffness tensor with symmetry constraints. Plasticity, fracture, viscosity and history dependence are not captured by one reversible Hooke relation. Elastic energy is quadratic only within the linear reversible regime. Continuum fields also assume a length scale large enough to average microscopic structure.


### Derive, test and transfer

For uniaxial small elastic deformation, stress is force over reference area and strain is relative length change. Hooke's relation σ=Eε gives a slope with pressure units; a larger modulus means more stress for the same small strain. Stored elastic energy density is ∫σ dε=Eε²/2 in this linear reversible regime.

Tensor stress includes shear and normal components; one uniaxial modulus does not replace the whole constitutive tensor. Plastic deformation, fracture, hysteresis and large geometry changes fall outside this model. A negative strain can represent compression while energy stays positive. Distinguish a signed stress/strain response from the energy density when testing the limits.

</LessonSection>
