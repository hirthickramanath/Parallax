<LessonSection id="story" title="Why the question matters" layer="story">

Probability models underlie photon counts, measurement noise and astronomical population inference. A density is not a probability at a point; confusing them creates wrong units and wrong likelihood arguments.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="intuition" title="Build a mental model" layer="intuition">

Probability is area under a density curve. A narrow distribution can have a tall curve while retaining total area one.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="concept" title="Definitions and hypotheses" layer="concept">

A probability space assigns nonnegative countably additive weights to admitted events with total weight one. A random variable is a measurable map from outcomes to numerical values.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="theory" title="Derivation, proof and limitations" layer="theory" fullOnly>

For disjoint events A₁,A₂,..., countable additivity gives $P(\bigcup_nA_n)=\sum_nP(A_n)$. Complements and the union formula follow from this axiom. The event collection must support the operations used; not every arbitrary subset in every mathematical construction is automatically assigned a probability.

A real random variable X induces a cumulative distribution $F_X(x)=P(X\le x)$, which is nondecreasing, right-continuous and tends to zero and one at the two infinite ends. A distribution can be discrete, continuous, mixed or neither purely of those two forms. A density f, when present, gives interval probabilities by integration. It has inverse-X units and can exceed one.

For a nonnegative or integrable variable, expectation is the probability-weighted integral. In a discrete case E[X]=∑xp(x); with a density E[X]=∫xf(x)dx. Linearity holds for integrable variables without independence. Independence becomes relevant for products and covariance: $\operatorname{Var}(X+Y)=\operatorname{Var}(X)+\operatorname{Var}(Y)+2\operatorname{Cov}(X,Y)$ when finite.

Conditional probability P(A|B)=P(A∩B)/P(B) requires P(B)>0. Conditioning a continuous variable on an exact point cannot be done by dividing two zero event probabilities; densities or regular conditional distributions provide the appropriate framework. Conditional expectation is a random variable determined by the conditioning information, not merely an informal updated guess.

The law of total expectation averages conditional means. The law of total variance splits $\operatorname{Var}(X)=E[\operatorname{Var}(X|Y)]+\operatorname{Var}(E[X|Y])$ when finite. The first part is average within-group variation, while the second is variation between group means. Ignoring the second can understate uncertainty in a mixed source population.


### Derivation and transfer

Conditional probability P(A|B)=P(A∩B)/P(B) requires P(B)>0. Independence makes P(A∩B)=P(A)P(B), but mutually exclusive nonzero-probability events cannot be independent. Expectations are linear even without independence.

The display assumes independent Bernoulli trials and computes P(all successes)=p^n. Correlation would change that joint probability. Neither a small conditional probability nor a rare observation establishes an impossible event; sampling design and the reference event matter.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="worked" title="A worked argument" layer="worked" fullOnly>

A uniform density on [0,0.2] is 5 in inverse units. Its total probability is 5×0.2=1, and the probability of [0.05,0.10] is 5×0.05=0.25. The height 5 is not a probability of an exact point.
