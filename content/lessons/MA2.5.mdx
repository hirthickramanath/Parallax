<LessonSection id="story" title="Why the question matters" layer="story">

Line integrals connect a field with an actual path. Conservative fields permit endpoint energy bookkeeping; other fields retain information about how the path was traversed.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="intuition" title="Build a mental model" layer="intuition">

At each point, only the field component along the displacement contributes to work. Adding the local contributions requires both the field and a parameterized curve.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="concept" title="Definitions and hypotheses" layer="concept">

For a smooth oriented curve r(t), the vector line integral of F is ∫F(r(t))·r′(t)dt. A scalar arc-length integral uses |r′(t)| instead.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="theory" title="Derivation, proof and limitations" layer="theory" fullOnly>

For a piecewise smooth curve with increasing parameter t, work is $\int_C F\cdot dr=\int_a^b F(r(t))\cdot r'(t)\,dt$. A smooth orientation-preserving reparameterization gives the same integral by one-variable substitution. Reversing orientation reverses a vector line integral. The scalar integral ∫f ds is orientation independent because ds uses speed magnitude.

If F=∇φ and φ is continuously differentiable on a domain containing the curve, the chain rule gives $\int_C F\cdot dr=\phi(B)-\phi(A)$. For an electrostatic convention F=−∇U, work is the negative potential-energy change. Every closed-loop integral then vanishes, and work between fixed endpoints is path independent.

For a continuously differentiable field on a suitable open simply connected domain, vanishing curl is sufficient for a potential under the standard theorem. The domain restriction matters. On the punctured plane, $F=(-y/(x^2+y^2),x/(x^2+y^2))$ has zero scalar curl away from the origin, but circulation around a unit circle is 2π. A single-valued angular potential cannot be defined globally there.

For a straight radial path in that punctured-plane field, the tangent is radial and F tangential, yielding zero local dot product. This does not contradict the nonzero circular integral, because the paths and endpoints or winding differ. A singularity excluded from the domain cannot silently be crossed to apply a theorem.

Existence of a potential on a connected domain determines it up to one additive constant. Disconnected components can have independent constants. A measured near-zero circulation along one loop does not prove a field is conservative everywhere; the mathematical theorem and the observational inference have different obligations.


### Derive, test and transfer

Parameterize a curve by r(t); vector work is $\int F(r(t))\cdot r'(t)dt$. Reversing orientation changes its sign. For F=(−y,x) on a circle of radius R traversed counterclockwise, r′ is tangent and the integrand is R², giving circulation 2πR².

A gradient field integrates to endpoint potential difference on its domain. Vanishing curl alone needs a topological qualification: the field (−y/r²,x/r²) is curl-free away from the origin yet has circulation 2π around it. Its punctured domain prevents a single-valued global potential. A local derivative test and a global path-independence claim must therefore be separated.

</LessonSection>
