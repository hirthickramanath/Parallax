<LessonSection id="concept" title="Definitions and hypotheses" layer="concept">

The Schwarzschild criterion compares the actual logarithmic temperature-pressure gradient with the adiabatic parcel gradient in a chemically homogeneous layer.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="theory" title="Derivation, proof and limitations" layer="theory" fullOnly>

Define $\nabla=d\ln T/d\ln p$. A small parcel displaced rapidly compared with heat exchange approximately follows ∇_ad. At the new pressure, its temperature and density relative to the surroundings determine buoyancy. For the ordinary homogeneous stellar setting, ∇>∇_ad is unstable; radiative transport alone would then demand a gradient that convection can modify.

Composition affects density at fixed pressure and temperature. A mean-molecular-weight gradient enters the Ledoux criterion through EOS derivatives. Using Schwarzschild while ignoring a stabilizing composition gradient can misclassify a layer. Rotation, magnetic fields and finite thermal/compositional diffusion introduce additional modes and qualifications.

Mixing-length theory assumes a characteristic travel length ℓ=αH_p with pressure scale height H_p=−dr/dlnp. It estimates parcel contrasts, speeds and convective flux under a closure model. The dimensionless α is calibrated within a chosen stellar-model framework; it is not a universal directly measured path length for every star and evolutionary phase.

Real convection is three-dimensional, time dependent and coupled to radiation. Overshoot and entrainment can mix material beyond a local stability boundary, while an efficient convective interior can remain close to adiabatic without requiring zero convective flux. Surface superadiabatic layers are especially sensitive to the closure.


### Derive, test and transfer

Compare an upward-displaced parcel, which expands approximately adiabatically, with its new environment at equal pressure. In a homogeneous composition, an environmental temperature gradient steeper than the adiabatic one leaves the parcel warmer and less dense, so buoyancy amplifies the displacement. The Schwarzschild condition is $\nabla_{rad}>\nabla_{ad}$, where $\nabla=d\ln T/d\ln P$.

A composition gradient changes density at fixed pressure and temperature; the Ledoux criterion includes that additional buoyancy. Even when a layer is unstable, the local sign test does not give the convective flux, overshoot length or mixing rate. Mixing-length theory supplies a closure with approximations and parameters, rather than a first-principles solution of turbulent flow. Separate a linear instability diagnosis from the nonlinear transport that follows.

</LessonSection>
