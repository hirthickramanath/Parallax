<LessonSection id="story" title="Why this matters" layer="story">

Thermodynamics distinguishes a system’s state from the process used to reach it. Stellar interiors and engines both need energy accounting and a direction criterion for possible processes.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="intuition" title="Build the picture" layer="intuition">

Internal energy is like a balance; heat and work are transfers, not contents stored in separate compartments.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="concept" title="State the model" layer="concept">

With work W done by the system taken positive, the first law is ΔU=Q−W. Temperature is a state variable; heat is energy transfer due to a temperature difference.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="theory" title="Derive and qualify the result" layer="theory" fullOnly>

For quasistatic boundary work, $W=\int p_{\rm ext}\,dV$; in a reversible mechanical limit internal and external pressures differ infinitesimally. The area under a p–V path depends on the path, whereas ΔU depends only on endpoints. For an ideal gas $pV=nRT$ and internal energy depends only on temperature. With constant molar heat capacity, $\Delta U=nC_V\Delta T$.

At constant volume W=0, so Q=nCᵥΔT. At constant pressure the gas expands and Q=nCₚΔT. For an ideal gas, Cₚ−Cᵥ=R. For a reversible adiabatic path with constant heat capacities, Q=0 and $pV^\gamma=$ constant, γ=Cₚ/Cᵥ. Adiabatic does not mean isothermal: the work changes internal energy.

Entropy changes satisfy $dS=\delta Q_{\rm rev}/T$ along a reversible comparison path. For any process the entropy production of an isolated total system is nonnegative. A system’s entropy alone can decrease by exporting entropy. Heat flows spontaneously from hot to cold because the total entropy change is positive: Q/Tcold−Q/Thot>0.

A cyclic engine has ΔU=0, hence net work equals net heat. Efficiency is W/Qhot and cannot exceed $1-T_{\rm cold}/T_{\rm hot}$ for two equilibrium reservoirs. Temperatures must be absolute. Equality requires reversibility, not merely an ideal-gas working fluid.

The ideal gas and constant-capacity assumptions fail near condensation, at high density and when new molecular degrees of freedom become thermally active. State the regime before extrapolating a classroom equation to a star or atmosphere.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="worked" title="A worked example" layer="worked" fullOnly>

One mole of monatomic ideal gas warms by 10 K. With Cᵥ=3R/2, ΔU≈124.7 J. Constant-volume heat equals this; constant-pressure heat is 5RΔT/2≈207.9 J. The difference ≈83.1 J is expansion work.

</LessonSection>
