<LessonSection id="story" title="Why the question matters" layer="story">

Interfaces and bounded geometry select transmitted amplitudes and allowed modes. Boundary conditions can create a cutoff absent from a free-space plane wave.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="concept" title="Definitions and hypotheses" layer="concept">

Reflection/refraction follow electromagnetic interface conditions. In a uniform guide, transverse mode structure determines the longitudinal propagation constant.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="theory" title="Derivation, proof and limitations" layer="theory" fullOnly>

At a charge/current-free interface the appropriate tangential E,H components are continuous. At normal incidence between lossless media with real impedances Z₁,Z₂, electric amplitude reflection is $r=(Z_2-Z_1)/(Z_2+Z_1)$ and transmission is t=1+r. Power reflection is |r|², while power transmission includes an impedance factor and is not simply |t|² in unequal media.

For isotropic nondispersive positive-index media, phase matching along the interface gives n₁sinθ₁=n₂sinθ₂. The polarization-resolved Fresnel coefficients follow the same boundary equations. Total internal reflection from higher to lower index produces an evanescent transmitted field, not a declaration of zero field on the second side. Lossy or anisotropic interfaces require more general analysis.

A simple perfectly conducting rectangular guide has allowed transverse wavenumbers set by its dimensions and TE/TM boundary conditions. For a mode with transverse k_c, $k_z^2=(\omega/c)^2-k_c^2$. Below ω_c=ck_c the longitudinal dependence is evanescent. A hollow simply connected ideal guide has no ordinary TEM mode; a two-conductor transmission structure can support one.

Above cutoff, the modal phase and group velocities differ, with their product c² for this ideal vacuum-guide dispersion. Energy flow remains compatible with causality. Wall loss, finite conductivity, bends and mode conversion modify the ideal model. A boundary-generated cutoff should not be confused with a material absorption resonance.


### Derive, test and transfer

At a stationary planar interface, tangential phase matching gives n₁sinθ₁=n₂sinθ₂ for simple isotropic media. Frequency stays fixed; propagation direction and wavelength change. At normal incidence between lossless nonmagnetic media, intensity reflectance is [(n₁−n₂)/(n₁+n₂)]² under the usual field/impedance assumptions.

Total internal reflection occurs from the higher-index side when the transmitted angle would require sine greater than one. The transmitted field is evanescent rather than identically absent at the boundary. A waveguide adds spatial boundary conditions and mode cutoffs; a single refraction angle is not its whole spectrum. Keep amplitude coefficients distinct from power coefficients when impedances differ.

</LessonSection>
