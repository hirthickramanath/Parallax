How can a subsystem have a well-defined average particle number without a fixed particle count? State a prediction and a reason before reading the argument.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="story" title="Why the question matters" layer="story">

Particle exchange is essential for chemical equilibrium, radiation and dense matter. Chemical potential is the variable conjugate to particle number, not an extra mechanical force.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="concept" title="Definitions and hypotheses" layer="concept">

At fixed T,V,μ the grand canonical weight is proportional to exp[−β(E−μN)] and the grand partition function sums over particle sectors and their states.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="theory" title="Derivation, proof and limitations" layer="theory" fullOnly>

A large reservoir exchanging energy and particles has entropy expansion S_b(E_tot−E,N_tot−N)≈constant−E/T+μN/T. Therefore $\Xi=\sum_{N,i}\exp[-\beta(E_{i,N}-\mu N)]$ normalizes subsystem probabilities. State counting, species labels and particle statistics must be specified before evaluating the sum.

The grand potential is $\mathcal\Omega=-k_BT\ln\Xi=U-TS-\mu\langle N\rangle$. For one species, dΩ=−S dT−p dV−N dμ in the thermodynamic setting, and N=k_BT ∂lnΞ/∂μ. At fixed T, $\operatorname{Var}(N)=k_BT(\partial\langle N\rangle/\partial\mu)_{T,V}$ for the corresponding fixed microscopic model. A susceptibility is therefore tied to exchange fluctuations.

For a homogeneous extensive bulk system without significant boundary or nonadditive contributions, Ω=−pV. This equality is not universal for every finite trap or gravitating system. The average N can be noninteger although each observed count is integer. A finite reservoir or a strict global conservation constraint changes the subsystem fluctuation model.

For one state that permits zero or one particle at energy ε, Ξ=1+z e⁻ᵝε with fugacity z=eᵝμ. Its occupation is a/(1+a), a=z e⁻ᵝε. Bosonic multiple occupation instead produces a geometric series with a convergence restriction. Choosing the allowed state space is what distinguishes these examples; one cannot swap their formulas while keeping the same counting assumptions.


### Derive, test and transfer

The grand canonical weight is proportional to exp[−β(E−μN)], so chemical potential couples to particle number rather than simply shifting every energy identically. For one spinless fermionic level ε with occupations zero or one, the grand partition factor is 1+exp[−β(ε−μ)] and mean occupation is 1/(exp[β(ε−μ)]+1).

An equal mean occupation need not imply fixed particle number: fluctuations remain except in special limits. The ensemble is appropriate to exchange with a reservoir under specified constraints. Bosonic modes have a different sum and convergence condition; substituting a sign without checking ε−μ can create an invalid occupation. The investigation isolates one fermionic mode rather than a full interacting gas.

</LessonSection>
