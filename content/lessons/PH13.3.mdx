</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="theory" title="Derivation, proof and limitations" layer="theory" fullOnly>

The energy release is $Q=(M_i-M_f)c^2$ with constituent conventions matched. Positive Q does not remove Coulomb barriers, angular-momentum constraints or weak-interaction bottlenecks. Endothermic threshold in a laboratory frame can exceed |Q| because momentum conservation requires final recoil.

A cross section converts incident flux and target count into an expected reaction rate under a specified geometry. Thermal averages integrate σv over relative velocities; one resonant energy does not represent an entire thermal population.

Fusion can release binding energy for suitable light-nucleus combinations, while fission can release energy from suitable heavy configurations. Products, prompt/delayed particles and neutron economy matter. A fission chain’s multiplication factor summarizes a modelled neutron balance, not every microscopic event.

Safety, shielding and regulated experimental conditions are real requirements for actual nuclear work. This lesson stays with theoretical energy/rate bookkeeping and does not provide apparatus construction or operating procedures. Astrophysical rates require plasma/environment corrections and networks.


### Derivation and transfer

Reaction Q equals the initial minus final rest energy. Positive Q releases energy but does not remove a Coulomb barrier or guarantee a rapid reaction. Fusion and fission yields depend on products, branching and kinetic conditions.

The display converts a signed mass defect to Q using a chosen c² scale. It is not a cross-section or stellar reaction-network calculation. A laboratory threshold for an endothermic fixed-target reaction also includes recoil and momentum conservation.

</LessonSection>
