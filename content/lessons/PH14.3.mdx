<LessonSection id="story" title="Why the question matters" layer="story">

Fermi occupation restricts thermally active states, while transport depends on response and scattering near the Fermi surface.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="concept" title="Definitions and hypotheses" layer="concept">

The free-electron model treats noninteracting fermions with a quadratic dispersion in a stated volume/boundary convention.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="theory" title="Derivation, proof and limitations" layer="theory" fullOnly>

Counting momentum states gives $k_F=(3\pi^2n)^{1/3}$ for spin degeneracy two in a uniform three-dimensional gas. The Fermi energy is ℏ²k_F²/(2m). More general degeneracy g changes the count; the formula is not dimension independent.

At T≪T_F, Pauli occupancy confines thermal excitations near the Fermi surface, giving a leading electronic heat capacity proportional to T in the ideal model. Equipartition over all electrons would overestimate it. Deep occupied states still contribute to total ground energy.

A relaxation-time Drude-like conductivity is σ=ne²τ/m in the elementary approximation. It treats scattering phenomenologically and does not explain every microscopic resistance mechanism. Magnetotransport and optical response add frequency/field dependence.

Band structure, interactions and disorder modify density of states and effective masses. A fitted relaxation time is not a proof of classical independent trajectories, and low-temperature phase transitions can invalidate the free-metal picture. Compare heat and transport observables within one consistent state-counting model.


### Derivation and transfer

Fermi–Dirac occupation is 1/[exp((E−μ)/(kT))+1], bounded by one per spin-orbital. At E=μ it equals one half for positive temperature. The density of states must also be included to convert occupation into particle counts.

The display uses k=1 and energy relative to μ. It does not calculate a material chemical potential or equate this occupation curve with the density of states. At zero temperature the limiting step needs separate handling.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="worked" title="A worked argument" layer="worked" fullOnly>

For normalized n=3,e=1,τ=2,m=2, Drude conductivity is 3. If temperature doubles within the same ideal low-T regime, the electronic heat capacity doubles, rather than staying at a classical constant per electron.
