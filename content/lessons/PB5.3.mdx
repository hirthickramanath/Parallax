<LessonSection id="story" title="Why this matters" layer="story">

Semiconductors connect quantum band structure with detectors and electronic circuits. Astronomical cameras exploit carrier generation by photons; their dark noise and efficiency depend on material and device conditions.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="intuition" title="Build the picture" layer="intuition">

Available states and their occupancy both matter. A nearly full band can be described by the motion of missing electrons, called holes.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="concept" title="State the model" layer="concept">

A semiconductor has a valence band and conduction band separated by a gap. Thermal excitation, light and dopants change mobile electron and hole populations.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="theory" title="Derive and qualify the result" layer="theory" fullOnly>

In a simple uniform low-field model, conductivity is $\sigma=q(n\mu_n+p\mu_p)$, where n and p are carrier densities and the mobilities relate drift speed to field. Resistivity is 1/σ for an isotropic ohmic material. Mobility depends on scattering and temperature, so changing temperature affects more than carrier count.

Donor impurities can supply electrons to the conduction band, producing n-type material; acceptors favour holes and p-type material. These labels do not mean the bulk crystal is macroscopically charged. Charge neutrality includes fixed ionized dopants. Under nondegenerate equilibrium assumptions, np equals the square of intrinsic carrier density; illumination or heavy doping requires more careful treatment.

At a p–n junction, carrier diffusion exposes ionized dopants and creates a depletion region with an electric field. Equilibrium drift and diffusion balance, giving no net external current without an applied bias. Forward bias reduces the effective barrier; reverse bias widens depletion until leakage or breakdown becomes relevant.

An idealized diode follows $I=I_s[\exp(qV/(\eta kT))-1]$ in a suitable regime, with ideality factor η representing the model’s recombination mechanism. Series resistance, high injection and breakdown limit it. A diode is not a perfect zero-threshold switch with one universal 0.7 V drop.

Photons with suitable energy can generate electron-hole pairs. A depletion field separates carriers, allowing photodiodes to detect light. Quantum efficiency, recombination, dark current and read noise influence real detectors. A gap energy gives a long-wavelength cutoff scale λ≈hc/Eg, but does not alone determine the detector’s full response curve.


### Transfer derivation and failure case

A doped bulk can remain approximately charge-neutral because ionized donors balance mobile electrons. Depletion regions and junction potentials require solving charge and field together; a uniform-neutral-bulk approximation cannot describe a junction everywhere.

### Derivation and transfer

An ideal diode obeys I=Is[exp(V/(nVT))−1] within its applicable regime. Forward bias gives a rapidly increasing current, but a real circuit includes series resistance, heating and breakdown limits. The thermal-voltage scale depends on temperature.

The display uses voltage normalized by VT and current normalized by Is. It does not predict a particular component without parameters or replace a safe operating limit. A band gap alone does not determine every device characteristic.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="worked" title="A worked example" layer="worked" fullOnly>

If n doubles while mobility stays fixed and electron conduction dominates, conductivity doubles and resistivity halves. If mobility simultaneously halves, the conductivity is unchanged. Carrier density alone is therefore insufficient to predict a temperature trend.
