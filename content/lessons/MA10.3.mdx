<LessonSection id="concept" title="Definitions and hypotheses" layer="concept">

Use commutative rings with identity. A field is a nonzero such ring in which every nonzero element has a multiplicative inverse.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="theory" title="Derivation, proof and limitations" layer="theory" fullOnly>

A zero divisor is a nonzero element whose product with another nonzero element is zero. In Z₆, 2×3=0 modulo six, so cancelling a factor two from an equation is unsafe. A field has no nonzero zero divisors because multiplying by an inverse would contradict nonzero status.

An element of Z_n is invertible exactly when its integer representative is coprime to n, by Bézout’s identity. Thus $\mathbb Z_p$ is a field when p is prime. The prime condition is not cosmetic: composite moduli supply nontrivial factor products equal to zero.

An ideal absorbs multiplication by arbitrary ring elements and permits a quotient ring. For a commutative ring with identity, quotient by a maximal ideal is a field under the standard proper-ideal hypotheses. A subgroup of the additive group need not automatically be an ideal.

Polynomial coefficients and base field determine factorization and roots. A polynomial irreducible over the reals can factor over the complex numbers; changing fields changes the algebraic question. Physical matrix equations also need an actual invertibility condition before division by an operator or determinant.


### Derivation and transfer

In a field every nonzero element has a multiplicative inverse. The integers modulo a prime form a field, while composite moduli can have zero divisors. In Z/6Z, 2·3=0 even though neither factor is zero.

The display multiplies residue representatives by two. Noninjectivity for an even modulus exposes a zero divisor; it does not classify every finite ring. Addition and multiplication, identity conventions and the chosen modulus must be fixed before making a field claim.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="worked" title="A worked argument" layer="worked" fullOnly>

In Z₆ only residues 1 and 5 are units, so there are two. In Z₅ all four nonzero residues are invertible. The equation 2x=2 modulo six has x=1 and x=4, demonstrating failed cancellation.
