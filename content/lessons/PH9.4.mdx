<LessonSection id="story" title="Why the question matters" layer="story">

Variational and semiclassical methods supply different kinds of approximations and must not be presented as equally rigorous error guarantees.

<LessonSection id="theory" title="Derivation, proof and limitations" layer="theory" fullOnly>

Expand a normalized trial state in a suitable spectral representation. Its energy is a positive-weight average, so $\langle H\rangle\ge E_0$. Optimizing parameters improves the bound within the chosen family, not necessarily the exact wavefunction. An approximate Hamiltonian gives a bound for that Hamiltonian, not automatically the physical system.

Excited-state variational principles require orthogonality/min–max conditions; a single unconstrained trial function usually optimizes toward the ground state. Boundary conditions and normalization cannot be omitted from trial admissibility.

WKB approximates slowly varying differential equations away from turning points. For a smooth one-dimensional bound orbit with two simple turning points, the leading action condition is $\oint p\,dx\approx2\pi\hbar(n+1/2)$ under matching assumptions. Hard walls, singular endpoints or different topology change the phase contribution.

A variational upper bound and a WKB estimate have different guarantee structures. Compare with exact/control limits, check turning-point connections and avoid calling a truncated semiclassical expression an exact inequality. Degeneracy and multiple coupled channels require extensions.


### Derivation and transfer

The variational principle bounds a ground energy from above using normalized trial states in the Hamiltonian domain. For a harmonic oscillator with ℏ=m=ω=1 and Gaussian width parameter a, E(a)=(a+1/a)/4, minimized at a=1 with E=1/2.

A lower trial energy is meaningful only for the same Hamiltonian and comparable approximation. WKB has a separate semiclassical regime and turning-point caveat. The display is an exact benchmark of one trial family, not an executed molecular calculation.

</LessonSection>
