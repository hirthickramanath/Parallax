<LessonSection id="concept" title="Definitions and hypotheses" layer="concept">

A fixed point satisfies f(x*)=0. Linearization studies the Jacobian there; invariant sets, bifurcations and Lyapunov spectra extend the analysis under stated regularity.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="theory" title="Derivation, proof and limitations" layer="theory" fullOnly>

For ẋ=f(x), perturbations near a fixed point satisfy $\delta\dot x=J\delta x$ at leading order. Eigenvalues with negative real parts give local asymptotic stability under the hyperbolic equilibrium theorem; positive real parts imply instability. Zero-real-part eigenvalues require nonlinear analysis rather than an automatic stable/unstable label.

For a planar continuous-time autonomous smooth flow, Poincaré–Bendixson-type conclusions have boundedness and equilibrium qualifications and do not transfer to arbitrary dimension. Forced systems can be made autonomous by adding variables, changing the dimension relevant to such theorems.

A Lyapunov exponent measures trajectory-wise infinitesimal growth with norm/time conventions. Lyapunov functions instead provide a scalar monotonicity stability argument; similar names do not make them the same object. Numerical finite-time exponents need tangent integration, rescaling and convergence checks.

Bifurcation theory asks how qualitative invariant structure changes with parameters. A computed diagram can miss unstable branches or exhibit discretization artifacts. A reproducible dynamical claim records equations, parameters, initial states, integration controls and the precise local/global assertion being tested.


### Derivation and transfer

For the logistic map xn+1=r xn(1−xn), fixed points are zero and 1−1/r when the latter exists. Linear stability depends on |f′(x*)|&lt;1; at the nonzero fixed point f′=2−r. Thus its stable range is 1&lt;r&lt;3.

The display plots the one-step map, not a long trajectory or Lyapunov estimate. Sensitive dependence requires a dynamical diagnostic; a nonlinear-looking curve alone does not establish chaos. Finite precision can alter long iterates.

</LessonSection>
