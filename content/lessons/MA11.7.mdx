<LessonSection id="story" title="Why the question matters" layer="story">

Bundles describe local state spaces and their transition rules; connections compare fibres while curvature measures their local transport structure.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="intuition" title="Build a mental model" layer="intuition">

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="concept" title="Definitions and hypotheses" layer="concept">

A fibre bundle locally resembles base space times a typical fibre, with compatible transition maps on overlaps. A connection specifies transport/comparison between nearby fibres.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="theory" title="Derivation, proof and limitations" layer="theory" fullOnly>

Local trivializations describe the same total bundle over overlapping base patches. Transition functions must satisfy consistency on triple overlaps. A global product structure is an additional property; local product appearance alone does not prove a bundle trivial.

A gauge choice is a local frame/trivialization. With a matrix connection convention, $F=dA+A\wedge A$ is curvature, and a gauge transformation uses the matching inhomogeneous connection term. In an abelian convention the commutator wedge term vanishes, leaving F=dA locally. Factors of coupling/i depend on the physical convention.

Parallel transport along a path gives holonomy, which depends on connection and path and transforms under endpoint frame changes. Closed-loop traces or suitable abelian phase combinations can be gauge invariant. A local potential is not itself a unique directly observed field.

Global flux/topology may require several patches even when the curvature is regular on the base. Quantization statements require the specified bundle and charge/representation assumptions, not only a sketch of a magnetic line. This introduction supplies geometric vocabulary; nonabelian gauge dynamics and topological classifications need the later field theory and topology hypotheses.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="interactive" title="Investigate the claim" layer="interactive">

Use two overlapping patches to describe a simple phase-valued fibre and check overlap consistency. Compare an abelian gauge shift with the curvature flux, then state what additional conventions would be needed for a matrix-valued connection.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="worked" title="A worked argument" layer="worked" fullOnly>

For an abelian local potential A=x dy on a unit square, curvature F=dx∧dy has flux 1. Replacing A by A+dχ leaves F unchanged because d²χ=0. Two overlapping descriptions must use a compatible transition rule.

</LessonSection>
