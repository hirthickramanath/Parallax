<LessonSection id="theory" title="Derivation, proof and limitations" layer="theory" fullOnly>

The linear density-wave relation is $\omega^2=c_s^2k^2-4\pi G\rho$. Modes with negative ω² grow in this ideal setup; the critical wavenumber is √(4πGρ)/c_s. The associated mass scale is proportional to c_s³/(G³ᐟ²ρ¹ᐟ²), with a numerical coefficient depending on the chosen length-to-mass definition.

Cooling can reduce pressure support and change the effective equation of state as gas compresses. The free-fall time for a pressureless uniform sphere is √(3π/(32Gρ)). Real clouds are neither pressureless nor uniform, so this is a dynamical reference time rather than an exact prediction for every parcel.

Angular momentum leads to discs, while magnetic stresses, turbulence and feedback alter collapse and fragmentation. A virial parameter compares terms under a geometry/velocity convention but does not independently prove boundedness or future star formation. External pressure and surface terms matter.

Protostellar radiation, outflows and the onset of nuclear burning affect accretion. Fragmentation requires thermodynamics and scale resolution; unresolved numerical fragmentation can be an artifact. An observed cold cloud is not necessarily a single isolated Jeans sphere, and model-generated core populations are not direct measurements of a stellar initial-mass function.


### Derive, test and transfer

A sound crossing time is proportional to R/c_s, while a gravitational collapse time is proportional to $(G\rho)^{-1/2}$. Equating scales yields a Jeans length proportional to $c_s/\sqrt{G\rho}$ and mass proportional to $c_s^3/(G^{3/2}\rho^{1/2})$. For fixed composition in an isothermal ideal gas, $c_s\propto\sqrt T$, so $M_J\propto T^{3/2}\rho^{-1/2}$.

Cooling and compression can therefore lower the characteristic fragmentation mass, but a real cloud also has magnetic fields, turbulence, rotation and finite boundaries. The familiar uniform-medium derivation idealizes the background gravity and does not prove every cloud above one nominal mass will collapse unchanged into a single star. Compare pressure support, cooling time and gravitational time before extending the scaling into an evolutionary prediction.

</LessonSection>

In normalized dispersion units with c_s²k²=3 and 4πGρ=7, ω²=−4, giving an ideal exponential growth rate 2. At fixed sound speed, quadrupling density halves the Jeans mass scaling.



### Worked investigation: A Jeans-scale sensitivity test

A pressure-restored perturbation has a sound-crossing time of order lambda/c_s, while self-gravity acts on a time of order 1/sqrt(G*rho). Equating scales suggests lambda_J proportional to c_s/sqrt(G*rho) and M_J proportional to c_s³/(G^(3/2)*sqrt(rho)). For an isothermal ideal gas at fixed composition, c_s is proportional to sqrt(T), so M_J scales as T^(3/2)*rho^(-1/2).

If T increases by four and density by sixteen, the Jeans mass changes by 4^(3/2)/sqrt(16)=2. Increased density alone would lower the threshold, but heating can reverse that tendency. This is a linear, uniform-medium diagnostic; turbulence, magnetic fields, boundaries, rotation and cooling require more than one critical number. Numerical prefactors depend on the defined geometry and wavelength convention.
