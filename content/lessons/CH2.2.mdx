</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="theory" title="Derivation, proof and limitations" layer="theory" fullOnly>

The molecular Hamiltonian can be expanded in electronic states parameterized by nuclear positions. Neglecting specified derivative couplings yields the familiar single-surface nuclear equation. The electron/nucleus mass separation motivates the approximation but does not ensure it at every crossing or degeneracy.

Near an equilibrium geometry, $V\approx V_0+\tfrac12\Delta R^TK\Delta R$. Mass weighting of the Hessian gives normal-mode frequencies after removing the appropriate translations/rotations. A stationary point with a negative mode is not a stable minimum.

At conical intersections or strong nonadiabatic coupling, one surface can be inadequate. Adiabatic versus diabatic representations distribute couplings differently; they are not arbitrary different molecules. Reaction paths also need an initial state, environment and dynamics, not merely a saddle-point height.

Zero-point motion, tunnelling and finite-temperature ensembles change nuclear behaviour relative to one optimized geometry. A computed electronic barrier and an observed reaction rate need not be related by a bare classical Boltzmann factor without prefactors, recrossing and state information.


### Derive, test and transfer

Born–Oppenheimer separation treats electronic states at fixed nuclear geometry before following nuclear motion on a potential surface. Small electron-to-nuclear mass scale helps, but adiabatic following also depends on electronic gaps and couplings. Near an avoided crossing, a small gap can make neglected nonadiabatic transitions important.

A two-state toy electronic Hamiltonian with diagonal entries ±R and coupling V has energies ±sqrt(R²+V²), so the minimum gap is 2|V|. This illustrates geometry-dependent surfaces without integrating nuclei or predicting a transition probability. Heavy nuclei alone do not justify ignoring every electronic crossing, especially when symmetry and coupled surfaces matter.

</LessonSection>
