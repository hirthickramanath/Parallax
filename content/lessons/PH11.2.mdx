Why is momentum no longer simply mass times velocity at high speed? State a prediction and a reason before reading the argument.

<LessonSection id="story" title="Why the question matters" layer="story">

Relativistic dynamics combines energy and momentum into a four-vector. It preserves conservation laws across inertial frames and distinguishes invariant mass from frame-dependent kinetic energy.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="intuition" title="Build a mental model" layer="intuition">

Energy is the temporal partner of momentum. Their separate values change with frame, while a particular quadratic combination identifies the invariant mass of the system.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="concept" title="Definitions and hypotheses" layer="concept">

For invariant mass m and four-velocity U=dx/dτ, four-momentum is P=mU=(E/c,p). Massive particles have E=γmc² and p=γmv.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="theory" title="Derivation, proof and limitations" layer="theory" fullOnly>

With signature (+,−,−,−), $P\cdot P=(E/c)^2-|p|^2=m^2c^2$, giving $E^2=p^2c^2+m^2c^4$. At rest E₀=mc². Kinetic energy is $K=(\gamma-1)mc^2$, recovering mv²/2 when v≪c through the binomial expansion. Mass here remains invariant; the phrase relativistic mass is unnecessary for these formulas.

For a photon, m=0 and E=pc with E=hν. Zero invariant mass does not mean zero energy or momentum. A collection of photons can have positive total invariant mass if their momenta do not all align: compute the norm of the total four-momentum, not a sum of individual rest masses.

Three-force is $F=dp/dt$. For constant invariant mass, its power is $dE/dt=F\cdot v$. Parallel and transverse acceleration responses differ because differentiating γv couples acceleration direction to velocity. The four-force dP/dτ is orthogonal to U when m is constant, since differentiating U·U=c² gives U·dU/dτ=0.

For an isolated interacting system, total four-momentum is conserved when all relevant particles and field contributions are included. Binding energy can reduce a composite invariant mass relative to the sum of separated constituent masses. Rest energy is not a guarantee that every energy transfer changes one particle's invariant mass; acceleration of a stable particle changes E and p while m stays fixed.

A boost along x transforms energy and parallel momentum as $E'=\gamma(E-vp_x)$ and $p_x'=\gamma(p_x-vE/c^2)$. These relations support frame-independent conservation checks. Subtracting energies from one frame and momenta from another breaks the four-vector bookkeeping and can manufacture an apparent violation.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="interactive" title="Investigate the claim" layer="interactive">

Compute E/(mc²), p/(mc) and their invariant for several speeds. Compare K with the Newtonian approximation at small β. Then add four-momenta of two opposite equal-energy photons to see a massive composite despite each individual mass being zero.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="worked" title="A worked argument" layer="worked" fullOnly>

For a massive particle moving at 0.6c, γ=1.25. Energy is 1.25mc², kinetic energy 0.25mc² and momentum 0.75mc. The invariant check is 1.25²−0.75²=1 in units normalized by m and c.

</LessonSection>
