<LessonSection id="concept" title="Definitions and hypotheses" layer="concept">

f-block descriptions involve partially occupied f-derived states, with different localisation and coupling trends in lanthanides and actinides.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="theory" title="Derivation, proof and limitations" layer="theory" fullOnly>

An f subshell has orbital label $\ell=3$ and 2ℓ+1=7 magnetic orbitals before spin; including two spin projections gives capacity 14. This state count does not decide the ground configuration or oxidation state of every species.

Spin–orbit and electron interactions set total angular character. Shielded lanthanide f states can retain relatively atomic-like patterns in some environments, while actinide covalency and relativistic effects can be more prominent. These are trends with exceptions, not a universal localised-versus-delocalised binary rule.

Magnetic response can include substantial orbital contributions and crystal-field splitting. A spin-only moment estimate can fail. Optical intensities and line patterns depend on symmetry, mixing, thermal populations and selection rules.

Distinguish stable electronic chemistry from nuclear radioactivity: being f-block is not itself one specific half-life. Actual handling of radioactive substances is outside this theoretical unit. Spectral claims need calibrated references and context, not inference from a periodic-table block alone.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="worked" title="A worked argument" layer="worked" fullOnly>

For ℓ=3 there are seven orbital m values and fourteen spin-orbitals in a single f subshell. Half filling is seven electrons in the elementary capacity count, not an automatic spin/magnetic-moment prediction for every environment.

</LessonSection>
