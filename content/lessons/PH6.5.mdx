<LessonSection id="theory" title="Derivation, proof and limitations" layer="theory" fullOnly>

Stimulated emission adds a field contribution into the stimulating mode in the elementary transition picture, while absorption competes with it. A population inversion is defined with the relevant level degeneracies and transition populations. A closed two-level system under ordinary resonant pumping cannot sustain arbitrary inversion in its simplest steady-state rate model; practical schemes use additional structure.

For intensity gain coefficient g and distributed loss α over length L with mirror intensity reflectivities R₁,R₂, threshold is $R_1R_2e^{2(g-\alpha)L}=1$. This small-signal round-trip balance includes output coupling. Above threshold, gain saturation and mode competition matter; infinite exponential growth is not a physical steady laser output.

Cavity resonances constrain longitudinal/transverse modes, but linewidth, coherence and noise depend on dynamics, pumping and environment. A narrow classical spectrum alone does not identify a unique quantum photon state. Ideal coherent light has Poisson photon-number statistics, while a single thermal mode has enhanced number fluctuations.

The normalized equal-time second-order correlation g²(0)=⟨n(n−1)⟩/⟨n⟩² distinguishes ideal examples: one for a coherent state, two for a single thermal mode and zero for an ideal one-photon state. Multimode averaging, detector timing and inefficiency affect measured values. Antibunching is a nonclassical signature in the stated correlation setting, not a claim that every laser output is exactly coherent.


### Derive, test and transfer

For an incoherently pumped ideal two-level population model, upward rate R acts on the lower population while stimulated downward rate R and spontaneous decay Γ act on the upper population. At steady state, R(1−pe)=(R+Γ)pe, yielding pe=R/(2R+Γ). It approaches one half from below and does not invert this simple two-level system.

A working laser uses a suitable gain mechanism, population preparation and optical feedback; multilevel schemes can evade the simple two-level pump limitation. Threshold compares gain with cavity losses, while saturation controls above-threshold behavior. The investigation intentionally tests the noninverting toy rate equation, not a complete laser spectrum or coherent drive dynamics.

</LessonSection>
