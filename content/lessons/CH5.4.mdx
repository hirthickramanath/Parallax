<LessonSection id="theory" title="Derivation, proof and limitations" layer="theory" fullOnly>

An isolated silicate tetrahedral unit has formal composition SiO₄ with charge $4+4(-2)=-4$ under Si(+4),O(−2) bookkeeping. Sharing oxygen changes network stoichiometry: a fully connected silica network has SiO₂, not a collection of independent unchanged charged tetrahedra.

Condensation depends on pressure, elemental abundances, oxygen/carbon chemistry and kinetics. A condensation sequence is model dependent and can be altered by incomplete equilibrium, shocks, irradiation and transport. Refractory does not mean indestructible at every temperature.

Amorphous and crystalline grains with similar bulk elements can have different vibrational bands and optical constants. Grain shape, size, porosity and temperature affect absorption/emission; a feature alone does not uniquely specify total dust mass.

An opacity-based dust mass estimate is conditional on optical depth, temperature distribution and adopted material coefficients. Preserve laboratory optical-data provenance and distinguish synthetic spectra from observed mineral identification. This unit supports astrochemical prerequisites without adding a separate full geoscience course to Parallax.


### Derive, test and transfer

An isolated SiO₄ tetrahedron has four oxygens per silicon. When a tetrahedron has n bridging oxygen corners, each shared oxygen is counted one half per tetrahedron, giving average O/Si=4−n/2. Full corner sharing gives the SiO₂ framework ratio rather than four independent oxygens per silicon.

Charge-balancing cations, aluminum substitution and network topology add chemical information beyond that mean ratio. Grain condensation and survival also depend on thermodynamics and kinetics. The display relates a chosen mean network connectivity to an oxygen number density; it is not a full mineral structure or a computed astrophysical condensation sequence.

</LessonSection>
