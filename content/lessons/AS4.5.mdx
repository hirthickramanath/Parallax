<LessonSection id="concept" title="Definitions and hypotheses" layer="concept">

Core-collapse and thermonuclear supernovae arise from different evolutionary channels; spectroscopic classifications and physical mechanisms are related but not identical labels.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="theory" title="Derivation, proof and limitations" layer="theory" fullOnly>

In core collapse, loss of support in an evolved massive core leads to rapid contraction and a compact remnant, with neutrino transport and multidimensional dynamics central to the explosion problem. Gravitational binding energy provides a large reservoir, but only a fraction becomes ejecta kinetic energy; a simple bounce picture is not a complete mechanism.

Thermonuclear events involve explosive burning in a degenerate stellar setting, commonly modeled with white-dwarf progenitors and multiple possible ignition/accretion/merger channels. Burning conditions and expansion set products. Treating every Type Ia event as one uniquely established progenitor path overstates current inference.

Nucleosynthesis depends on temperature, density, electron fraction and reaction/expansion time scales. Hydrostatic burning, explosive burning and neutron-capture processes contribute differently. A nuclear network must conserve baryon number and track weak reactions and neutrino effects; one detected element does not identify a unique production event.

Radioactive decay can power late luminosity. For a single isolated decay population, $N(t)=N_0e^{-\lambda t}$ and activity λN, but a real light curve also depends on decay chains, energy deposition and escape. Dust, distance, extinction and asymmetry affect inferred yields. Distinguish observed spectral lines from model-derived ejecta masses.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="worked" title="A worked argument" layer="worked" fullOnly>

After one half-life a single radioactive population has half its nuclei; after three it has one eighth. If initial count is 80, the remaining count after three half-lives is 10. This does not automatically make bolometric luminosity proportional to the remaining count if trapping changes.

</LessonSection>
