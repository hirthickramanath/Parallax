<LessonSection id="puzzle" title="Commit to a prediction" layer="puzzle">

Why can an electrode current depend on transport even when its reaction is intrinsically fast? State a prediction and a reason before reading the argument.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="story" title="Why the question matters" layer="story">

Electrode measurements combine interfacial electron transfer with solution transport and circuit response. An apparent rate can be limited by a different step from the one a simple fit assumes.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="intuition" title="Build a mental model" layer="intuition">

Reactants must arrive at the interface and electrons must cross it. Speeding one part does not remove a bottleneck in the other.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="concept" title="Definitions and hypotheses" layer="concept">

Overpotential is electrode potential relative to the relevant equilibrium value under a declared convention. Current–overpotential models require reaction, area, reference and transport definitions.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="theory" title="Derivation, proof and limitations" layer="theory" fullOnly>

A common charge-transfer model is Butler–Volmer, $j=j_0[\exp(\alpha_anF\eta/RT)-\exp(-\alpha_cnF\eta/RT)]$, with the sign convention stated and suitable kinetic assumptions. In a simple elementary description the transfer coefficients can sum to one; more complex effective kinetics needs care before imposing that relation.

Near η=0, linearization gives $j\approx j_0(\alpha_a+\alpha_c)nF\eta/(RT)$. At a sufficiently large one-direction overpotential where one exponential dominates and transport remains negligible, a Tafel relation links ln|j| to η. A straight segment alone does not prove one unique elementary mechanism.

If reactant transport limits supply, a diffusion-limited current can arise. In a simple steady planar film model, $j_{\rm lim}=nFDc_{\rm bulk}/\delta$ when surface concentration approaches zero. The film thickness δ is a model parameter reflecting hydrodynamics and geometry, not a universal molecular length.

Measured cell voltage includes uncompensated ohmic drop and may include reference offsets or junction potentials. Current can also charge the double layer without net Faradaic conversion. In a potential sweep, transient charging, diffusion and kinetic response all contribute, so peak shape is not a direct equilibrium spectrum.

A useful experiment or synthetic model separates electrode area, mass transport, scan rate, temperature and reference electrode. Compare currents under changed transport conditions before assigning a limiting plateau to an intrinsic electron-transfer barrier. Fit only over a regime consistent with the assumptions.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="worked" title="A worked argument" layer="worked" fullOnly>

In a normalized Butler–Volmer example with j₀=1, αa=αc=1/2 and nFη/(RT)=2 ln2, current is exp(ln2)−exp(−ln2)=2−0.5=1.5. The cathodic term is not negligible at that value.
