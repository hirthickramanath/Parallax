Why is the most probable molecular speed smaller than the root-mean-square speed? State a prediction and a reason before reading the argument.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="story" title="Why the question matters" layer="story">

Molecular velocity statistics explain pressure, thermal broadening and collision scales. A speed histogram includes a geometric factor absent from one Cartesian velocity component.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="intuition" title="Build a mental model" layer="intuition">

Start with the concrete worked example below. Track which quantities belong to the system, which are imposed by its surroundings, and which conclusions require a limiting approximation.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="concept" title="Definitions and hypotheses" layer="concept">

A dilute classical ideal gas in equilibrium has independent Gaussian Cartesian velocity components; its speed distribution includes the spherical velocity-space measure.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="theory" title="Derivation, proof and limitations" layer="theory" fullOnly>

For particle mass m and temperature T, the normalized velocity density is $f(\mathbf v)=(m/2\pi k_BT)^{3/2}\exp[-m v^2/(2k_BT)]$. Rotational symmetry allows a velocity-space spherical shell of volume 4πv²dv, so the speed density is 4πv²f(v). A density per unit speed is not the same quantity as a density per unit Cartesian velocity volume.

Differentiating the speed density gives v_mp=√(2k_BT/m). Gaussian moments yield mean speed √(8k_BT/(πm)) and rms speed √(3k_BT/m). The tail weights larger speeds differently in these statistics. None is a universal drift velocity: an equilibrium gas has zero mean velocity vector even though its mean speed is positive.

Momentum transfer to an ideal wall gives $p=nm\langle v_x^2\rangle=nk_BT$, where n is number density. Equipartition assigns k_BT/2 per independent quadratic term under classical equilibrium assumptions. Frozen quantum modes, constraints and nonquadratic energies can invalidate naive degree counting. Elastic wall collisions and isotropy are part of the elementary derivation.

For hard spheres of collision cross-section σ, the equilibrium mean free path is approximately 1/(√2 nσ) in a dilute homogeneous gas. This is a model-specific average, not the distance every particle travels. Local thermodynamic equilibrium requires collision relaxation fast compared with relevant gradients and evolution. Rarefied gas, shocks, nonthermal plasma tails and relativistic particles require extensions beyond this Maxwell–Boltzmann model.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="worked" title="A worked argument" layer="worked" fullOnly>

In units with k_BT/m=4, v_mp=√8 and v_rms=√12. Their squares are 8 and 12. The mean velocity vector remains zero if no bulk flow is present; substituting v_rms for the vector mean would falsely predict a preferred direction.

</LessonSection>
