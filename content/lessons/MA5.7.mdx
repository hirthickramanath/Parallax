<LessonSection id="story" title="Why the question matters" layer="story">

Green functions connect diffusion, static potentials and forced waves. Operator signs, boundaries, causality and zero modes determine which kernel is appropriate.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="intuition" title="Build a mental model" layer="intuition">

Break a source into tiny weighted contributions, propagate each with the chosen point-source response and add them. The walls and time direction are part of that response.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="concept" title="Definitions and hypotheses" layer="concept">

A Green kernel inverts a linear operator with specified conditions through LₓG(x,ξ)=δ(x−ξ), where an inverse exists or is defined on a constrained subspace.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="theory" title="Derivation, proof and limitations" layer="theory" fullOnly>

For a linear operator L, a source representation $u(x)=\int_\Omega G(x,\xi)f(\xi)d\xi$ solves Lu=f only when the kernel, function spaces and interchange of operations are justified and the boundary/initial conditions are appropriate. Nonhomogeneous boundary data generally add a separate boundary or lifting contribution. The delta is a distribution characterized by its action on test functions, not an ordinary finite-valued point spike.

For L=−d²/dx² on 0&lt;x&lt;L with zero Dirichlet values, the Green function is $G(x,\xi)=\min(x,\xi)[L-\max(x,\xi)]/L$. It is continuous at x=ξ and piecewise linear. Integrating −G″=δ across the source gives G′(ξ⁺)−G′(ξ⁻)=−1. The derivative jump sign follows from the chosen operator sign. This kernel is symmetric and nonnegative.

For a positive self-adjoint operator with a complete normalized eigenbasis and nonzero eigenvalues λₙ, a formal spectral inverse is $G(x,\xi)=\sum_n\phi_n(x)\overline{\phi_n(\xi)}/\lambda_n$. The series may need a weak or distributional interpretation rather than pointwise absolute convergence. A zero eigenvalue obstructs a full inverse: the source must be orthogonal to the nullspace, and a reference condition selects a solution on its complement. This is why a pure Neumann Laplacian requires compatibility and a constant-mode convention.

In three-dimensional free space, $-\Delta[1/(4\pi|x-\xi|)]=\delta(x-\xi)$ in the distributional sense. Away from the source the Laplacian vanishes; integrating the normal derivative over a small sphere fixes the normalization and sign. This free-space kernel does not automatically satisfy conducting-wall or other finite-domain boundary data.

A retarded evolution kernel vanishes before the source time. For diffusion it is the heat kernel for positive elapsed time; wave operators have causal propagation structures depending on dimension. A static inverse does not encode an initial velocity or a time direction. Reciprocity of a symmetric Green function requires self-adjoint structure and compatible boundary conditions; arbitrary advective, dissipative or non-self-adjoint operators need not have the same symmetry.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="worked" title="A worked argument" layer="worked" fullOnly>

For L=1 and x=ξ=1/2, the Dirichlet kernel is (1/2)(1−1/2)=1/4. At fixed ξ=1/2, the left derivative is 1/2 and the right derivative is −1/2, so the jump is −1. Integrating this kernel against f=2 recovers u=x(1−x), checking both source and boundary.

</LessonSection>
