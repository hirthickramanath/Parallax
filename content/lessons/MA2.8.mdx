<LessonSection id="puzzle" title="Commit to a prediction" layer="puzzle">

Why does radial flow change when its component stays constant? State a prediction and a reason before reading the argument.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="story" title="Why the question matters" layer="story">

Curvilinear coordinates simplify spherical and cylindrical physics, but their basis vectors and volume cells vary with position. Copying Cartesian derivative formulas omits that geometry.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="intuition" title="Build a mental model" layer="intuition">

A sphere gets larger with radius. Even the same radial vector magnitude crosses more area at a larger radius, changing the local flux balance.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="concept" title="Definitions and hypotheses" layer="concept">

In orthogonal coordinates qᵢ with scale factors hᵢ, a displacement is the sum of hᵢdqᵢ along local unit vectors. Differential operators must account for those scale factors and changing bases.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="theory" title="Derivation, proof and limitations" layer="theory" fullOnly>

For orthogonal coordinates, $ds^2=\sum_i h_i^2dq_i^2$ and $dV=h_1h_2h_3\,dq_1dq_2dq_3$. For scalar f, $\nabla f=\sum_i e_i(\partial f/\partial q_i)/h_i$. These eᵢ are local unit vectors, not generally fixed Cartesian vectors.

For a vector field with physical unit-basis components Aᵢ, flux through a small coordinate cell gives

$$\nabla\cdot A=\frac{1}{h_1h_2h_3}\sum_i\frac{\partial}{\partial q_i}\left(\frac{h_1h_2h_3}{h_i}A_i\right).$$

In cylindrical coordinates (s,φ,z), scale factors are (1,s,1), so divergence is $s^{-1}\partial_s(sA_s)+s^{-1}\partial_\phi A_\phi+\partial_zA_z$. In spherical coordinates (r,θ,φ), using θ from +z, scale factors are (1,r,r sinθ). A purely radial field then has divergence $r^{-2}d(r^2A_r)/dr$.

The scalar Laplacian is divergence of gradient, giving $\nabla^2f=(h_1h_2h_3)^{-1}\sum_i\partial_{q_i}[(h_1h_2h_3/h_i^2)\partial_{q_i}f]$. For radial spherical f this is $r^{-2}d(r^2f')/dr$. The radial component of a vector Laplacian is not generally the scalar radial Laplacian applied to that component; the basis directions also vary.

Curl can be derived from circulation around small coordinate loops. For cyclic (i,j,k) in a right-handed orthogonal system, $(\nabla\times A)_i=[\partial_{q_j}(h_kA_k)-\partial_{q_k}(h_jA_j)]/(h_jh_k)$. State whether components are in a unit basis or coordinate basis before using such a formula.

Coordinate axes and poles can be singular even when the physical field is regular. Conversely, an inverse-square field has a physical singularity at the origin that cannot be removed by renaming coordinates. Operator expressions valid at positive r do not establish what happens distributionally at r=0.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="worked" title="A worked argument" layer="worked" fullOnly>

For A=r eᵣ in spherical coordinates with r>0, divergence is r⁻² d(r³)/dr=3. For A=eᵣ/r² it is zero away from the origin, consistent with constant flux through concentric spheres but not with absence of a source at the excluded origin.

</LessonSection>
