<LessonSection id="concept" title="Definitions and hypotheses" layer="concept">

Gravitational lensing maps source angles to image angles through a deflection potential in an appropriate weak-field/thin-lens approximation.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="theory" title="Derivation, proof and limitations" layer="theory" fullOnly>

For a point lens, $\theta_E^2=(4GM/c^2)D_{ls}/(D_lD_s)$ with angular-diameter distances in a consistent geometry. A physical deflection angle and observed image angle are not the same quantity. Alignment produces an Einstein-ring scale in the ideal symmetric model.

Convergence and shear describe local magnification and distortion. Weak lensing infers ensemble shape changes rather than measuring one unlensed galaxy’s shape directly. Strong-lensing multiple images add constraints but require source identification and a mass model. Magnification, intrinsic shapes and selection all affect sample inference.

Lensing is sensitive to total projected gravitating effects and is not limited to luminous matter. A single-plane mass-sheet degeneracy can preserve some image observables under simultaneous source rescaling. Redshift distributions, multiple source planes and other information can help constrain degeneracies.

Clusters also provide X-ray gas and galaxy-dynamical probes. Hydrostatic X-ray mass estimates require thermal/pressure support assumptions and can be biased by turbulence or merger state. Combining independent probes helps but must account for correlated geometry, calibration and selection rather than treating every quoted mass as an independent direct measurement.


### Derive, test and transfer

For an isolated thin point lens, $\theta_E^2=(4GM/c^2)D_{ls}/(D_lD_s)$ in angular-diameter distances. At fixed geometry the angular scale is proportional to √M. A cluster lens has an extended mass distribution, shear and potentially multiple images; its lens mapping cannot be reduced to a point-mass radius in all cases.

Lensing depends on projected mass and distance geometry, while dynamical measurements probe different combinations. Degeneracies can preserve image observables while changing an inferred mass normalization unless additional constraints are supplied. The investigation tests the square-root scale only. It does not generate a cluster map, infer a cosmological parameter or reproduce an observed lens system.

</LessonSection>
