<LessonSection id="theory" title="Derivation, proof and limitations" layer="theory" fullOnly>

For independent equal-variance repeats, the mean standard error scales as $\sigma_{\bar x}=\sigma/\sqrt N$. A common calibration offset is correlated and does not fall by that rule. Distinguish repeatability scatter, resolution, drift and systematic model error.

For a derived f(x), first-order covariance propagation is Var(f)≈∇fᵀC∇f under a sufficiently linear local approximation. Correlated inputs can increase or reduce uncertainty. Nonlinearity, bounds and asymmetric distributions may require direct likelihood or Monte Carlo propagation.

A pendulum experiment measures length from pivot to the appropriate mass centre and times a stated number of small-amplitude cycles. Gate alignment, reaction delay, finite amplitude, air drag and pivot effects can bias a simple T=2π√(L/g) model. Observing apparatus and recording controls is part of the evidence, not decoration.

Use calibration/check standards, repeats across settings and residual analysis before inferring a parameter. Report significant digits according to uncertainty, not every displayed calculator digit. Synthetic bench readings verify a model workflow but do not establish a new physical measurement of local g.


### Derivation and transfer

Independent standard uncertainties combine in quadrature for a linear sum, while correlated contributions introduce covariance terms. A systematic offset shared by repeated measurements does not shrink as independent noise under averaging.

The display varies two standard deviations and assumes independence. Real reports must state calibration, units, uncertainty type and propagation method. A small displayed error bar is not evidence that an unmodeled bias is absent.

</LessonSection>
