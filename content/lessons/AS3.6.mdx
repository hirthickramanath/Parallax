<LessonSection id="concept" title="Definitions and hypotheses" layer="concept">

A one-dimensional quasi-static stellar model evolves enclosed mass, pressure, luminosity and temperature, with EOS, opacity, energy generation and composition closures.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="theory" title="Derivation, proof and limitations" layer="theory" fullOnly>

The spherical equations include dm/dr=4πr²ρ, dp/dr=−Gmρ/r² and $dL/dr=4\pi r^2\rho\epsilon_{net}$ when the local net energy term includes the appropriate heating, losses and gravothermal contribution for the evolution model. A transport law supplies dT/dr or its logarithmic gradient.

At the centre m and L vanish for a regular nonsingular model, while near-surface conditions match an atmosphere or a stated simplified boundary. Central densities/temperatures are not arbitrary independent guesses that guarantee a valid surface. Shooting, relaxation and mass-coordinate methods solve a coupled boundary-value problem with numerical conditioning and convergence checks.

For a polytropic approximation $p=K\rho^{1+1/n}$, a suitable radial/density scaling yields the Lane–Emden equation (ξ²θ′)′/ξ²=−θⁿ with θ(0)=1 and θ′(0)=0. The physical density is ρ_cθⁿ where the model remains valid. A polytropic index is not automatically the same as every adiabatic exponent of a realistic stratified star.

Dimensionless solutions must be scaled back using K,ρ_c,G and the chosen index. Surface zeros and finite-mass/radius behaviour depend on n. Composition gradients, ionization and changing transport can make a single polytrope only a qualitative approximation. Hydrostatic convergence alone does not verify an evolutionary track or observed stellar parameters.


### Derive, test and transfer

For $P=K\rho^{1+1/n}$ define $\rho=\rho_c\theta^n$ and r=αξ. Hydrostatic balance and mass conservation reduce to the Lane–Emden equation $(\xi^2\theta')'=-\xi^2\theta^n$ with regular-center conditions θ(0)=1 and θ′(0)=0. For n=1, substitution verifies $\theta=\sin\xi/\xi$, with its central value defined by a limit.

The first positive zero π defines the surface of this idealized polytrope. Enclosed dimensionless mass is $-\xi^2\theta'=\sin\xi-\xi\cos\xi$, giving π at the surface. The dimensional scale α depends on K, n, G and central density. A normalized curve alone therefore cannot specify a physical stellar mass or radius. Negative values beyond the first zero are not a physical continuation of a positive-density star.

</LessonSection>
