Induction joins electric and magnetic fields into a local dynamical theory, while a moving circuit requires care about what voltage means.

<LessonSection id="concept" title="Definitions and hypotheses" layer="concept">

In SI vacuum, Maxwell equations relate electric and magnetic fields to charge/current, and a charge experiences q(E+v×B). A loop emf includes the force on charges in the moving conductor.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="theory" title="Derivation, proof and limitations" layer="theory" fullOnly>

For a fixed oriented surface and boundary, Faraday’s law is $\oint\mathbf E\cdot d\mathbf l=-d\Phi_B/dt$. A moving wire instead has emf ∮(E+v_wire×B)·dl, and the flux rule must use the moving surface consistently. The minus sign is Lenz’s law with the chosen orientation, not a separate arbitrary sign choice for each segment.

The local equations are ∇·E=ρ/ε₀, ∇·B=0, ∇×E=−∂ₜB and ∇×B=μ₀J+μ₀ε₀∂ₜE. Taking the divergence of the final equation and using Gauss’s law gives charge continuity ∂ₜρ+∇·J=0. Dropping displacement current while retaining arbitrary time-dependent charge would violate that consistency.

Integral laws follow from divergence and Stokes theorems with appropriate regularity or distributional treatment of sheets. At an interface, normal B is continuous and a surface charge changes normal E by σ/ε₀ in vacuum; tangential field jumps must include surface currents and the relevant constitutive setting. A macroscopic D,H description separates bound and free sources by convention and needs constitutive relations.

Maxwell equations require initial data and boundary/radiation conditions; the differential laws alone do not pick a unique field everywhere. Electromagnetic disturbances propagate at c=1/√(μ₀ε₀) in vacuum. Quasistatic circuit approximations apply only when geometry, time scales and radiation justify them; a lumped voltage is not automatically a path-independent scalar potential in an inductive field.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="worked" title="A worked argument" layer="worked" fullOnly>

For a fixed loop with oriented flux increasing at 3 Wb/s, the oriented emf is −3 V. A capacitor gap with electric flux derivative 4 in normalized units ε₀=1 has displacement-current contribution 4; the gap does not require conduction charge crossing the vacuum.

</LessonSection>
