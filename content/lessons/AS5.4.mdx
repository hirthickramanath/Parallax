<LessonSection id="concept" title="Definitions and hypotheses" layer="concept">

Cosmic rays are energetic particles whose measured spectrum reflects source injection plus propagation, losses and detection selection.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="theory" title="Derivation, proof and limitations" layer="theory" fullOnly>

In an idealized first-order Fermi shock picture, scattering on either side can return particles across a velocity discontinuity. A cycle provides a fractional energy gain, while escape limits the number of cycles. If energy multiplies by g and survival by s per cycle, cumulative counts scale as $N(>E)\propto E^{\ln s/\ln g}$ in this simple steady multiplicative model.

The familiar strong nonrelativistic test-particle shock predictions require assumptions about compression, isotropic scattering, steady geometry and negligible particle feedback. Nonlinear acceleration changes the flow and spectrum. Injection from the thermal population is a separate physical issue.

A confinement argument requires a gyroradius not greatly exceeding an acceleration region scale; E, charge, field strength and geometry enter. This Hillas-type condition is necessary in a basic model, not sufficient for efficient acceleration. Loss and escape times must also permit the energy gain.

Galactic magnetic fields scramble charged-particle arrival directions; composition and secondary-to-primary ratios constrain propagation. Photons and neutrinos can test hadronic scenarios but carry their own source/interaction ambiguities. Solar modulation, detector response and energy calibration matter when comparing measured spectra. No synthetic power law in this exercise is an actual cosmic-ray measurement.


### Derive, test and transfer

The transverse Lorentz force bends a charged particle with gyroradius $r_L=p_\perp/(|q|B)$. A source of size R cannot easily confine particles whose gyro-orbits greatly exceed R. In relativistic units this yields a Hillas-type energy scale proportional to charge, field and size, but confinement alone supplies no acceleration mechanism.

Acceleration time must be shorter than escape and loss times. Synchrotron radiation, interactions and finite shock lifetimes can impose stricter limits. A measured cosmic-ray energy is not proof of a unique source class from a point on a B–R plot. The display varies momentum and field independently, while an actual accelerator calculation must specify the electric fields, scattering and transport that change the particle energy.

</LessonSection>
