<LessonSection id="story" title="Why this matters" layer="story">

Trigonometry connects angular measurements to lengths. Astronomical parallax and wave phase both require careful distinction between an angle, its unit and a periodic observable.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="intuition" title="Build the picture" layer="intuition">

On a unit circle, cosine is the horizontal coordinate and sine the vertical coordinate. Repeating a complete turn repeats both coordinates.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="concept" title="State the model" layer="concept">

Radians measure arc length divided by radius. The unit-circle definitions extend sine and cosine beyond right triangles.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="theory" title="Derive and qualify the result" layer="theory" fullOnly>

For a point $(\cos\theta,\sin\theta)$ on the unit circle, the distance equation gives $\sin^2\theta+\cos^2\theta=1$. Tangent is their ratio wherever cosine is nonzero. Sine and cosine have period $2\pi$, while tangent has period π on its admitted domain.

Composition of plane rotations gives $\cos(a+b)=\cos a\cos b-\sin a\sin b$ and $\sin(a+b)=\sin a\cos b+\cos a\sin b$. Setting b=a yields double-angle identities. Dividing an identity by sine or cosine may exclude genuine solutions if that factor vanishes; keep the excluded cases separately.

To solve $\sin\theta=s$, first require |s|≤1. With a principal arcsine α, the complete real solution consists of $\theta=\alpha+2\pi k$ and $\theta=\pi-\alpha+2\pi k$ for integer k. An inverse trigonometric function gives a principal value, not every angle solving the equation. Intersect the resulting family with the specified angular interval.

For angles measured in radians near zero, $\sin\theta\approx\theta$ and $\cos\theta\approx1-\theta^2/2$. These approximations follow from Taylor expansion and their first omitted terms scale as θ³ and θ⁴ respectively. A numerical angle stated in degrees must be converted before using these formulas. Small-angle parallax inherits the same restriction.

The cosine rule $c^2=a^2+b^2-2ab\cos C$ follows by resolving one side along the other and using the distance formula. It includes Pythagoras at C=π/2 and avoids assuming every triangle is right angled.


### Transfer derivation and failure case

A zero product is solved by retaining every factor: $\sin\theta\cos\theta=0$ implies either $\sin\theta=0$ or $\cos\theta=0$. Dividing by the second factor before considering its zeros loses valid solutions.

### Derivation and transfer

The identity sin²θ+cos²θ=1 constrains trigonometric equations, but inverse functions return a principal value rather than every solution. Solving sinθ=s requires both angular branches and periodic shifts where applicable.

The explorer varies a phase and amplitude without pretending a local graph gives all roots. Degrees and radians must not be mixed. Squaring an equation can introduce extraneous roots, so verify candidates in the original signed equation.

</LessonSection>
