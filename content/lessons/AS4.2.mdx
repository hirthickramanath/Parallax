<LessonSection id="concept" title="Definitions and hypotheses" layer="concept">

A theoretical HR diagram relates luminosity and effective temperature; observational colour-magnitude diagrams add distance, extinction, bandpass and calibration effects.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="theory" title="Derivation, proof and limitations" layer="theory" fullOnly>

The surface definition gives $L=4\pi R^2\sigma_{SB}T_{eff}^4$. At equal T_eff, a larger radius produces a larger luminosity. Colour is not automatically a direct thermometer without accounting for reddening, metallicity and atmospheric spectral response.

Main-sequence stars primarily balance core hydrogen fusion with losses over the appropriate quasi-static evolutionary interval. Their luminosity–mass relation depends on mass, composition and energy/transport regime; a single universal power is an approximation. The nuclear lifetime scale is available fuel divided by luminosity, not simply total rest-mass energy divided by luminosity.

If locally L∝M^α and usable fuel fraction is held fixed, the lifetime scales as M^(1−α). Massive stars can therefore be shorter lived despite containing more fuel. Composition, convective-core extent, rotation and mass exchange alter that estimate.

In a coeval population, the turnoff can constrain age through a model comparison, while unresolved binaries, distance, extinction and abundance spreads create degeneracies. An isochrone fit is conditional inference, not a unique age read directly from one colour. Main-sequence position alone does not specify every interior profile.


### Derive, test and transfer

Effective temperature is defined so the surface bolometric flux is $\sigma T_{eff}^4$. Multiplying by spherical area gives $L=4\pi R^2\sigma T_{eff}^4$. Taking ratios removes constants and yields $L/L_\odot=(R/R_\odot)^2(T/T_\odot)^4$. A radius can therefore be inferred only with temperature and luminosity, each with its own calibration and distance uncertainty.

The main sequence is a structural/evolutionary locus of core hydrogen-burning models, not an arbitrary constant-radius line. A luminosity–mass power law holds only over a restricted regime and composition. A rough lifetime scale is available fuel energy divided by luminosity, so a more massive luminous star may live less long despite containing more fuel. A point's location on an HR diagram alone can have degeneracies in mass, age, composition and binary contamination.

</LessonSection>
