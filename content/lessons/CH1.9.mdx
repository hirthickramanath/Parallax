<LessonSection id="story" title="Why the question matters" layer="story">

Electrode potentials depend on composition as well as chemical identity. Electrochemical potential combines material and electrical contributions to the reversible transfer of charged species.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="intuition" title="Build a mental model" layer="intuition">

A charged species responds to both its chemical environment and electric potential. Their contributions must be combined before judging equilibrium.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="concept" title="Definitions and hypotheses" layer="concept">

For molar charge number z, electrochemical potential is μ̃=μ+zFφ under the stated electrical-potential convention. Reversible cell emf is related to the reaction Gibbs energy.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="theory" title="Derivation, proof and limitations" layer="theory" fullOnly>

For a reaction transferring n moles of electrons per mole of reaction, $\Delta_rG=-nFE$ for reversible operation with a consistent reaction direction. Combining activities with ΔrG=ΔrG°+RT lnQ gives $E=E^\circ-(RT/nF)\ln Q$. Activities are dimensionless and n belongs to the balanced electron transfer, not an arbitrarily selected coefficient.

Multiplying a balanced reaction by two doubles ΔrG and n but leaves E unchanged. Reversing the reaction reverses E and ΔrG. Standard electrode potentials are intensive reference quantities, not extensive energies to add without charge accounting.

For a concentration cell with the same redox couple at different activities, the chemical-potential difference can produce emf even when the electrode materials match. Liquid junction potentials and nonideal solution activities can complicate a direct comparison with the simplest Nernst expression.

An individual electrode potential is measured relative to a reference; a complete cell difference is the directly accessible quantity. A loaded terminal voltage differs from reversible open-circuit emf through resistance, activation and concentration effects. Equilibrium theory does not set the delivered current without kinetic and circuit information.

The chemical potential of a single ion involves conventions and cannot always be isolated experimentally from its electrical environment in the same way as a neutral combination. Report reference electrode, reaction direction, activity model and temperature with an electrochemical potential or voltage.

</LessonSection>
