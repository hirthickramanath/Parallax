<LessonSection id="concept" title="Definitions and hypotheses" layer="concept">

Phonons are quantized normal-mode excitations of a lattice within a harmonic or controlled anharmonic description.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="theory" title="Derivation, proof and limitations" layer="theory" fullOnly>

For identical masses m joined by springs K in a monatomic chain of spacing a, $\omega^2=4K\sin^2(ka/2)/m$. At small k the branch is acoustic and approximately linear; the Brillouin-zone edge is not another copy of an unbounded continuum wavenumber.

Multiple atoms per cell introduce additional branches, including optical modes in appropriate cases. Polar materials couple optical displacement to electromagnetic response. Not every mode has the same group velocity or couples equally to a probe.

Quantization gives mode energies ℏω(n+1/2). Thermal occupation is bosonic when phonon number is not a conserved equilibrium constraint. A Debye low-temperature T³ heat capacity uses three-dimensional acoustic assumptions, not a universal law for all dimensions.

Anharmonicity allows interactions, thermal expansion and finite lifetimes. Pure harmonic periodic models do not alone supply every measured thermal conductivity; scattering, defects, boundaries and electronic contributions matter. A simulated dispersion requires force-constant and convergence validation.


### Derivation and transfer

A monatomic nearest-neighbor harmonic chain has ω(k)=2sqrt(K/m)|sin(ka/2)|. Near zero, the acoustic branch is linear, but the full lattice dispersion bends near the zone edge. Group and phase velocities therefore differ away from the long-wavelength limit.

The display fixes a=m=1. It is a dispersion relation rather than an integrated thermal lattice. Anharmonicity, scattering and multiple-atom optical branches require a richer model.

</LessonSection>
