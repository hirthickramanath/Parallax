<LessonSection id="story" title="Why the question matters" layer="story">

Exoplanet detection methods have geometry and selection biases; combining measurements can reduce but not erase inference degeneracies.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="intuition" title="Build a mental model" layer="intuition">

Start with the concrete worked example below. Track which quantities belong to the system, which are imposed by its surroundings, and which conclusions require a limiting approximation.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="concept" title="Definitions and hypotheses" layer="concept">

A transit measures a modelled loss of stellar flux, while radial velocities measure a line-of-sight stellar motion signal with instrument and stellar-activity contributions.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="theory" title="Derivation, proof and limitations" layer="theory" fullOnly>

For a small opaque planet on a non-grazing chord of a uniform-brightness star, depth is approximately $\delta=(R_p/R_*)^2$. Limb darkening, dilution, grazing geometry and stellar heterogeneity alter the relation. Detection does not directly supply an absolute radius without a stellar radius model.

For a nearly circular orbit, radial-velocity amplitude depends on M_p sin i relative to stellar mass and orbital period; eccentricity adds a factor and waveform changes. Without inclination a minimum-mass-like constraint is obtained in the small-companion approximation, not a unique planet mass.

Transit probability scales roughly with (R_*+R_p)/a for the simple circular isotropic-orientation case. Short-period large planets are easier to find in many transit surveys. Observation windows, noise and pipeline completeness must enter population inference; detected counts are not the underlying occurrence distribution.

Astrometry, microlensing, direct imaging and timing probe other parameter regimes with their own selection and host constraints. False positives, activity and blends require validation. Combining transit and velocity can constrain density, but correlated stellar/instrument uncertainties and orbital assumptions remain.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="worked" title="A worked argument" layer="worked" fullOnly>

For R_p/R_*=.1, the uniform-star approximate depth is .01. With R_*/a=.2 and negligible planet radius, the circular geometric probability is about .2. Neither number includes the survey’s detection completeness.

</LessonSection>
