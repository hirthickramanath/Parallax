Why does a first-order relativistic equation require matrix-valued coefficients? State a prediction and a reason before reading the argument.

<LessonSection id="story" title="Why the question matters" layer="story">

Dirac algebra linearizes relativistic dispersion and naturally includes spinor structure, while antiparticle interpretation belongs to field theory.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="intuition" title="Build a mental model" layer="intuition">

Start with the concrete worked example below. Track which quantities belong to the system, which are imposed by its surroundings, and which conclusions require a limiting approximation.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="concept" title="Definitions and hypotheses" layer="concept">

The free Dirac Hamiltonian is H=cα·p+βmc² with matrices obeying anticommutation relations that make H²=p²c²+m²c⁴.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="theory" title="Derivation, proof and limitations" layer="theory" fullOnly>

The requirements $\{\alpha_i,\alpha_j\}=2\delta_{ij}I$, $\{\alpha_i,\beta\}=0$ and β²=I can be written as a Clifford algebra. In covariant form, $[i\hbar c\gamma^\mu\partial_\mu-mc^2]\psi=0$ with $\{\gamma^\mu,\gamma^\nu\}=2\eta^{\mu\nu}I$. Matrix dimension and transformation rules are part of the spinor representation.

For a free momentum, energies have ±√(p²c²+m²c⁴) branches. The positive-density ψ†ψ and conserved current do not eliminate the need to interpret negative-energy/frequency sectors through a quantum field framework. A naive single-particle filled-sea story is not a full modern interacting theory.

A controlled low-velocity expansion with electromagnetic coupling produces Pauli spin coupling and leading relativistic corrections, with the tree-level electron magnetic factor g=2 in the ideal model. Radiative corrections and composite particles change magnetic moments. Minimal coupling conventions and charge signs matter.

Lorentz transformations act on spinor components differently from ordinary four-vectors. The equation is not a license to treat every classical spin vector as a spinor, and the nonrelativistic reduction requires separating large/small components within an admitted energy regime.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="interactive" title="Investigate the claim" layer="interactive">

Choose explicit gamma matrices and verify anticommutators numerically. Square the free Hamiltonian and check its dispersion. Keep matrix identities separate from a physical many-particle interpretation.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="worked" title="A worked argument" layer="worked" fullOnly>

For m=3,p=4,c=1, free energies are ±5. The ideal tree-level spin-half g factor is 2; that value is a model prediction before radiative/structure corrections, not an exact universal magnetic factor for every particle.

</LessonSection>
