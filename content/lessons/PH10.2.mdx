<LessonSection id="concept" title="Definitions and hypotheses" layer="concept">

The free Dirac Hamiltonian is H=cα·p+βmc² with matrices obeying anticommutation relations that make H²=p²c²+m²c⁴.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="theory" title="Derivation, proof and limitations" layer="theory" fullOnly>

The requirements $\{\alpha_i,\alpha_j\}=2\delta_{ij}I$, $\{\alpha_i,\beta\}=0$ and β²=I can be written as a Clifford algebra. In covariant form, $[i\hbar c\gamma^\mu\partial_\mu-mc^2]\psi=0$ with $\{\gamma^\mu,\gamma^\nu\}=2\eta^{\mu\nu}I$. Matrix dimension and transformation rules are part of the spinor representation.

For a free momentum, energies have ±√(p²c²+m²c⁴) branches. The positive-density ψ†ψ and conserved current do not eliminate the need to interpret negative-energy/frequency sectors through a quantum field framework. A naive single-particle filled-sea story is not a full modern interacting theory.

A controlled low-velocity expansion with electromagnetic coupling produces Pauli spin coupling and leading relativistic corrections, with the tree-level electron magnetic factor g=2 in the ideal model. Radiative corrections and composite particles change magnetic moments. Minimal coupling conventions and charge signs matter.

Lorentz transformations act on spinor components differently from ordinary four-vectors. The equation is not a license to treat every classical spin vector as a spinor, and the nonrelativistic reduction requires separating large/small components within an admitted energy regime.


### Derivation and transfer

The Dirac Hamiltonian linearizes relativistic momentum through matrices satisfying an anticommutation algebra. Its free energies are ±sqrt(p²+m²) in c=1 units. Spin and negative-energy structure require spinor solutions; the scalar energy curve alone does not encode all components.

The display compares kinetic energy above rest energy on the positive branch. At low momentum it approaches p²/(2m), while the exact relativistic expression remains the reference. No external-field spin dynamics is solved.

</LessonSection>
