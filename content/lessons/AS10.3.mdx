Bayesian inference combines a likelihood and prior, with posterior predictive validation needed before treating parameter estimates as scientifically adequate.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="concept" title="Definitions and hypotheses" layer="concept">

Bayes’ rule gives p(θ|d) proportional to p(d|θ)p(θ); every factor is tied to a data-generating/selection model and a parameter convention.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="theory" title="Derivation, proof and limitations" layer="theory" fullOnly>

For independent Gaussian residuals with known variances, log likelihood contains $-\tfrac12\sum_i[(d_i-m_i(\theta))^2/\sigma_i^2+\ln(2\pi\sigma_i^2)]$. If variances or correlations depend on parameters, the normalization cannot be discarded as a constant. A covariance matrix replaces independent terms when errors are correlated.

Priors encode a measure on parameters. A flat prior in θ is not flat in logθ, because transformation includes a Jacobian. State physical bounds and sensitivity rather than calling every convenient prior uninformative. Selection or truncation can require an explicit likelihood normalization over what could be observed.

Posterior samples need convergence/mixing and effective-sample-size diagnostics. A credible interval is a probability statement within the model/prior, not automatically a frequentist coverage guarantee. Degeneracy and multimodality should not be hidden behind one symmetric error bar.

Posterior predictive checks ask whether replicated data from the fitted model reproduce relevant observed features. Good chain convergence only validates exploration of the stated posterior; it does not prove the model explains reality. Record code, seeds, parameterization and data provenance, and distinguish synthetic inference from an actual source result.


### Derive, test and transfer

For two exhaustive hypotheses, Bayes' theorem in odds form is $O(H|D)=\mathrm{LR}\,O(H)$, with $O=p/(1-p)$. Converting back gives $p'=\mathrm{LR}p/[1-p+\mathrm{LR}p]$. A strong likelihood ratio can still yield a modest posterior for a sufficiently rare prior hypothesis.

The likelihood is a probability model for the data conditional on parameters or hypotheses, not the posterior probability of those hypotheses. Survey selection, nuisance parameters and correlations belong in the analysis model. A prior sensitivity check should show how conclusions move across defensible assumptions rather than silently picking the prior that produces a desired claim. The interactive odds calculation assumes its likelihood ratio is already valid; it does not estimate one from observations.

</LessonSection>
