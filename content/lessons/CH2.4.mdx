<LessonSection id="theory" title="Derivation, proof and limitations" layer="theory" fullOnly>

A rotor has $E_J=BJ(J+1)$ with energy B=ℏ²/(2I). If B is instead quoted as a wavenumber, factors hc relate it to energy. A reported cm⁻¹ value is not an angular frequency without conversion.

A harmonic vibrational angular frequency is √(k/μ), while rotational B scales as 1/μ for fixed geometry. Therefore isotopic scaling differs between the two. Anharmonicity, centrifugal distortion and vibration-rotation coupling produce deviations.

Electric-dipole rotational/vibrational selection rules depend on symmetry and dipole behaviour; Raman probes polarizability. Nuclear-spin statistics can alter line intensities and allowed rotational populations. Temperature and nonequilibrium excitation affect occupation, not only transition energy.

Assign spectra through multiple lines and uncertainty/response models, especially in blends. A fitted constant is conditional on the selected Hamiltonian expansion. A model line list is not a new laboratory spectrum or astronomical detection.


### Derive, test and transfer

A rigid linear rotor has EJ=BJ(J+1), so neighboring rotational spacings are 2B(J+1), not a constant energy gap. A harmonic vibration instead has constant adjacent spacing in its ideal approximation. Real rovibrational spectra combine both structures and can include centrifugal distortion and anharmonicity.

A transition needs the appropriate matrix element, not merely a pair of different energies. Electric-dipole infrared activity and Raman activity use different response operators. Isotopic substitution changes reduced mass and therefore characteristic rotational/vibrational scales, while preserving some electronic features approximately. The display isolates the integer rotational ladder and does not generate a full molecular spectrum.

</LessonSection>
