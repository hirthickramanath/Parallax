<LessonSection id="story" title="Why the question matters" layer="story">

Survey analysis requires quality cuts, completeness, provenance and measurement-model choices before any population conclusion.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="intuition" title="Build a mental model" layer="intuition">

Start with the concrete worked example below. Track which quantities belong to the system, which are imposed by its surroundings, and which conclusions require a limiting approximation.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="concept" title="Definitions and hypotheses" layer="concept">

A survey workflow transforms a versioned input catalogue or time series into a specified sample and statistic, retaining every selection and uncertainty assumption.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="theory" title="Derivation, proof and limitations" layer="theory" fullOnly>

Preserve source release, exact query, raw response/checksum, column units, masks and quality flags. A Gaia-like astrometric sample, TESS-like light curve and SDSS-like spectrum have different windows, calibration and selection. Do not label a synthetic exercise as one of these actual survey products.

An inverse-variance weighted mean is $\bar x=\sum x_i/\sigma_i^2\,/\sum1/\sigma_i^2$ for independent Gaussian measurements of one common quantity. Correlated systematics and intrinsic variability invalidate that simple uncertainty interpretation. Censoring and nondetections need explicit treatment rather than zero substitution.

Quality cuts can improve measurements while altering population completeness. A magnitude or signal-to-noise threshold can correlate with distance, colour or physical state. Save both the selection expression and exclusion counts, and test sensitivity to reasonable alternative cuts.

Validate a pipeline on small hand-checkable cases and independent synthetic injections, then propagate uncertainty to the scientific statistic. Reproducibility includes code/environment and fixed data, not an undocumented mutable web query. No real survey dataset is fetched or certified by this lesson.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="interactive" title="Investigate the claim" layer="interactive">

Build a synthetic masked table and a provenance manifest, perform stated quality cuts and compare exclusion counts. Test a weighted mean on the worked example before using a real release with documented calibration.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="worked" title="A worked argument" layer="worked" fullOnly>

For values 2 and 4 with uncertainties 1 and 2, weights are 1 and 1/4 and the common-mean estimate is (2+1)/1.25=2.4. This formula is not justified if the values represent an intrinsically changing source without that model.

</LessonSection>
