<LessonSection id="story" title="Why the question matters" layer="story">

Markov chain Monte Carlo constructs a correlated sequence targeting a distribution. A stationary target and a large file of samples are not proof that the simulation explored the distribution adequately.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="intuition" title="Build a mental model" layer="intuition">

A chain can move slowly or get trapped in one region. Adjacent draws then repeat much of the same information, so effective sample size is smaller than the recorded length.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="concept" title="Definitions and hypotheses" layer="concept">

MCMC uses a Markov transition kernel with a target invariant distribution. Convergence, exploration and Monte Carlo uncertainty require conditions and diagnostics beyond invariance alone.

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection id="theory" title="Derivation, proof and limitations" layer="theory" fullOnly>

Metropolis–Hastings proposes y from q(y|x) and accepts with $\alpha(x,y)=\min[1,\pi(y)q(x|y)/(\pi(x)q(y|x))]$ on the admitted support, with suitable treatment of zero densities. Otherwise it stays at x. The construction satisfies detailed balance for the target under its assumptions; an unknown common normalizing constant in π cancels.

A symmetric proposal simplifies the ratio to π(y)/π(x). Proposal asymmetry must not be ignored. Invariance alone does not establish ergodicity: the kernel must be able to reach the relevant support and avoid other convergence obstructions. Rejecting nearly every proposal can freeze a chain, while accepting nearly every tiny step can still explore very slowly.

For a stationary scalar estimand with summable autocorrelations ρk, a rough effective sample size is $N_{\rm eff}\approx N/(1+2\sum_{k\ge1}\rho_k)$. It depends on the particular estimated function, not only the parameter count. Estimation of that sum is itself uncertain. Thinning generally discards information and is not a universal cure for poor mixing.

Run multiple dispersed chains and inspect trace plots, rank-based convergence diagnostics, effective sample sizes and Monte Carlo standard errors for the quantities that matter. No finite diagnostic proves all modes were found. A chain trapped in the same missed mode as its companions can look deceptively consistent.

Warm-up adaptation can tune proposal or Hamiltonian parameters, but naively changing the transition rule indefinitely can invalidate the simple stationary-chain argument. Separate adaptation from retained sampling or use a justified adaptive method. Validate on a known distribution before applying an implementation to an expensive scientific posterior, and keep computational Monte Carlo error distinct from uncertainty in the underlying physical parameters.


### Derivation and transfer

For a symmetric Metropolis proposal, the acceptance probability is min(1,π(y)/π(x)). This ratio cancels an unknown normalizing constant, but stationarity alone does not guarantee fast mixing. Autocorrelation and separated modes can leave apparently long chains uninformative.

The display uses a dimensionless negative log-target difference ΔU so acceptance is min(1,exp(−ΔU)). It does not execute a chain. Asymmetric proposals require the Hastings proposal-density ratio. Effective sample size and multiple-chain diagnostics remain necessary for an actual sampling project.

</LessonSection>
